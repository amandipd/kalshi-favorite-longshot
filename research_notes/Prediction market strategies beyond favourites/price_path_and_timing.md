# Price-path and timing patterns in prediction markets

Scope: how prices move over a contract's life. That means momentum (prices keep going the way they moved), mean reversion (prices snap back), overreaction and underreaction, time decay on "will X happen by date" markets, calendar and time-of-day effects, and the last minutes before close. The static favourite-longshot bias (cheap unlikely bets are overpriced, heavy favourites underpriced) is out of scope, and so is everything already covered in earlier notes.

Evidence labels used below:
- **[STRONG]**: peer-reviewed, or a large real-trade study with clear methods.
- **[MEDIUM]**: a working paper or preprint built on real trade data, not yet peer-reviewed.
- **[WEAK]**: a blog, a vendor, a hobby backtest, a simulation, or a search summary that could not be checked against the full text.

Fee yardstick for every number below. The Kalshi taker fee is 7% × price × (1 − price) per contract. That is about 1.75c at a 50c price, 0.63c at 90c and 0.33c at 95c. Any edge under about 1-2c at mid prices is gone after fee plus spread (the gap between the buy and sell price, the cost of getting in).

Tick note for older TradeSports papers: a TradeSports contract paid $10 and was split into 100 ticks of $0.10. So 1 tick = 1% of the payout. That is the same as 1c on a $1 Kalshi contract.

## Momentum vs mean reversion: do prices drift after moves, or snap back?

### Takeaway
Across old and new markets, prices are close to a random walk (past moves do not predict future moves). Where a pattern shows up, it is small, it is mostly in sports, and it is eaten by spread and fees. No study found a momentum or mean-reversion rule that makes money after costs on a large, out-of-sample (tested on data the rule never saw while being built) sample.

### Cited findings
- **[STRONG, 2004 paper, data Mar 18 to Oct 4 2003] Tetlock, TradeSports.** 51,650 pairs of past and future 30-minute returns. In non-sports contracts (financial and other), a past move predicts nothing: coefficient 0.001, t = 0.03 (t-stat: how far from luck; about 2 is the usual bar). Prices there are "statistically indistinguishable from a random walk." Source: [Tetlock 2004, "How Efficient Are Information Markets?"](http://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock-Efficient_Info_Markets-01_02.pdf)
- **[STRONG, 2004]** Same paper, sports-game contracts: a price move is followed by a reversal (a snap back) of 13.8% of the move. That is only just past the luck bar (t = −2.04). Big "featured" games showed no extra reversal (t = 0.55). The bid-ask spread and volume did not change the size of the reversal. Source: [Tetlock 2004](http://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock-Efficient_Info_Markets-01_02.pdf)
- **[STRONG, 2004] Tetlock's fade rule, with hindsight.** The rule bought after a 30-minute fall of more than 5 ticks and sold after a rise of more than 5 ticks. It used sports contracts within 4 hours of expiry with a spread of 3 ticks or less, held to settlement. It traded 752 times (340 buys, 412 sells). Gross: +1.29 ticks a trade, about 1.29c on a $1 contract. After the 0.8-tick round-trip commission: +0.49 ticks a trade. The 95% CI (the range the true number is probably in) for the gross figure was −1.69 to +4.27 ticks. Tetlock says the result "could be ascribed to chance." The 2003 commission was much lower than today's Kalshi fee. Source: [Tetlock 2004](http://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock-Efficient_Info_Markets-01_02.pdf)
- **[STRONG, 2004] "New underdog" overreaction.** These are sports contracts that had just fallen below 50 ticks. They returned 5.36 against 0.45 for new favourites. The gap of −4.91 is far past the luck bar (t = −3.71). New underdogs in financial contracts showed no such effect (t = 0.37). A rule bought the 731 new sports underdogs whose spread was 5 ticks or less. It made +3.79 ticks on 37.13 ticks invested **after spread and commission**. That is 10.2% over about one day per game. Tetlock also notes the favourite-longshot bias was strongest when contracts were first listed. That matches this project's own finding that favourites are overpriced at a market's first trade. Source: [Tetlock 2004](http://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock-Efficient_Info_Markets-01_02.pdf)
- **[STRONG, survey, 2012]** An NBER survey says there is no evidence that trading on past prices makes a profit in prediction markets. It cites Tetlock (2004). It also cites Berg, Forrest and Rietz, who found Iowa Electronic Markets prices follow a random walk. (Search summary; the full survey was not read.) Source: [NBER w18222](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18222/w18222.pdf)
- **[WEAK but careful, hobbyist, data 2023 to Oct 2026] polylab, Polymarket.** 112,636 resolved two-outcome markets, with a train/test split (build the rule on older data, score it once on newer data). It ran 66 pre-registered rules with a 1c cost per trade. Result: "7-day momentum and reversal: flat to negative." Exact figures per rule are not on the page. The author sells nothing and placed no trades. Source: [GitHub benaticak-alt/polylab](https://github.com/benaticak-alt/polylab)
- **[WEAK, vendor-ish, Apr 2026] Quantpedia, Polymarket mean reversion.** The rule buys "No" when it hits its 5-, 10- or 20-day low, then holds 1-5 days. It used 10-minute prices on three long-shot markets ("Jesus returns in 2025", "China invades Taiwan in 2025", "US confirms aliens in 2025") over about a year. At zero cost the best Sharpe ratios (return per unit of risk) were 2.97, 1.58 and 1.72. With a 0.1% cost per trade, the fastest version on the Jesus market fell from Sharpe +2.97 to −2.60. Only the slowest version (20-day low, 5-day hold) stayed positive. There was no out-of-sample test and only 3 hand-picked markets. Quantpedia sells research subscriptions. Source: [Quantpedia, 17 Apr 2026](https://quantpedia.com/exploiting-mean-reversion-in-decentralized-prediction-markets-evidence-from-polymarket-binary-contracts/)
- **[WEAK, vendor, Apr 2026, Kalshi BTC 15-minute]** Of 4,904 strategies, 0 of 432 mean-reversion versions made money after fees and depth. "Panic-fade" made money in 93 of 96 versions, as reported. This was already in earlier notes. Turbine sells trading tools. Source: [TurbineFi](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/do-automated-trading-bots-make-money-prediction-markets-2026)
- **[MEDIUM, data Nov 2022 to Apr 2026] Polymarket-v1 database.** 1.2 billion trades. Trade *direction* comes in runs: a buy is usually followed by another buy. This is "strongly positive at mid-market prices (0.2-0.8)." That is order flow, not price returns. The paper does not report whether price returns are autocorrelated (follow on from each other). Source: [arXiv 2606.04217](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04217v1)
- **[WEAK, case studies, 2024 events] Polymarket Trump contract.** At the June 2024 debate, the price jumped +11.1c, then stood at only +2.0c four hours later (mostly snapped back). After the July 2024 assassination attempt, it jumped +10.9c and stayed +10.9c at four hours (lasting repricing). The Biden dropout moved it −3.9c, then −2.0c at four hours. That is three events, quoted from Tsang and Yang (2026a). No trading test. Source: [Nechepurenko, arXiv 2604.24147](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24147v1)
- **[WEAK, Sep 2026] Crowd comments and reversals.** In 79 non-political Polymarket markets, comment stance turned against the leading outcome before a reversal in 23 of 27 markets that could be scored. It gave "no clear improvement" in forecasts of individual blocks of trading. No returns are tested. Source: [Sultan & Morstatter, arXiv 2609.28965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28965)
- **[WEAK, search summary only]** Polymarket Bitcoin threshold markets: Yes prices ran about 5.6 points above prices implied by Binance options. The gap shrank back toward fair with about a 4-hour half-life (the time for half the gap to close). This is a fair-value gap, close to the cross-venue topic, and not price momentum. Source: [arXiv 2606.19517](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19517v1)

### Inferences
- Sports contracts are the one place where old data show a reversal after moves. Tetlock's sports reversal was only 13.8% of the move. His fade rule netted 0.49c per contract with commission at about 0.8c round trip. Kalshi's fee alone is 1.75c at 50c. The same rule would almost surely lose on Kalshi today.
- Tetlock's "new underdog" result (+10.2% a game after costs, 2003) is the strongest older evidence of overreaction. But it clashes with this project's own test, where buying the underdog of a weak price band lost. It also clashes with the newer Kalshi NBA work below, which finds *underreaction*. It is a 2003 result on a small exchange. It is best treated as a lead to re-test on Kalshi sports, not as an edge.
- Momentum in trade direction (order runs) does not mean momentum in price. It most likely reflects one trader splitting a big order, or market makers working inventory.

### Gaps
- No large study of price-return autocorrelation (do returns follow on from each other) was found on Kalshi itself, at any horizon.
- The NBER survey and the Berg/Forrest/Rietz IEM random-walk result were seen only through a search summary.
- polylab does not publish per-rule numbers for momentum and reversal, only "flat to negative."

## Price impact reversal: after a big trade moves a thin market, does the price come back?

### Takeaway
Yes, partly. About half of a single trade's price move comes back on Polymarket. Near-settlement pushes in 5-minute crypto markets also partly reverse within seconds. But the reversing part is under a cent per trade in normal markets. No study shows a resting-order or fade strategy that captures it after fees. In single-name Kalshi markets, big one-sided flow tends to be informed, so fading it means getting picked off (adverse selection: getting picked off by someone who knows more than you).

### Cited findings
- **[MEDIUM, 6,223 Polymarket markets, 2022-2026] Permanent vs temporary impact.** The paper used a VAR (a model of how trades and prices feed each other over time). The median permanent part of a trade's price impact was 0.00152 ($1 price units, about 0.15c). The median temporary part was 0.00177 (about 0.18c). Median permanent share: 45.5%. So about 55% of a typical trade's move comes back. Thin markets gave unstable fits: the mean permanent impact was about 45 times the median. Source: [Polymarket-v1, arXiv 2606.04217](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04217v1)
- **[STRONG, 2004]** On TradeSports, the share of net bids in the order book predicted the next 30-minute return: coefficient 0.419, past the luck bar (t = 2.29). Tetlock reads this as "temporary price pressure." His model predicts "slight return reversals within the arbitrage bounds," never big enough to profit from. Source: [Tetlock 2004](http://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock-Efficient_Info_Markets-01_02.pdf)
- **[MEDIUM, Apr 2026, 41.6M Kalshi trades] Bartlett & O'Hara.** Single-name markets (one person, company or team) show more *informed* price impact than broad markets. Effective spreads there are "only modestly wider." Market makers earn about twice as much per contract there. One-sided order flow predicts maker losses in single-name markets but not in broad ones. Only the summary page was readable, not the permanent/temporary split. Sources: [Stanford Law summary](https://law.stanford.edu/press/adverse-selection-in-prediction-markets-evidence-from-kalshi/); [SSRN 6615739](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6615739)
- **[MEDIUM, Polymarket BTC 5-minute, about 2 months in 2026] Settlement pushes partly reverse.** In near-even cycles, a final-seconds push on the reference spot price had reverted by about a quarter within ten seconds. In other cycles it reverted by about a tenth. The authors read this as a temporary push, not news. Source: [Dai, Jia & Yu, arXiv 2606.31675](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31675v1)
- **[WEAK, search summary] Iowa Electronic Markets.** Rietz (2006) is quoted as saying prices "recover quickly" after large trades that carry no news. Rhode & Strumpf placed deliberate price-moving trades on TradeSports in 2004 and on the IEM. Their recovery times could not be found. Sources: [Rhode & Strumpf 2006 NBER draft](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2006/daes06/rhode.pdf); [arXiv 2503.03312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03312)
- **[WEAK, vendor] Kalshi BTC 15-minute "panic-fade"** was profitable in 93 of 96 versions in a 30-day vendor backtest after fees and depth limits. The rules and sample size per version are not public. Turbine sells tools. Source: [TurbineFi](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/do-automated-trading-bots-make-money-prediction-markets-2026)
- **[WEAK, search summary] Depth example.** A 50,000-contract order on Polymarket US filled about 3.6c above the displayed ask. This shows how far a big order moves a price. It says nothing about whether the price comes back. Source: [River Markets](https://www.rivermarkets.com/insights/prediction-market-execution-economics.html)

### Inferences
- The temporary part of a normal trade's impact (median about 0.18c) is far smaller than the Kalshi fee (0.33c at 95c, 1.75c at 50c). A taker fade cannot pay for itself. A maker (resting) order pays no fee on many Kalshi series, so it is the only plausible way to collect it. But that is market making, which is out of scope here.
- Bartlett & O'Hara's split matters for any fade idea. In broad, rule-based markets (crypto, index, economic data), one-sided flow did not predict maker losses. Fading big moves there is less dangerous than in single-name markets. That is a direction to test, not a result.
- The 5-minute settlement-push reversal is real but lasts about ten seconds. It mostly hits whoever holds the losing side at settlement. It is a risk to the bot more than a trade for it.

### Gaps
- No study measures, on Kalshi, what happens to the price 1, 5 or 30 minutes after a large taker trade. Nor does any test a resting order placed behind such a move.
- The Bartlett & O'Hara permanent/temporary numbers were not reachable (summary page only).

## In-play / live overreaction: betting exchanges, and whether it carries over to Kalshi and Polymarket

### Takeaway
The newest and largest Kalshi live-sports studies find **underreaction**, not overreaction. Prices move only about 64c for every $1 of true change in win chance, then catch up over the next 5-15 minutes. The drift is real at the midpoint, but **a strategy that crosses the spread loses money**, even before Kalshi's fee. The older Betfair "overreaction to surprises" story does not carry over cleanly to Kalshi.

### Cited findings
- **[MEDIUM, Jun 2026; Kalshi NBA, 15 Apr 2025 to 25 May 2026] Angelini & De Angelis.** 1,438 games, 2,876 team contracts and 409,512 contract-minutes, matched to play-by-play data. When a model's win probability moves, the Kalshi price moves "only about a 0.64-for-one" in the same minute (0.638 with controls; clearly below 1). That is underreaction. Source: [arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07811)
- Same paper, catch-up: for every 10 points of initial gap, the price moves about 2.0 points more in the next 5 minutes (4.6 net of model changes). At 15 minutes, the figures are 2.4 and 4.8 points. Source: [arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07811)
- Same paper, by game phase: the same-minute response was 0.641 in Q1, 0.663 in Q2, 0.673 in Q3 and 0.620 in Q4. In clutch time (last 5 minutes of Q4, margin of 5 or less) it was only 0.509. Underreaction is larger in thin markets. Salient (eye-catching) events are absorbed faster in liquid markets. Source: [arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07811)
- Same paper, **trading test**: buy Yes after a positive gap, sell after a negative gap, and hold 5 minutes. At the midpoint it earned +0.39c per contract (all gaps) and +0.87c (gaps of 2 points or more). **Crossing the spread** it earned −1.20c (all gaps) and −0.36c (high-quality quotes, gaps of 2 points or more). Fees were not modelled. The authors say the predictability is "largely absorbed by the bid-ask spread." Source: [arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07811)
- **[STRONG/MEDIUM, SSRN Jun 2026; journal version JBEF Sep 2026] Lee, Lee & Lee.** 41.8 million Kalshi transactions across 1,496 NBA and NFL games. Prices adjust to scoring news "slowly and incompletely." NBA prices drift steadily toward fair value. NFL prices settle flatter and less completely after rarer, bigger shocks. Highlight-style plays get a weaker short-run response. Prices do **not** chase scoring runs (no hot-hand pricing). Exact numbers were not reachable (SSRN blocked; summary via search). Sources: [SSRN 6964226](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6964226); [ResearchGate (JBEF version)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/414405841_Underreaction_salience_and_hot-hand_beliefs_in_sports_prediction_markets_Evidence_from_Kalshi_NBA_and_NFL_event_contracts)
- **[MEDIUM, Jul 2026; 23M Kalshi moneyline trades] Sports calibration** (whether a 70c price really wins 70% of the time) over the game. It is near perfect mid-contract and departs sharply near expiry. In the final ten minutes the curve becomes "step-like." The author reads this as "insurance demand" by traders holding losing positions. Cross-game parlays are overpriced against the product of their legs, more so with more legs. No profit test. Source: [arXiv 2607.14430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14430)
- **[MEDIUM, Oct 2026 revision; Kalshi hourly Aug 2021 to Apr 2026] Sports prices move in bursts.** A single-game contract makes 54% of its total price movement in its three largest hourly moves (31% for other sports contracts). Only 4.1% of sports hours have a 5c+ surprise move, but those hours carry 47.8% of sports volume. Source: [Xi, Moallemi, Pai & Wang, arXiv 2607.08199](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08199)
- **[STRONG, 2004] TradeSports sports games:** a 13.8% reversal after moves, and a "new underdog" overreaction worth +10.2% a game after costs in 2003 data (details in the first section). Source: [Tetlock 2004](http://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock-Efficient_Info_Markets-01_02.pdf)
- **[STRONG, older Betfair work, already in earlier notes]** Choi & Hui (2014): in-play soccer underreacts to expected events and overreacts to very surprising ones. Croxson & Reade (2014): prices adjust fast and fully to goals near half-time. Gauriot & Page: underreaction to big late shocks. Not re-researched. Source: [Choi & Hui](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256013000_The_Role_of_Surprise_Understanding_Overreaction_and_Underreaction_to_Unanticipated_Events_using_In-Play_Soccer_Betting_Market)
- **[WEAK, search summary, 2026] Betfair in-play football model.** It bet against in-play 1X2 odds (home win, draw, away win) with 2% commission. It returned +4.5% ROI with Kelly sizing (the bet size that grows money fastest without risking ruin) but −3.4% with flat stakes. So the result depends on sizing. Source: [arXiv 2605.16066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16066)
- **[WEAK, practitioner blogs]** Betfair tennis "fade the break" guides claim the price overshoots after a service break. They give no tested results. One guide warns that fills arrive 5-10 ticks worse than the price seen. Sources: [Traderline](https://traderline.com/education/betfair-tennis-trading-strategy); [Caan Berry](https://caanberry.com/tennis-trading-strategies/) (Caan Berry sells trading courses.)
- **[WEAK, search summary]** Kalshi in-game depth (how much money you can trade before moving the price) falls about 76% versus pre-game, despite more trades. Source: [Blockworks](https://app.blockworks.com/report/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)

### Inferences
- On Kalshi live sports, the sign is underreaction, so the trade is "follow the model," not "fade the move." But the best version earned +0.87c at the midpoint before fees. The Kalshi fee at mid prices is about 1.75c, so it fails once fee and spread are added. This would need a maker (resting) order, or a much better fair-value model, to work. The second is a forecasting model, which is out of scope.
- Underreaction is biggest in clutch time and thin markets. The spread is also widest there. The pattern and its cost sit in the same place.
- Betfair findings carry over poorly. Betfair's commission is on net winnings, not per contract. Kalshi's per-contract fee is largest near 50c, which is where live sports contracts spend most of their time.

### Gaps
- No study tests the Kalshi live-sports drift with a resting (maker) order and real queue position.
- No Polymarket live-sports study of over- or underreaction was found.
- Lee, Lee & Lee numbers (size of the drift and time to adjust) were not reachable.

## Time decay in "will X happen by date" markets: do Yes prices decay too slowly or too fast? Does selling Yes pay in any category?

### Takeaway
The best older evidence is Page & Clemen (Intrade). Long-dated prices are pulled toward the middle: cheap Yes too dear, likely Yes too cheap, by roughly 5-11 points beyond 100 days. Near expiry the pull is gone. The excess return vanishes once you charge a 15-25% yearly cost of capital. Kalshi and Polymarket now pay interest on positions, which removes much of that reason. No source tests "buy No on deadline markets" by category, net of fees, out of sample.

### Cited findings
- **[STRONG, Economic Journal 2013; Intrade] Page & Clemen.** 1,787 markets and 512,612 trades. Prices are "reasonably well calibrated when time to expiration is relatively short." They are "significantly biased for events farther in the future," in the favourite-longshot direction. The bias was stronger for prices seen more than 100 days before expiry. A longer time to expiry made the calibration curve bend more (coefficient −0.001 per day, significant). Political markets were the most biased. Source: [Page & Clemen 2013 (PDF)](https://people.duke.edu/~clemen/bio/Published%20Papers/45.PredictionMarkets-Page&Clemen-EJ-2013.pdf)
- Same paper, **trading test** on the second half of the data: "buy over 0.50, sell under 0.50" averaged 9.55%. A fitted-curve rule averaged 5.25%. Then they priced in the cost of tying up money. A 10% yearly discount rate cut the fitted rule to 2.47%. A 15% rate wiped it out, and a 25% rate wiped out the simple rule. These returns are before Intrade fees and spread. Source: [Page & Clemen 2013](https://people.duke.edu/~clemen/bio/Published%20Papers/45.PredictionMarkets-Page&Clemen-EJ-2013.pdf)
- **[WEAK, simulation, Feb 2026] Maresca.** It cites Page & Clemen's bias as 4.7-10.9 points for markets more than 100 days out. In an LLM-trader simulation, a 2-year horizon added only 0.72 points of bias. Paying interest on positions removed about 83% of that and raised participation from 17% to 62% of wealth. At the time of writing, Kalshi paid 3.25% and Polymarket 4% on positions. Kalshi started paying interest in October 2024. Source: [arXiv 2602.21091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21091)
- **[MEDIUM, May 2026; Polymarket Oct 2020 to Apr 2026] Deadline decay is partly mechanical.** On a "by date" market, the fair Yes price should fall as the window shrinks, even with no news. The paper builds a hazard-decay baseline (the expected fall from the shrinking window) to separate that from real information. A constant-hazard curve (straight exponential decay) was rejected for a Weibull curve (decay that speeds up or slows down over time) on pooled post-2024 markets. The authors say this reflects a mix of categories, not one shape within each. Only 88 of 12,708 markets could be scored. No returns. Source: [arXiv 2605.00459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00459)
- **[WEAK, journalism, 2026] "Nothing Ever Happens" (buy No on every non-sports market).** Polymarket says 73.3% of its resolved markets end No. Decrypt's quick look at about 2,300 closed positions found 73.5% No for markets open 90-180 days, against 52% No for markets open under a week. The bot's live P&L was not shared. A No *base rate* is not a return: the price paid matters. Sources: [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/364381/polymarket-bot-bets-no-has-a-point); [GitHub sterlingcrispin/nothing-ever-happens](https://github.com/sterlingcrispin/nothing-ever-happens)
- **[WEAK]** A Hacker News commenter's "about 100% APR" backtest of the same idea was "cheating" by using known resolution times. Source: [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47753472)
- **[WEAK but careful, data 2023 to Oct 2026] polylab** tested a "buy NO" rule among its 66 rules but does not publish its result. Its headline underdog edge (t = 8.4 on held-out data) came from the sample filter. It kept only markets that ended with $25k+ volume, and upsets attract late volume. On the $5k-$25k markets the filter dropped, the underdog won 20.9% against 23.2% implied, an ROI of −14%. The lesson for any deadline study: pick markets only on facts known at entry. Source: [GitHub polylab](https://github.com/benaticak-alt/polylab)
- **[WEAK, Apr 2026, hand-picked] Buy-and-hold No on three 2025 long-shot deadline markets** (Jesus returns, China invades Taiwan, aliens confirmed) earned +5.98%, +12.69% and +8.63% a year before costs. All three resolved No, and they were chosen with hindsight. Source: [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/exploiting-mean-reversion-in-decentralized-prediction-markets-evidence-from-polymarket-binary-contracts/)
- **[MEDIUM, Sep 2026; 588M Polymarket trades] Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe.** Longshot returns were negative under every closing-date cut they tried. They do not split out deadline markets or Yes vs No. Returns exclude fees. Source: [arXiv 2609.12878](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1)
- **[WEAK, search summary] Reichenbach & Walther (SSRN, 124M Polymarket trades).** No general longshot bias. Price errors cluster early in a contract's life and near resolution. Source: [SSRN 5910522](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5910522)
- **[WEAK, lab preprint] Brier loss** (a forecast-error score; lower is better) fell about 60% from 30 days to 12 hours before close, across 1,818 Polymarket markets. Source: [Minami Lab](https://minami-lab-hp.pages.dev/en/research/polymarket-uncertainty)

### Inferences
- The Page & Clemen pattern predicts that Yes on long-dated unlikely events is too expensive. So buying No far from the deadline should earn a premium. But most of that premium looked like payment for tying up money. Kalshi now pays about 3-4% a year on positions, so the premium on Kalshi should be smaller than in 2003-2010 Intrade data.
- For a small bot, a yearly 5-10% edge on capital locked up for months is a poor use of money next to a 15-minute strategy that turns capital over many times a day.
- "Too slow vs too fast" decay has no clean answer. The only deadline-specific paper shows the decay shape varies by category. It does not test whether prices decay slower than the true hazard (the chance per day that the event happens).

### Gaps
- No source tests, on Kalshi, buying No on "by date" markets by category, net of fees and spread, out of sample.
- No source compares the actual fall in Yes prices with a fair hazard curve and turns the difference into a return.
- polylab's "buy NO" and per-horizon results are not published.

## Calendar and time-of-day effects: weekend, overnight, holiday, market-open and pre-data-release patterns

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly old or indirect. Betfair and horse-racing studies find weekend prices are noisier because more casual bettors trade then. Manipulation in Polymarket 5-minute crypto clusters in overnight and weekend hours, when books are thin. On Kalshi economic-data markets, no sign of a pre-release pricing error was found. No published study measures hour-of-day or day-of-week mispricing on Kalshi.

### Cited findings
- **[MEDIUM, 2013 working paper, Betfair] Flepp, Nüesch & Franck.** They used tick-size changes as a natural experiment in liquidity. More liquidity *reduced* price efficiency for weekend matches but not weekday ones. Their explanation: more uninformed "noise" bettors on weekends. (Abstract-level; numbers not seen.) Sources: [NEP listing](https://nep.repec.org/nep-mst/2014-01-24); [UZH](https://www.merlin.uzh.ch/publication/show/8965)
- **[MEDIUM, 2012, UK horse racing]** Weekend prices are a weaker guide to race results than weekday prices, which the authors put down to more noise traders. A Kelly strategy on high-probability runners was reported to earn positive returns. (Abstract-level; size and costs not seen.) Sources: [Southampton eprint 339957](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/339957); [EFMA 2007 draft](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0263.pdf)
- **[STRONG, 2008] Tetlock, "Liquidity and Prediction Market Efficiency."** More liquidity does not reduce, and sometimes increases, gaps between prices and outcomes on TradeSports. The proposed reason: naive limit-order traders are picked off by better-informed ones. Source: [Tetlock 2008 (SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=929916)
- **[MEDIUM, 2026, Polymarket BTC 5-minute] Manipulation clusters in thin hours.** About 1,600 cycles in the top tenth of final-ten-second order flow were flagged as pushed. 56% were overnight, against 40% of normal cycles. 44% were on weekends, against 27%. Source: [Dai, Jia & Yu, arXiv 2606.31675](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31675v1)
- **[MEDIUM, Fed staff working paper, Feb 2026] Kalshi macro markets.** On release day, Kalshi's headline CPI mean was off by about 7 basis points (0.07 percentage points), against 8 for the Bloomberg consensus. Error falls steadily as the release nears. The Kalshi median and mode had a perfect record on fed funds the day before each FOMC meeting. A forecast-shape test, run both 28 days before and on release day, looked "close to uniform." So there is no clear sign of a built-in pre-release bias. The far-out strikes "often suffer from low trading volume," which "can lead to stale prices." Spread midpoints were unreliable there because tail spreads are large. Source: [Diercks, Katz & Wright, FEDS 2026-010](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026010pap.pdf)
- **[MEDIUM] Kalshi economics contracts move smoothly** toward their scheduled release. Their share of large-move hours by volume (3.0%) is below their share of hours (4.9%). The paper gives no time-of-day or day-of-week results. Source: [arXiv 2607.08199](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08199)
- **[WEAK, vendor, one day: 21 Aug 2026]** Kalshi BTC 15-minute median spread was 0.82c, against 1.02c on Polymarket. Kalshi had about 12 times more size at the best price. No hour-by-hour breakdown. CryptoStruct sells tick data. Source: [CryptoStruct](https://cryptostruct.com/guides/kalshi-vs-polymarket-data)
- **[WEAK, search summary]** Neither Kalshi nor Polymarket publishes historical depth or spread. Independent time-of-day studies need self-recorded order books. Source: [Kalshi vs Polymarket comparison](https://www.assymetrix.com/blog/polymarket-vs-kalshi)

### Inferences
- The common thread is that thin hours bring more noise and more manipulation. For a favourite-buying bot, that cuts both ways. Mispricing may be larger, but late flips are also more common in thin hours. This project already found its own timing rule (skipping the 9:30 ET window held up out of sample). That in-house data is likely the best evidence available for Kalshi 15-minute markets.
- Kalshi data markets (CPI, jobs, fed funds) look well priced on release day. Nothing found suggests a repeatable pre-release pricing error to trade. The stale far-out strikes are a liquidity issue, not an edge.

### Gaps
- No study of hour-of-day, day-of-week or holiday effects on Kalshi pricing errors or spreads.
- No study of the US stock-market open (9:30 ET) or close on Kalshi crypto or index contracts, apart from this project's own backtest.
- The Betfair and horse-racing weekend studies were seen at abstract level only, so their sizes and costs are unknown.

## Price behaviour in the final minutes and hours before close, other than favourite buying

### Takeaway
Volatility (how much the price swings) jumps in the last hours. It follows a simple rule: it grows as time runs out and peaks near 50c. The last two hours carry about a quarter of all Kalshi volume. In 5-minute crypto markets, final-seconds pushes on the settlement price flip outcomes. Even 90-100c favourites lost about a third of the time in pushed cycles, against 1% otherwise. The signature is much weaker at 15 minutes. Live-sports underreaction is largest in clutch time. No study of "pinning" (prices sticking to a level before close) in prediction markets was found.

### Cited findings
- **[MEDIUM, Oct 2026 revision; Kalshi hourly, Aug 2021 to Apr 2026; 41,348 contracts, 6.75M contract-hours] Xi, Moallemi, Pai & Wang.** Volatility "rises sharply in the final hours before resolution." Time to resolution has the biggest single effect, a 22.6× gap between its highest and lowest bins. Volatility peaks near 50c and falls toward the edges (3.5× across price bins). It follows the shape p × (1 − p) ÷ time left. Source: [arXiv 2607.08199](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08199)
- Same paper: the final two hours carry "about a quarter of all volume." There, time-to-deadline alone forecasts volatility best. Adding order-flow information makes forecasts worse. Further out, order flow does most of the work. The paper does not report a reversal statistic or test any strategy. Source: [arXiv 2607.08199](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08199)
- **[MEDIUM, 2026; Polymarket BTC 5-minute, plus 15-minute and 4-hour for comparison] Settlement manipulation.** Spot order flow in the final ten seconds rose about 50% after the 5-minute contract launched. It jumped 3.9× more in near-even cycles (about 6% of cycles). In near-even cycles, a push against the favoured side flipped the winner 65% of the time, against 41% normally. Where one side was priced 90-100%, pushed cycles reversed 34% of the time, against 1% without a push. 821 wallets took $8.2 million. The signature was "significantly weaker" at 15 minutes. Source: [arXiv 2606.31675](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31675v1)
- **[WEAK, affiliate-linked, already in earlier notes] Kalshi BTC 15-minute, 2,400 windows, 4 Sep to 23 Oct 2026.** Windows finishing $50+ from the start price flipped in the last two minutes 13 of 1,318 times (about 1%). Windows finishing within $50 flipped 230 of 1,082 times (about 21%). Source: [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/are-kalshi-15-minute-bitcoin-markets-rigged)
- **[WEAK, vendor, one day, 97 windows per venue] Endgame path, BTC 15-minute.** The leading side averaged about 58c with 14 minutes left and about 89c with 2 minutes left. It crossed 80c about 5 minutes out and 90c with about 90 seconds left, and was about 98c in the final minute. Kalshi and Polymarket stayed within about 1c of each other at every minute. Source: [CryptoStruct](https://cryptostruct.com/guides/kalshi-vs-polymarket-data)
- **[MEDIUM, Jul 2026; Kalshi sports]** Calibration is near perfect mid-game and "departs sharply" near expiry. It is "step-like" in the last ten minutes. Source: [arXiv 2607.14430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14430)
- **[MEDIUM, Kalshi NBA]** Same-minute price response to news fell to 0.509 in clutch time, against 0.62-0.67 in normal quarters. So prices lag most at the very end. Source: [arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07811)
- **[STRONG, 2004]** Tetlock aimed his fade rule at sports contracts within 4 hours of expiry, "when overreaction is strongest." It still netted only 0.49 ticks a trade (details above). Source: [Tetlock 2004](http://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock-Efficient_Info_Markets-01_02.pdf)
- **[STRONG, 2013]** Page & Clemen found Intrade prices well calibrated near expiry. The bias was in long-dated prices, not the last days. Source: [Page & Clemen 2013](https://people.duke.edu/~clemen/bio/Published%20Papers/45.PredictionMarkets-Page&Clemen-EJ-2013.pdf)
- **[WEAK, commentary, Jul 2026]** Books can thin out a few hours before resolution, leaving "air pockets." The last trade can then sit at an extreme price. Not a study. Source: [CryptoDaily](https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/07/prediction-markets-vs-options-pricing-gaps)

### Inferences
- The final-hours volatility rule, p × (1 − p) ÷ time left, gives the bot a simple, tested yardstick. A favourite at 92c with 7 minutes left has a known "normal" swing size. Moves much bigger than that are unusual and worth flagging. This is a risk-control use, not a new edge.
- The 5-minute manipulation evidence is the clearest "late reversal" pattern found. It is concentrated near 50c and in thin hours, and much weaker at 15 minutes. That matches the bot's current design (15-minute markets, heavy favourites). It is a reason not to move to 5-minute contracts.
- Underreaction near the end (clutch time) and the step-like late calibration both point the same way: late prices lag the truth. That is the same mechanism behind the favourite edge the bot already trades, not a separate strategy.

### Gaps
- No study of "pinning" in prediction markets: prices sticking at a round number or strike before close.
- No Kalshi-wide study of how often a 90c+ price reverses in the final minutes, by category, outside BTC 15-minute markets.
- The settlement-manipulation study covers about two months of one Polymarket product. It used Binance spot as its measure of the settlement price. Kalshi settles on the CF Benchmarks BRTI index instead (per this project's own Oct 16 check), so the size of the effect on Kalshi is untested.
