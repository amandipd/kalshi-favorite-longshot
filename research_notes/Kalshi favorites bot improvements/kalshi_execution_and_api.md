# Kalshi order execution, fees and API capabilities for a minute-8 favorites bot

Research date: 2026-10-09 (US time; Kalshi API timestamps were already 2026-10-10 UTC). Primary sources: docs.kalshi.com pages, Kalshi help center, and live calls to Kalshi's public REST API (`https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2`) run from the project's research computer. The official fee schedule PDF (kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf) and kalshi.com/fee-schedule returned HTTP 429 on all four tries, so the fee notes rely on the API's own per-series fee fields, the docs' fee-rounding page, a search-engine summary of the official page, and secondary guides.

Note on the docs changelog: it contains five entries dated **October 15, 2026**, six days after today, written in the present tense and not marked "upcoming" ([Kalshi changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)). Treat the newest changelog dates with care.

Bot context checked in the repo: `bot/kalshi_bot/kalshi.py` already uses the V2 order endpoint `POST /portfolio/events/orders` and the base URL `https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2`. The repo lists these 15-minute series: KXBTC15M, KXETH15M, KXSOL15M, KXXRP15M, KXDOGE15M, KXBNB15M, KXHYPE15M, KXZEC15M, KXNEAR15M, KXGOLD15M, KXSILVER15M, KXWTI15M, KXNATGAS15M, KXCOPPER15M, KXPLATINUM15M, KXPALLADIUM15M and others.

## 1. Fee schedule (taker, maker, multipliers, 2025-2026 changes, rounding) and rebate, liquidity, market-maker or volume programs

### Takeaway
Every 15-minute crypto and commodity series the bot trades reports `fee_type: "quadratic"` with `fee_multiplier: 1` in the live API. Kalshi's docs define that as the general taker fee with no maker-fee section, so **resting (maker) fills on these series should cost nothing**. The taker fee is still 0.07 x C x P x (1-P). The bigger fee news is rounding: accounts held directly with Kalshi now round fees to $0.0001, not up to the next cent, and over-rounding is refunded across an order's fills. That cuts the taker fee on a $10 order at 95-99c by roughly 17-65%. The only open liquidity reward in the 15-minute family is on KXCRYPTOLEAD15M ($20 per 15-minute market). Crypto and commodity 15-minute markets have **designated** liquidity providers, which requires a signed Market Maker Agreement. No active volume programs were found.

### Cited findings
**Live per-series fee settings (primary, pulled from the API on 2026-10-09)**
- `GET /series/{ticker}` returned `fee_type: "quadratic"`, `fee_multiplier: 1`, `exchange_index: 2`, `frequency: "fifteen_min"` for KXBTC15M, KXETH15M, KXSOL15M, KXXRP15M, KXDOGE15M, KXBNB15M, KXHYPE15M, KXZEC15M, KXNEAR15M, KXGOLD15M, KXSILVER15M, KXWTI15M, KXNATGAS15M, KXCOPPER15M, KXPLATINUM15M and KXPALLADIUM15M. The `last_updated_ts` values were Oct 7-8, 2026. Source: [Kalshi API, e.g. /series/KXBTC15M](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series/KXBTC15M)
- `fee_type` has four values. `quadratic` is "described by the General Trading Fees Table". `quadratic_with_maker_fees` uses the same table plus the schedule's separate maker-fee section. `quadratic_with_combo_maker_fees` is "the same maker-fee structure" with a maker multiplier of 0.5 instead of 0.25. `flat` is "described by the Specific Trading Fees Table". `fee_multiplier` is "a floating point multiplier applied to the fee calculations." Source: [Kalshi docs: Get Series](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-series.md)

**Official schedule as summarized by search (PDF not readable here)**
- The official fee page lists a standard taker fee of $0.07 to $1.75 per 100 contracts on most markets, with multiplier 1. Combos (excluding uncorrelated NFL) carry maker fees at 50% of the taker fee. Some non-standard series (e.g. ATP tennis matches, US gas price markets) list maker fees of $0.02-$0.44 per 100 contracts. The page said no fee changes were scheduled. The PDF is titled "Fee Schedule for July 2026 - 7.7.26 Update", is effective July 7, 2026, and says resting orders are not charged unless listed in the "Maker Fees" section. Sources: [Kalshi fee schedule PDF](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf); [kalshi.com/fee-schedule](https://kalshi.com/fee-schedule) (both from a search summary; both returned HTTP 429 when fetched directly)

**Maker-fee history**
- Kalshi charged only takers, 0.07 x P x (1-P) per contract with the total rounded up to the nearest cent, and "began to charge fees on Makers after April 2025". Source: [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan, "Makers and Takers", Jan 2026 version](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)
- A guide dated Aug 6, 2026 says resting orders "pay a quarter of that rate, and only on designated markets", which matches the 0.25 maker multiplier for `quadratic_with_maker_fees` series. Source: [Predictefy, Kalshi Fees Explained](https://blog.predictefy.com/kalshi-fees)
- Changelog, Aug 2026: "Maker fees will be enabled at 5:00 AM ET on Thursday, August 20" for combos. The combo maker fee uses multiplier 0.5 rather than the standard 0.25. A **"quoter fee swap"** started Aug 21: "For combo trades, if a quoter executes against an order that has rested on the book for less than five seconds", the quoter pays the maker fee and the resting order pays the taker fee. Combos made entirely of independent NFL legs, created after Aug 19, have no maker fee. Source: [Kalshi changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)
- Third-party sources disagree on 15-minute maker fees. One says KXBTC15M has no maker fee. Another says small maker fees round to $0.00. A third describes an older flat $0.0025 per contract maker fee. Sources: [search results incl. predictionmarketspicks.com](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/kalshi-bitcoin-15-minute-markets), [kalshibacktest.com](https://kalshibacktest.com/resources/kalshi-btc-15-minute-markets) (snippets only, not fetched)

**Rounding rules (primary)**
- Each fill produces a trade fee (the model fee rounded **up to $0.000001**), a rounding fee, and a rebate. The rounding accumulator "is maintained per order across all fills regardless of whether the fills are taker or maker". Accumulated rounding is refunded in balance-precision increments, but a fill's net fee never goes below zero. Source: [Kalshi docs: Fee Rounding](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/fee_rounding.md)
- "Direct member balances align to $0.0001 (0.01¢). Non-direct member balances align to $0.01 (1¢)." The worked example is an FCM-cleared (non-direct) buy with revenue -$0.055 and model fee $0.00363825: trade fee $0.003639, balance change -$0.06. Source: [Kalshi docs: Fee Rounding](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/fee_rounding.md)
- Predictefy agrees: rounding is per order, direct accounts round "to a hundredth of a cent", broker-cleared accounts round to the cent, and overpayment is "rebated across an order's fills." Source: [Predictefy](https://blog.predictefy.com/kalshi-fees)
- The order response returns `average_fee_paid` ("volume-weighted average fee per contract for fills from this request"). This is the only fee field in the V2 create-order response. Source: [Kalshi docs: Create Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/create-order-v2.md)

**Incentive programs: live API data (primary, pulled 2026-10-09/10)**
- `GET /incentive_programs?status=active` returned **6,222 active programs, all of type `liquidity`, and zero `volume` programs**. The only 15-minute programs were 5 on **KXCRYPTOLEAD15M** (BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP/HYPE legs). Each had `incentive_description: "series_lip"`, `period_reward: 200000` (centi-cents, i.e. **$20.00 per 15-minute market**), `target_size_fp: "1000.00"`, `discount_factor_bps: 5000`, and ran for exactly the market's 15-minute window. KXBTC15M, KXETH15M, KXGOLD15M and the other up/down series had **no** open liquidity programs. Source: [Kalshi API /incentive_programs](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/incentive_programs?status=active)
- Endpoint docs: incentive types are `liquidity`, `volume`, `margin_maker_volume` and `margin_taker_volume`. Fields include `period_reward` ("Total reward for the period in centi-cents"), `discount_factor_bps`, `target_size_fp` and `max_reward_per_account`. Source: [Kalshi docs: Get Incentives](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/incentive-programs/get-incentives.md)
- How the Liquidity Incentive Program (LIP) scores (help center, via search summary): Kalshi snapshots the book once per second at a random time within each second. Orders at the best bid or ask get full credit, and orders further away are discounted by the Discount Factor. Target Size is 100-20,000 contracts. Payout = your score / all scores x pool, e.g. 20% of qualifying liquidity in a $100 period earns $20. The help center listed the program window as Sep 15, 2025 to Sep 1, 2026. Source: [Kalshi help: Liquidity Incentive Program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program). The CFTC filing caps each period at 31 days or less, the discount factor at 1.00 or less, and the per-day reward at $10-$1,000. Source: [CFTC filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules07152610358.pdf) (search summary). **Conflict:** the help center's Sep 1, 2026 end date is past, but the live API shows thousands of active liquidity programs, so the program has evidently been renewed or extended.
- **Designated Liquidity Provider Program:** "Members who have executed a Market Maker Agreement with Kalshi may become a Designated Liquidity Provider" for Incentivized Series. Members join by emailing Kalshi to enter a periodic "auction" stating the minimum reward they would accept. Active programs include **"Crypto 15min markets" (2 providers)**, **"Commodities 15min markets" (1 provider)**, "Crypto Hourly & Daily" (1), "Hourly Commodities" (1), "FX 15min markets" (2), and perpetuals. All terminate 12/31/26. The page gives no reward amounts or quoting requirements; those are in a cover letter and Appendix A PDF. Source: [Kalshi help: Liquidity Provider Program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15410219-liquidity-provider-program)
- Sports prop combo legs program: market makers in sports props get up to 25% of the fees from combos containing those props, paid monthly as trading credits. Not relevant to 15-minute markets. Sources: [Kalshi help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/17184676-sports-prop-combo-market-component-legs-liquidity-incentive-program); [NextPredict](https://nextpredict.io/market-news/industry/kalshi-files-prop-market-liquidity-incentives/)
- Volume Incentive Program: a TradersUnion headline and snippet say Kalshi will end it "no earlier than Oct. 13" after CFTC scrutiny over inflated perpetual-futures volume. The article returned 403 and was not read. Source: [TradersUnion](https://tradersunion.com/news/cryptocurrency-news/show/3578035-kalshi-ends-liquidity-incentive-program/). This fits with the API showing zero active volume programs.
- Volume tiers: event-contract fees have no volume tiers. Perpetual-futures fees are tiered by volume, and makers pay less there. Source: [Predictefy](https://blog.predictefy.com/kalshi-fees); `GET /margin/fee_tier_rates` was added Sep 3, 2026 for margin products. Source: [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)

### Inferences
- With `fee_type: quadratic` on every 15-minute series the bot trades, a resting buy that gets filled should pay **$0 fee**. A taker buy at 95c pays about 0.33c per contract (at $0.0001 precision) or 0.40c (rounded to the cent). With an edge of "a few tenths of a cent", the maker-or-taker choice alone can decide whether the trade makes money.
- The bot's fee model `ceil(0.07*C*P*(1-P))` rounded to the cent is probably **too pessimistic** if the account is a direct Kalshi member. Retail kalshi.com accounts are most likely direct members, but this is unverified. At 97c it overstates the fee by about 50%, and at 99c by about 43%. This is cheap to check: compare `average_fee_paid` (or `fee_cost` from the fills endpoint) on the 76 real orders against both rounding rules. If the fees are not whole cents, the account is on $0.0001 precision and the backtest's break-even line moves.
- The per-order rounding accumulator means splitting a buy into many small orders costs more than one larger order under cent rounding. Under $0.0001 rounding the difference is negligible.
- The two designated crypto 15-minute liquidity providers are probably a large share of the resting asks the bot is racing for. They are under contract and likely latency-optimized. Retail cannot join without a Market Maker Agreement and winning a reverse auction.
- The combo "quoter fee swap" shows Kalshi is willing to charge orders that rest under 5 seconds as takers. It applies only to combos today. If it spread to 15-minute series, a "post just before minute 8" tactic could lose its zero-fee advantage. Watch `GET /series/fee_changes`.

### Gaps
- Could not read the official fee PDF or fee-schedule page (HTTP 429). The full list of `quadratic_with_maker_fees` series, any series multipliers other than 1, and the PDF's change history are therefore unverified here. The live API fields are the best available primary evidence.
- Not verified whether a retail kalshi.com account counts as a "direct member" ($0.0001 fee precision) or rounds to the cent.
- Designated LP reward amounts and quoting obligations for crypto and commodity 15-minute markets are not public (Appendix A not accessible).
- Whether the Volume Incentive Program actually ended Oct 13 could not be confirmed from a readable source.

## 2. Order types and parameters in Trade API v2, and which help a minute-8 favorite buyer

### Takeaway
Create Order V2 supports `fill_or_kill`, `good_till_canceled` (with optional `expiration_time`, which works as good-til-time), and `immediate_or_cancel`, plus `post_only`, a **required** `self_trade_prevention_type`, `cancel_order_on_pause`, `reduce_only`, `client_order_id`, `order_group_id` and `exchange_index`. Prices are fixed-point dollars with 2-4 decimals, and **contract counts can be fractional down to 0.01**. Two facts matter most for this bot. First, **KXBTC15M and KXETH15M quote in 0.1c ticks between 90c and $1.00**. Second, amending price loses queue priority, while lowering size or changing only the expiry keeps it.

### Cited findings
- Endpoint `POST /portfolio/events/orders` (CreateOrderV2). Production base URL `https://external-api.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2`, demo `https://external-api.demo.kalshi.co/trade-api/v2`. The legacy `/portfolio/orders` endpoint "will be deprecated no earlier than May 6, 2026." Source: [Kalshi docs: Create Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/create-order-v2.md)
- Required fields: `ticker`, `side` (`bid` buys YES, `ask` sells YES), `count` ("fixed-point contract count string... 0 to 2 decimal places, and fractional contracts down to 0.01 are supported"), `price` (fixed-point dollars, "2 to 4 decimal places, and valid intervals depend on the market's price level structure"), `time_in_force` (`fill_or_kill`, `good_till_canceled`, `immediate_or_cancel`; "GTT is not a valid API value. Use good_till_canceled with expiration_time"; IOC cannot be combined with `expiration_time`), and `self_trade_prevention_type` (`taker_at_cross` cancels the incoming order; `maker` "cancels the resting maker order and continues matching"). Source: [Create Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/create-order-v2.md)
- Optional fields: `expiration_time` (Unix seconds), `post_only` (the spec gives no description), `cancel_order_on_pause` (cancels the order if trading is paused), `reduce_only` (only allowed with IOC), `client_order_id`, `order_group_id`, `subaccount`, `exchange_index` (shard; auto-routes when `ticker` is given). Source: [Create Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/create-order-v2.md)
- Response fields: `fill_count` ("Number of contracts filled immediately upon placement"), `remaining_count` (for IOC, the final state after the unfilled part is cancelled), `average_fill_price`, `average_fee_paid`, and `ts_ms` (matching-engine timestamp in ms). Source: [Create Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/create-order-v2.md)
- There is no separate "market order" type in V2. A marketable limit order with IOC or FOK does that job. Source: inferred from the V2 parameter list above, which has no `type` field ([Create Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/create-order-v2.md))
- **Amend** (`POST /portfolio/events/orders/{order_id}/amend`) can change price, count and `expiration_time`. "Amending only expiry or decreasing size preserves queue position. Increasing size or changing price forfeits queue position and places the order at the back of the queue." An amend that crosses the book fills, and the response returns `fill_count`, `average_fill_price` and `average_fee_paid`. Source: [Kalshi docs: Amend Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/amend-order-v2.md). Since Oct 1, 2026, REST amend accepts `expiration_time`, and expiry-only amendments keep priority. Source: [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)
- Other order endpoints: Batch Create V2, Decrease V2, Cancel V2, Batch Cancel V2, and Cancel All (added Aug 27, 2026). Sources: [docs index](https://docs.kalshi.com/llms.txt); [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)
- **Order groups:** you set a contracts limit (1 to 1,000,000). If fills of the group's orders within a **rolling 15-second window** exceed the limit, the group triggers and cancels all its resting orders. New orders are rejected while it is triggered. A group can also be triggered manually, reset or deleted. Limit: 100,000 groups per user (raised Aug 13, 2026). Sources: [Kalshi docs: Order Groups](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/order_groups.md); [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)
- **Tick sizes (primary, live API):** open KXBTC15M and KXETH15M markets report `price_level_structure: "tapered_deci_cent"` with `price_ranges` steps of $0.001 on $0.00-$0.10, $0.01 on $0.10-$0.90, and **$0.001 on $0.90-$1.00**. A sample KXBTC15M market showed `yes_bid_size_fp: "4373.54"`, a fractional size, so fractional contracts trade in these markets. Source: [Kalshi API /markets?series_ticker=KXBTC15M](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXBTC15M&status=open&limit=1)
- The changelog lists newer structures (center_whole_edge_half_cent, ..., center_quint_edge_deci_cent, and center_deci_edge_centi_cent with $0.0001 ticks above $0.99). Pilot markets switched the week of July 27, 2026, and higher-volume markets from the week of Aug 3. `market_lifecycle_v2` events carry `price_ranges`, so a structure change can be detected live. Source: [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)
- `fractional_trading_enabled` and `response_price_units` were removed July 9, 2026. `liquidity_dollars` was removed Oct 1, 2026 in favor of `yes_bid_size_fp` and `yes_ask_size_fp`. Source: [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)

### Inferences
- **Check the bot's price handling for sub-cent ticks.** Above 90c the best ask can be 0.953 and so on. If the bot rounds the ask to whole cents, it can end up posting an IOC below the real ask (zero fill) or above it (overpaying). Some of the 17 zero-fill orders may come from this; worth checking in the order logs.
- **IOC with a price cushion, not exactly at the best ask.** Set the IOC limit to the highest price still worth paying (e.g. best ask + 0.2-0.5c, capped at the strategy's maximum). An order that arrives after someone lifted the top level can then still fill at the next 0.1c levels instead of cancelling with nothing. Price improvement is automatic, since a limit fills at resting prices.
- **Fractional sizing.** `count` accepts 2 decimals, so $10 at 95c can buy 10.52 contracts instead of 10. This uses about 5% more of the budget, and there is no whole-contract rounding loss near 99c ($10/0.991 = 10.09).
- **Resting bid with an automatic expiry.** A `good_till_canceled` buy with `post_only: true`, `expiration_time` set to a few seconds after the decision time, and `cancel_order_on_pause: true` lets the bot sit at or near the bid without ever paying taker fees. It also cancels itself if the bot dies. Amending the price later resets queue position, so pick the price once. Lowering size or changing the expiry keeps the position.
- **Join the queue one tick better.** With 0.1c ticks above 90c, bidding 0.951 when the bid is 0.95 puts the bot first in the queue for 0.1c, which is less than the 0.33c taker fee it avoids.
- **Order groups as a safety cap.** One group per 15-minute cycle with limit = intended contracts stops resting orders from over-filling if several markets fill at once. A manual trigger is a one-call "cancel everything" for the bot.
- **Batch create** can send all of a minute's IOC orders (crypto plus metals closing at the same time) in one HTTP round trip. It is billed per order, so it saves latency, not rate-limit tokens.
- **Self-trade prevention is required in V2.** `taker_at_cross` is the safe default if the bot ever has a resting bid and an IOC in the same market.
- Pass `exchange_index: 2` explicitly. The live series records show all these 15-minute series on shard 2. Per the rate-limit page, leaving it out with a ticker can bill every shard's write bucket (see section 4).

### Gaps
- The V2 spec does not describe `post_only` behavior (whether it rejects or reprices an order that would cross). This needs a demo-environment test.
- Not confirmed whether a 0.01-contract minimum is accepted on every 15-minute series, or whether minimum order value rules apply.
- Not verified which price structure each commodity 15-minute series uses. The KXGOLD15M and KXWTI15M queries returned no open market at query time.

## 3. WebSocket feeds: channels, latency, keeping a local order book, versus REST polling

### Takeaway
One authenticated WebSocket (`wss://external-api-ws.kalshi.com`) carries `orderbook_delta` (a snapshot followed by signed deltas with sequence numbers), `ticker`, `trade`, `fill`, `user_orders`, `market_positions`, `market_lifecycle_v2`, `order_group_updates`, and **CF Benchmarks index channels, including 5 Hz** (`cfbenchmarks_value_5hz`). CF Benchmarks' Real Time Index is the source the 15-minute crypto markets settle on. Messages carry `sending_ts_ms` and `ts_ms`, so a bot can measure feed latency itself. No published WebSocket-vs-REST benchmark was found.

### Cited findings
- Endpoint `wss://external-api-ws.kalshi.com`. Authentication uses API key headers in the handshake, even for public market-data channels. Source: [Kalshi docs: WebSocket Connection](https://docs.kalshi.com/websockets/websocket-connection.md)
- Channel list: `orderbook_delta`, `ticker` (optional `send_initial_snapshot`), `trade`, `fill`, `market_positions`, `market_lifecycle_v2`, `multivariate_market_lifecycle`, `communications` (RFQs and quotes), `order_group_updates`, `user_orders`, `cfbenchmarks_value`, `cfbenchmarks_value_5hz`, `pyth_value`. Error codes 26 and 27 enforce a per-subscription market limit and a per-subscription command rate limit; the page gives no numbers. Source: [WebSocket Connection](https://docs.kalshi.com/websockets/websocket-connection.md)
- `orderbook_delta` "Sends `orderbook_snapshot` first, then incremental `orderbook_delta` updates." Snapshots carry `yes_dollars_fp` and `no_dollars_fp` arrays of `[price_in_dollars, contract_count_fp]` pairs. Deltas carry `price_dollars`, signed `delta_fp`, `side` (yes/no), `ts_ms`, and your own `client_order_id` when your order caused the change. `seq` is a "Sequential number that should be checked if you want to guarantee you received all the messages." `update_subscription` with `get_snapshot` returns a fresh snapshot without changing the subscription. `sending_ts_ms` is "when Kalshi queued this message at the network layer." Source: [Kalshi docs: Orderbook Updates](https://docs.kalshi.com/websockets/orderbook-updates.md)
- The book is expressed as YES bids and NO bids. A YES ask at price p is the NO bid at 1-p. Sources: [Orderbook Updates](https://docs.kalshi.com/websockets/orderbook-updates.md); the Whelan paper explains the same structure ([Bürgi et al.](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf))
- Recent changes: `sending_ts_ms` on all messages (Oct 1, 2026). Optional `permessage-deflate` compression for public connections (Sep 24). Since Sep 17, subscriptions are ready once acknowledged, which fixed "a race that could skip early events". `cfbenchmarks_value_5hz` was added Sep 3 and `pyth_value` July 23. `user_orders` includes `exchange_index` (Aug 27). Trade messages include `is_block_trade` (Aug 13). Source: [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)
- KXBTC15M settles on CF Benchmarks' Real Time Index (RTI): "At the last minute before expiration, 60 RTI prices are collected. The official and final value is the average of these prices." Source: [Kalshi API /series/KXBTC15M product_metadata](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series/KXBTC15M)
- FIX also has a Market Data session (KalshiMD, port 8233) with book snapshots and incremental updates. Source: [Kalshi docs: FIX Connectivity](https://docs.kalshi.com/fix/connectivity.md)
- Measured from the test machine's home connection: a fresh REST `GET /markets/{ticker}/orderbook` took 146-169 ms to first byte, including new TCP+TLS setup (TCP connect 32-43 ms). Responses showed `X-Cache: Miss from cloudfront`, so order book data is not served stale from the CDN. A cached `GET /exchange/status` took 91-120 ms. The same order book call to `external-api.kalshi.com` took 250-290 ms. Source: the project's own measurement, 2026-10-09, curl against [api.elections.kalshi.com](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/exchange/status)

### Inferences
- **Event-driven firing instead of a 2-second delay.** Subscribe to `orderbook_delta` for the upcoming markets, keep a local book (snapshot, then apply deltas, check `seq`, resync with `get_snapshot` on a gap), and send the IOC the moment minute 8 ends. This removes the deliberate ~2 s wait, the polling interval, and one REST round trip (~80-100 ms from the test machine on a kept-alive connection). It cannot remove the order's own trip to the engine.
- **Streaming the settlement index.** `cfbenchmarks_value_5hz` gives the bot the same RTI the market settles on, five times a second, from Kalshi itself. That enables (a) fair-value checks at minute 8 without a third-party feed and (b) cancelling a resting bid when the index moves against the favorite, which is the main defense against adverse selection (section 5).
- **Fill confirmation.** Subscribe to `fill` and `user_orders` so the bot learns about resting-order fills and cancellations without polling.
- Use `sending_ts_ms` against local receipt time (with an NTP-synced clock) to measure one-way feed latency from home versus a cloud VM.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of Kalshi WebSocket latency versus REST, or of how often book updates are batched or throttled.
- No published connection-count limits or per-subscription market caps (only the error codes).
- Whether the CF Benchmarks channels need any special entitlement was not checked.

## 4. Rate limits by tier, how to qualify, and FIX availability

### Takeaway
Rate limits are token buckets. Most requests cost 10 tokens, so Basic is about 20 reads/s and 10 writes/s, and **Advanced (about 30 reads and 30 writes per second) is free: one API call upgrades the account.** Higher tiers need trailing-30-day volume of at least 0.075% of exchange volume, which a $10-per-order bot will not reach. The FIX API exists, but order entry is gated through institutional@kalshi.com. PrivateLink needs Premier or above, and VPC peering needs Prime or above. The bot's few orders per minute are nowhere near any limit.

### Cited findings
- Per-second budgets (event contracts), read/write: Basic 200/100, Advanced 300/300, Expert 600/600, Premier 1,200/1,200, Paragon 2,400/2,400, Prime 4,800/4,800, Prestige 12,000/9,600. "Most requests cost 10 tokens." `GET /account/endpoint_costs` lists non-default costs. Source: [Kalshi docs: Rate Limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits.md)
- Qualifying: Basic comes with signup. **Advanced: "Call the Upgrade Account API Usage Level endpoint."** Expert and above are earned from volume share (trailing 30-day volume / (previous month's exchange volume x 2), counting both sides) or assigned by Kalshi. Earn/Keep thresholds: Expert 0.075%/0.05%, Premier 0.125%/0.10%, Paragon 0.25%/0.20%, Prime 0.50%/0.40%, Prestige 1.00%/0.80%. A qualifying daily review grants the tier for 30 days. Source: [Rate Limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits.md)
- Tier changes: qualification thresholds were halved June 25, 2026. Premier through Prestige limits were raised 20% on Sep 24, 2026, which the table above may or may not already include. Source: [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)
- The write bucket covers order placement, amends, cancels, order groups, RFQ quotes and block-trade accepts. REST and FIX share buckets. Write buckets above Basic hold 3 seconds of budget (bursts up to 3x). Basic's write bucket holds 1 second. Source: [Rate Limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits.md)
- Batch create is billed per item (25 orders = 250 tokens). Batch cancel costs 2 tokens per order. "The whole batch must fit in the bucket at once, or the entire batch is rejected." Source: [Rate Limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits.md)
- Shard routing: `exchange_index` of 1 or more bills that shard's write bucket. "-1, or omitting it when market_ticker is provided, bills every shard's Write bucket." Batch creates and cancels bill the unscoped bucket. Source: [Rate Limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits.md). Crypto moved to a dedicated exchange instance (announced Aug 24, 2026), and new commodities markets go to shard 2 from Sep 10. Source: [changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog/index.md)
- A rate-limited call gets `429` with `{"error": "too many requests"}`, no `Retry-After` header, and no cooldown penalty. Source: [Rate Limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits.md)
- FIX: production order-entry host `mm.fix.elections.kalshi.com` and market-data host `marketdata.fix.elections.kalshi.com`. Sessions: KalshiNR 8228 (order entry, no retransmission), KalshiRT 8230, Drop Copy 8229, Post Trade 8231, RFQ 8232, Market Data 8233. Order entry with retransmission and Post Trade: "Contact institutional@kalshi.com for access". Drop Copy is "gated access". One FIX connection per API key. "Members on the Premier tier or above can contact institutional@kalshi.com to provision a PrivateLink endpoint", and Prime or above can discuss VPC peering, so "FIX traffic is routed entirely within the AWS backbone". Source: [Kalshi docs: FIX Connectivity](https://docs.kalshi.com/fix/connectivity.md)

### Inferences
- Advanced costs nothing and triples the write burst, so the bot should call the upgrade endpoint. It matters only if the bot ever fires many orders in the same second (e.g. 15+ markets closing together, plus cancels).
- FIX is unlikely to be available or worthwhile for a $10-order retail bot. PrivateLink and VPC peering, the real latency tools, are reserved for Premier or Prime volume.
- Passing `exchange_index: 2` on single orders avoids billing every shard's bucket. It does not matter at this bot's volume, but it is free.

### Gaps
- The docs do not say whether the "KalshiNR" order-entry session (no retransmission) is open to retail API keys without contacting Kalshi.
- Not clear whether the Sep 24 20% increase is already in the published table.

## 5. Resting limit orders to buy favorites (e.g. joining the bid at 93-95c before minute 8): fill probability, adverse selection, queue position

### Takeaway
The economics favor trying to be the maker: no maker fee on these series, sub-cent ticks for cheap queue-jumping, and academic evidence that Kalshi makers beat takers, especially on high-priced contracts. The risk is classic adverse selection. A resting bid on the favorite gets hit mostly when sellers know the underlying just moved against it (latency arbitrage from spot feeds). Polymarket added taker fees to its 15-minute crypto markets to fund maker rebates, which shows the venue sees maker-side liquidity in these markets as needing support. No public study measures fill rates or adverse selection for resting orders in Kalshi 15-minute markets. This has to be measured with the bot's own data.

### Cited findings
- Whelan et al. used Kalshi transaction data (~314k contracts; sample cut off before Kalshi began charging makers after April 2025). Makers bought **56.5% of contracts traded at 90-99c** (60,024 of 106,209). Average post-fee return: **Makers -9.64% vs Takers -31.46%**. "Makers who buy contracts costing 50c and over earn a 2.6% rate of return." "A 95c contract that wins 98% of the time has a pre-fee average return of 3.1%." Makers' high-price returns were mostly not significant day by day. On closing-day prices, Makers' losses looked "similar to those for Takers... over-optimism from Makers being a bigger issue as the market comes closer to closing." Sources: [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan, "Makers and Takers" (Jan 2026)](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf); also [CESifo WP](https://www.ifo.de/en/cesifo/publications/2025/working-paper/makers-and-takers-economics-kalshi-prediction-market)
- The paper's model: traders with strong beliefs take immediately, while others post as makers and accept a probability of not being matched, which "results in worse returns for Takers." Source: [Bürgi et al.](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)
- Adverse selection on binaries: resting quotes fill when new information has made them wrong. Vendor guides recommend cancelling stale orders quickly, and say losses are larger on binaries because contracts settle to $1 or $0. Sources: [botforkalshi: Kalshi Market Making](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/kalshi-market-making-strategy); [startpolymarket: Market Making](https://startpolymarket.com/strategies/market-making/) (vendor content, search snippets)
- Polymarket enabled taker fees on 15-minute crypto markets to fund a Maker Rebates program that pays daily USDC rebates to liquidity providers. Current docs extend it to 5-minute crypto, NCAAB and Serie A, and say the rebate percentage is at Polymarket's discretion. Sources: [Polymarket docs: Maker Rebates](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/market-makers/maker-rebates-program); [The Block, Jan 2026](https://www.theblock.co/post/384461/polymarket-adds-taker-fees-to-15-minute-crypto-markets-to-fund-liquidity-rebates). A search summary attributes the fee to an aim of neutralizing latency-arbitrage strategies under the earlier zero-fee structure. That wording was not verified in a fetched article.
- An academic dataset (OpenMarket) synchronizes Polymarket BTC 15-minute order books with Binance for high-frequency research. It is a possible empirical source for adverse-selection estimates. Source: [arXiv 2607.26245](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26245) (not read)
- TurbineFi's backtest inventory (Sep 29, updated Oct 8, 2026) of 5,732 KXBTC15M bot variants: a maker sweep had 26 of 100 positive variants and "its permutation test failed". Late-favorite studies: "BTC Extreme Hold" 60/100 positive, "Late Expensive Momentum" 36/100. The authors caution that "maker studies use different assumptions from directional taker strategies" and flag extreme-price fill artifacts. Source: [TurbineFi](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/5732-backtests-kalshi-bitcoin-15-minute-bots-2026)
- Queue mechanics: price-time priority is implied by the amend rules. Lowering size or changing only the expiry keeps position; raising size or changing price goes to the back of the queue. Source: [Amend Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/amend-order-v2.md)
- Liquidity is thick at the top of these books. A sample KXBTC15M market showed 4,373.54 contracts on the bid at 84c and 1,995.43 on the ask at 85c. Source: [Kalshi API markets query](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXBTC15M&status=open&limit=1). Designated providers (2 for crypto 15-minute, 1 for commodities) are under contract to quote. Source: [Liquidity Provider Program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15410219-liquidity-provider-program)

### Inferences
- **Where the adverse selection comes from.** A resting bid on the favorite at 94c is hit by someone selling the favorite (buying NO at about 6c). In a market tied to BTC spot, the sellers most likely to hit a stale bid are the ones who saw CF Benchmarks or Coinbase move against the favorite a few hundred ms earlier. Unconditional maker win rates will therefore overstate what a resting bid earns. The bot needs a fast cancel rule tied to `cfbenchmarks_value_5hz` (or a direct exchange feed) and to time left in the market.
- **A hybrid worth testing.** Slightly before the minute-8 decision point, post a `post_only` bid at best bid + 0.1c with `expiration_time` = decision time + a few seconds, inside an order group capped at the target size. If it fills before the decision, the bot saves the fee and the spread. If not, it fires the IOC with a price cushion at the decision time. Cancel the bid if the index moves against the favorite by more than a threshold. This replaces "race for the ask" (the bot loses about 30% of contracts to faster takers) with "be in the queue first."
- **Measure before scaling.** Log every resting bid: time posted, queue depth ahead (from the local book), whether it filled, time to fill, and outcome. Compare win rates of filled resting bids against IOC fills at the same price band. The edge survives only if filled-maker win rates stay close to the backtest's unconditional win rates.
- The Whelan paper's sample is mostly longer-dated markets before April 2025. Its maker advantage on 90-99c contracts suggests resting bids can pay, but it is not direct evidence for 15-minute crypto markets, where latency arbitrage is far more intense.
- Joining a 4,000-contract queue at the same price as the designated providers means a $10 bid fills only after thousands of contracts ahead of it. That is why improving by one 0.1c tick, which puts the bot first in the queue, matters on these books.

### Gaps
- No public data on fill probability, queue dynamics or adverse-selection costs for resting orders in Kalshi 15-minute markets, nor firsthand Reddit or Discord reports found within this search budget.
- The Whelan paper does not break returns out for crypto or 15-minute markets, or by seconds before close.
- Polymarket's maker rebate percentage and its effect on 15-minute market spreads were not quantified.

## 6. Server and matching-engine location, and latency from home versus a cloud VM

### Takeaway
Kalshi does not publish its location. Third-party monitoring places the origin and matching engine in **AWS us-east-2 (Ohio)** behind CloudFront. The "1 ms to Kalshi from Chicago" claims measure the CloudFront edge, not the engine. One developer measured about 27-29 ms order acknowledgement from a US-East host. From the test machine's home connection, REST calls take roughly 90-170 ms including connection setup. A VM in us-east-2 is the obvious step for a bot losing races at the ask, but it will not match designated market makers on PrivateLink.

### Cited findings
- "Fully centralized order book at api.elections.kalshi.com, behind AWS CloudFront CDN". Origin servers are in AWS us-east-2 (Ohio). The monitor measures REST round trips, refreshed every 5 s, and gives no Kalshi numbers on the About page. Source: [Glassnode latency monitor: About](https://latency.glassnode.com/prediction-markets/about)
- A GitHub latency write-up (search snippet; the repo now returns 404, so unverified) places the exchange in us-east-2 at about 21 ms TCP RTT from the author's host, with the CloudFront edge (JFK) 1.3 ms away. It reports order-ack round trip **27.3 ms p50** via the best host and **29.0 ms p50** direct to origin, with create, amend, decrease and cancel within about 0.5 ms of each other. Source: [YLiu-101/latency-expos](https://github.com/YLiu-101/latency-expos) (404 when fetched)
- QuantVPS (vendor selling Chicago hosting; published Mar 2, updated Oct 7, 2026) claims Kalshi servers are "likely located in the Chicago metro area", based on round trips "as low as 1.14ms" to api.elections.kalshi.com recorded in Feb 2026, and says home connections often exceed 100 ms. It does not mention CloudFront or measure order round trips. Sources: [QuantVPS: Where are Kalshi's servers](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/kalshi-servers-location). A search summary notes that pinging hosts behind CloudFront "does not measure the trading engine" (from a VPS vendor page, [TradoxVPS](https://tradoxvps.com/why-kalshi-traders-need-a-1ms-vps-in-chicago/), not fetched).
- FIX PrivateLink and VPC peering connect "from their AWS VPC" and keep traffic "entirely within the AWS backbone". This shows the exchange runs in AWS, though the docs name no region. Source: [FIX Connectivity](https://docs.kalshi.com/fix/connectivity.md)
- The order response includes `ts_ms` (matching-engine timestamp), and WebSocket messages include `sending_ts_ms`. Both let a bot measure its own one-way and round-trip latency. Sources: [Create Order V2](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/orders/create-order-v2.md); [Orderbook Updates](https://docs.kalshi.com/websockets/orderbook-updates.md)
- Own measurements from the test machine, 2026-10-09, through a nearby CloudFront POP. Fresh-connection REST to `api.elections.kalshi.com`: TCP connect 31-86 ms, TLS done at 67-173 ms, order book first byte 146-169 ms (CloudFront miss, i.e. fetched from origin). `external-api.kalshi.com` (the base URL in the current docs) was consistently slower from there: 240-310 ms to first byte. Source: the project's own curl measurements against [api.elections.kalshi.com](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/exchange/status) and [external-api.kalshi.com](https://external-api.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/exchange/status)

### Inferences
- From the test machine, each order spends about 80-100 ms reaching the Ohio engine and confirming, even on a warm keep-alive connection (estimated from the timings above: first byte minus handshake). A us-east-2 VM would cut this to a few ms (Ohio intra-region) to the high 20s of ms (the US-East figure reported above). Add the 2-second fixed delay and the REST polling, and the bot is likely arriving hundreds of ms to seconds after competitors who stream and fire on the minute boundary. That fits the 30% shortfall and the 17 empty fills.
- Reuse a single HTTP/2 or keep-alive session for orders. A new TCP+TLS handshake costs another 70-170 ms from the test machine.
- Keep using `api.elections.kalshi.com` unless Kalshi announces it is going away. From the test machine it measured roughly 100 ms faster than `external-api.kalshi.com`. Re-measure from any cloud VM, since routing differs.
- Even in us-east-2, the bot will not beat designated providers on PrivateLink or co-located HFT takers. Latency improvements help most when combined with being in the queue first (section 5) rather than winning a pure race to the ask.

### Gaps
- No official Kalshi statement of the matching-engine region. The us-east-2 attribution rests on Glassnode and an unverifiable GitHub write-up.
- No measured order round trip from a us-east-2 EC2 instance found. Only the ~27-29 ms US-East figure, whose host location is unclear.
- Home-to-engine order round trips were not measured (no orders were placed). The figures above are GET requests.
