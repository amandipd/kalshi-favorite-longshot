# Cross-venue real-time signals for Kalshi 15-minute up/down favorites

Scope: external data that could tell us, at the end of minute 8, whether a 90-99.9c Kalshi favorite is about to lose. Researched 2026-10-09. Many sources here are preprints, theses, vendor blogs or third-party mirrors; each is flagged. Older (pre-2024) academic work is marked with its year.

Framing used throughout (an inference, not a sourced claim): with about 7 minutes left, a 90c+ favorite loses only if the index travels back across the opening reference before the closing 60-second average. External data can help three ways: (1) **nowcast**, a fresher read of where the settlement index is right now than Kalshi's quotes; (2) **volatility forecast**, a better estimate of how far the index can move in 7 minutes; (3) **direction**, a real predictor of drift. Most evidence below supports (1) and (2) far more than (3).

## 1. Lead-lag between venues (perps, CME, spot exchanges vs the CF Benchmarks composite)

### Takeaway
Older studies (2019-2022) agree that perpetual futures (perps: futures with no expiry, kept near spot by periodic "funding" payments) on offshore venues, especially Binance's USDT-margined perp, lead US spot exchanges and CME in price discovery (the process of new information first showing up in a price). Modern cross-venue lags are sub-second, which is far too short to predict a 7-minute outcome on its own. The usable value is a nowcast: rebuilding the settlement index live, and spotting when Kalshi quotes are stale during fast moves. No study measures perp-vs-BRTI lead in seconds directly.

### Cited findings
- Kalshi crypto contracts settle on CF Benchmarks Real-Time Indices (CFB RTIs, e.g. BRTI for bitcoin), which are real-time USD spot prices calculated from multiple exchanges. A 15-minute market compares the average of the minute before close with the average of the minute before open. Source: [Kalshi Help Center: Crypto Markets](https://help.kalshi.com/markets/popular-markets/crypto-markets)
- Rule text for a KXBTC15M market: Yes if "the simple average of the sixty seconds of CF Benchmarks' BRTI before 3:45 PM EDT ... is at least the simple average of the sixty seconds of CF Benchmarks' BRTI before 3:30 PM EDT". 60 RTI prints are collected in the last minute and averaged, rounded to 2 decimals. A tie resolves Yes. Source: [PMXT mirror of KXBTC15M rules](https://exchange.pmxt.dev/markets/kxbtc15m/bitcoin-price-abovebelow/kxbtc15m-26sep021545-45) (third-party mirror; check against Kalshi's rulebook)
- Active Kalshi 15-minute crypto series (as of Sep 26, 2026): KXBTC15M, KXETH15M, KXXRP15M, KXSOL15M, KXDOGE15M, KXHYPE15M, KXZEC15M, KXNEAR15M, KXBNB15M, plus KXCRYPTOLEAD15M ("Coin Race", which coin has the best return over the window). All settle on CF Benchmarks. Source: [PredictionMarketsPicks, "How Kalshi 15-Minute Markets Settle" (updated Sep 28, 2026)](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/how-kalshi-15-minute-markets-settle) (third-party)
- BRTI-family indices are computed every second from the "Relevant Order Books" (unmatched limit orders) of all Constituent Exchanges. CME describes the value as a weighted mid-price built from a price-volume curve, not a median. Source: [CME CF Cryptocurrency Benchmarks FAQ](https://www.cmegroup.com/articles/faqs/cme-cf-cryptocurrency-benchmarks-faq.hideRightRail.hideHeader.hideSubnav.hideFooter.html)
- Under the current method, crossed order books across venues stay in the price-volume curve. A June 10, 2026 consultation proposes adding an "uncrossing" step, so the index formula may change. Source: [CF Benchmarks consultation on CME CF Real-Time Indices methodology](https://www.cfbenchmarks.com/blog/announcement-of-consultation-on-changes-to-the-cme-cf-real-time-indices-methodology)
- The related BRRNY index (a once-daily New York reference rate, not the real-time BRTI) has used six constituent exchanges since Nov 22, 2022: Bitstamp, Coinbase, Gemini, itBit, LMAX Digital, Kraken. Source: [CF Benchmarks blog on BRRNY replication](https://www.cfbenchmarks.com/blog/optimizing-capital-efficiency-in-replicating-the-brrny-for-the-creation-and-redemption-of-shares-for-us-spot-bitcoin-etfs)
- Alexander & Heck, *Journal of Financial Stability* (minute-level data, pre-2020): perps and futures on unregulated exchanges (Huobi, OKEx, BitMEX) are "much the strongest" instruments for bitcoin price discovery. CME futures and US spot exchanges react to, rather than lead, moves on those venues. Source: [Sussex repository record](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Price_discovery_in_Bitcoin_the_impact_of_unregulated_markets/23308262) (abstract only; full text returned 403)
- "The Role of Binance in Bitcoin Volatility Transmission" (5-minute data, published 2022): the Binance USDT-margined perp is the main emitter of volatility, "continuously transmitting strong flows to all other instruments throughout the day". It receives only weak volatility flows from Binance spot, and even less from Coinbase. Sources: [arXiv 2107.00298](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00298); [Applied Mathematical Finance version](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/1350486X.2022.2125885)
- An NHH master's thesis (about 2021) finds Binance's linear (USDT-margined) perp leads the perp market, and finds a stronger leading role for CME bitcoin futures than earlier work did. Source: [NHH Open Access](https://openaccess.nhh.no/nhh-xmlui/handle/11250/2766908)
- A 2021 exhibit filed with the SEC (NYSE Arca) reported that CME led spot platforms by information share (a standard measure of which venue's price moves first) in 90% of evaluated months. Source: [SEC exhibit 34-93445](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nysearca/2021/34-93445-ex3a.pdf) (applicant-supplied analysis, 2021)
- Conflicting older view: Corbet et al. (2018) and Baur & Dimpfl (2019) found spot led futures in high-frequency data in CME's early years. Kapar & Olmo (2019) found CME dominant at daily frequency. Summarized in [Frijns et al. working paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/296424/B-Frijns-Bitcoin_Paper.pdf) and [Waikato working paper](https://repec.its.waikato.ac.nz/wai/econwp/1913.pdf) (2018-2019 data)
- Lags between modern venues are sub-second. A synchronized 2026 Polymarket-Binance dataset found a median cross-venue lag of about 16 ms on venue clocks (with about ±99 ms of offset ambiguity). Polymarket quotes responded to large Binance moves after a median of 347 ms. Source: [Young, "OpenMarket", arXiv 2607.26245 (Jul 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26245)
- Gold analog: a Michigan State dissertation found that modern markets spread new gold pricing information in under 100 ms. Sampling at 1 second or slower "lost most of the crucial timing information" needed to see which venue leads. Source: [MSU dissertation, "Essays in high-frequency trading"](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/3657?q=Low+Mary)
- Glassnode (May 2025) described the April-May 2025 rally to about $104k as spot- and ETF-led, with derivatives lagging. Its evidence was daily cumulative volume delta (CVD: running total of taker buy volume minus taker sell volume) and ETF flows. This is a descriptive regime note, not a lead-lag measurement. Source: [Glassnode via Bitget News, "Spot Leads, Derivatives Lag"](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604758612)

### Inferences
- Leadership is measured in milliseconds, so the perp signal cannot directly forecast a minute-15 average from minute 8. What it can do is tell the bot, at minute 8, where BRTI is heading over the next second or so. That matters most in fast moves, when Kalshi's ask may still show 95c after the index has already dropped toward the reference.
- Most useful build: a live BRTI proxy, i.e. a depth-weighted mid across Coinbase, Kraken, Bitstamp, Gemini, LMAX and others, with Binance/Bybit/OKX perp mids as a leading adjustment. From it, compute the opening 60-second average exactly (known by minute 1) and the current gap from that reference in volatility units. Then compare a model probability against the Kalshi ask, and skip when the ask is well above the model.
- Each 15-minute market settles on a 60-second average, so the last minute moves the result less than a single closing print would. For a Brownian path, the variance of a 1-minute average is about one-third of the endpoint variance over that minute. This slightly favors favorites and should go into any model probability.
- Crypto losses clustering across coins fits a shared BTC factor. One BTC perp nowcast/vol signal would flag every crypto favorite in a window at once, which argues for per-window exposure caps, not per-market ones.

### Gaps
- No study found measures Binance/Bybit/OKX perp lead over BRTI (or Coinbase spot) in seconds for 2024-2026. Measuring it ourselves would need tick data from both sides plus BRTI history.
- The current BRTI constituent roster (vs BRRNY's six) was not confirmed. CF Benchmarks' methodology guide was not opened.
- Whether Kalshi quotes actually lag BRTI moves (and by how long) was not found in any source. It can be tested with the bot's own Kalshi snapshots against reconstructed BRTI.
- The Alexander & Heck full text was inaccessible (403). Lag lengths and information shares are not reported here.

## 2. Polymarket 15-minute and hourly crypto up/down markets vs Kalshi

### Takeaway
Polymarket's 15-minute crypto markets settle on Chainlink Data Streams (start vs end price). Its hourly markets settle on the Binance BTC/USDT 1-hour candle. Kalshi settles on CF Benchmarks 60-second averages. So the "same" window can legitimately resolve differently near the reference. No rigorous Polymarket-vs-Kalshi lead-lag study on crypto exists. The best available evidence (a 2026 preprint) finds Polymarket's order-book price already absorbs Binance microstructure: a 43-feature model could not beat it out of sample.

### Cited findings
- Polymarket 15-minute crypto up/down markets use Chainlink Data Streams (low-latency, timestamped prices) with Chainlink Automation for on-chain settlement. Launched around Sept 12, 2025 per Polymarket's post. Sources: [MEXC News](https://www.mexc.com/news/94651); [MEXC News](https://www.mexc.com/news/94912). One later report says short-term markets launched in October 2025. Source: [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/) (dates conflict)
- Rule (mirrored): resolves "Up" if the Chainlink BTC/USD stream price at window end is at or above the price at window start, else "Down". Other exchanges are not used. Source: [Polyguana market mirror](https://polyguana.com/market/2107306) (third-party mirror)
- Chainlink Data Streams crypto reports carry a consensus mid price plus simulated bid/ask at a set liquidity depth, built by node operators from "premium data providers". It is a pull model with sub-second latency claimed by Chainlink. The exact aggregation formula was not found. Sources: [Chainlink Data Streams docs](https://docs.chain.link/data-streams.md); [Synthetix SIP-398](https://sips.synthetix.io/sips/sip-398); [Crypto Top-of-Book Streams](https://docs.chain.link/data-streams/concepts/crypto-top-of-book-and-mark-price.md)
- Polymarket hourly "Bitcoin Up or Down" markets resolve on the Binance BTC/USDT 1H candle: Up if close is at or above open. Only Binance BTC/USDT counts. Sources: [struct.to mirror, July 7 2026 12PM ET market](https://explorer.struct.to/markets/bitcoin-up-or-down-july-7-2026-12pm-et); [Polyguana mirror](https://polyguana.com/market/1668525) (third-party)
- OpenMarket preprint (Feb 12 to May 15, 2026; 727M rows; Polymarket 15-minute BTC binaries vs Binance BTC/USDT): Polymarket quotes react to large Binance moves after a median 347 ms. A walk-forward logistic model with 43 microstructure features did not beat, and slightly underperformed, the probability implied by Polymarket's own order book out of sample. Simulated trading lost -0.116 normalized payoff units per attempted trade after fees and slippage. Top-of-book spreads are typically one tick. Source: [arXiv 2607.26245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26245) (single-author preprint; data/code public)
- Polymarket added dynamic taker fees to 15-minute crypto markets specifically to curb latency arbitrage (fast traders picking off stale quotes using Binance prices). Fees are highest near 50c and fall toward zero at the extremes, and they go to liquidity providers. Reported peak fee: ~1.56% (formula C × 0.25 × (p(1−p))²); other outlets say ~1.80% or ~3.15%. Sources: [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/polymarket-introduces-taker-fees-in-15-minute-markets/); [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/polymarket-introduces-fees-on-15-minute-crypto-bets) (figures conflict)
- One report says Polymarket removed its 500 ms taker delay on Feb 18, 2026. Source: [BlockBeats](https://m.theblockbeats.info/en/news/61326) (unverified secondary source)
- Open-source cross-venue bot: flags Polymarket + Kalshi opposite positions costing under $1 combined. It targets the BTC 1-hour market; "risk-free" is the project's own claim. Because the two venues use different settlement sources, it is not actually risk-free. Source: [GitHub CarlosIbCu/polymarket-kalshi-btc-arbitrage-bot](https://github.com/CarlosIbCu/polymarket-kalshi-btc-arbitrage-bot)
- Kalshi 15-minute bots on GitHub mostly do single-venue YES+NO < $1 arbitrage or market making on KXBTC15M. No audited results. Sources: [simple-kalshi-bot strategies](https://www.mintlify.com/DeweyMarco/simple-kalshi-bot/strategies/overview); [seanrobenalt/kalshi-bot](https://github.com/seanrobenalt/kalshi-bot)
- A personal blog claims a Binance-websocket-fed Polymarket 5-minute bot won 69.6% of 23 trades. The sample is tiny and predates the fee change. Source: [chudi.dev](https://chudi.dev/blog/how-i-built-polymarket-trading-bot) (anecdotal)
- On prediction markets generally: a secondary blog cites an SSRN paper saying Polymarket led price discovery and Kalshi "often lagged by minutes", but that was for the 2024 election, not crypto. Source: [NYC Servers blog](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/prediction-market-arbitrage-guide) (unverified secondhand)
- Oddpool reports 340+ markets with persistent cross-venue gaps above 10c in a month; whether any were crypto was unspecified. Source: [Oddpool market differences](https://www.oddpool.com/market-differences) (vendor)

### Inferences
- A Polymarket 15-minute price on the same window is a useful second opinion, especially where its favorite is much weaker than Kalshi's. But a gap can reflect real settlement differences (Chainlink end price vs Kalshi's 60-second BRTI average vs Binance USDT candle), not just mispricing. That matters most exactly when the price sits near the reference, which is when 90c favorites flip.
- The OpenMarket null result is a caution. If a liquid, bot-heavy prediction market already prices in Binance microstructure, Kalshi's 90c+ asks may already do the same. An edge from external data has to be shown on Kalshi's own history, not assumed.
- Polymarket felt it had to add fees against latency arbitrage. That is indirect evidence that, at least before the fees, short-dated crypto binaries lagged Binance by enough to pick off stale quotes. Whether Kalshi's 15-minute books are similarly stale is untested.

### Gaps
- No study compares Polymarket and Kalshi prices on matching 15-minute crypto windows (lead-lag, divergence rate, or which is better calibrated near 90c+).
- How often Chainlink-, BRTI- and Binance-based outcomes disagree on the same window was not found. It could be measured from Polymarket and Kalshi settlement histories.
- Polymarket's own fee documentation and current 15-minute rules were not read directly (aggregator mirrors only).
- Possible lead (unverified): Polymarket may publish a public real-time crypto price socket carrying the Binance and Chainlink prices it settles on. Check Polymarket's developer docs.

## 3. Order-flow signals (book imbalance, trade flow, liquidations, funding, open interest) at 1-10 minute horizons

### Takeaway
Order-flow imbalance (OFI: net pressure of buy vs sell orders arriving at the best bid and ask) predicts crypto returns over seconds, robustly across assets. But a 2026 study finds 1- and 5-minute imbalances have negligible predictive power at longer horizons, and the one study run directly on 15-minute binaries found no edge over the market price. Funding is too slow (settles every 8 hours). Liquidation and open-interest (OI: total open futures contracts) data mainly mark fragility and cascade risk, not direction, and good intraday liquidation data now needs a paid source. Use these as volatility and "danger" flags, not direction calls.

### Cited findings
- Bieganowski & Ślepaczuk (arXiv 2602.00776, Jan 31, 2026): Binance Futures perp order books and trades at 1-second frequency, Jan 2022 to Oct 2025, for BTC, LTC, ETC, ENJ and ROSE. The same order-book and trade-flow features (tied to OFI, spread and adverse selection) predict short-horizon returns with stable importance across assets. Tested with taker and maker backtests. Accuracy and horizon figures are not in the abstract. Source: [arXiv 2602.00776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00776) (preprint, abstract only)
- A Charles University thesis on Binance futures (BTC, ETH, LTC) finds order-book asymmetry measures have a statistically significant effect on returns, "especially over short time intervals", robust across model specifications. Source: [Charles University repository](https://dspace.cuni.cz/handle/20.500.11956/200516) (thesis; snippet only)
- A July 2026 preprint on quarter-hour effects: quarter-hour opening order imbalance forecasts 4-12 hour returns, "whereas one- and five-minute order imbalances have negligible predictive power" (at those horizons). Source: [Emergent Mind summary of arXiv 2607.09426](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2607.09426) (secondary summary)
- A July 2026 preprint title, "When Does Order Flow Matter? State-Dependent L2 Liquidity-State Transitions in Crypto Futures", suggests order flow's predictive value depends on the liquidity regime. Not read beyond the title. Source: [arXiv 2607.09230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09230)
- Hawkes-process (self-exciting event model) forecasting on limit-order-book data improved return-sign accuracy over benchmarks, on samples of about 2 minutes. Source: [arXiv 2312.16190 (Dec 2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.16190v1)
- On Polymarket 15-minute BTC binaries, 43 microstructure features (including Binance order flow) in a logistic model did not beat the market-implied probability out of sample. Source: [arXiv 2607.26245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26245)
- A 2026 liquidation-cascade preprint (seven crypto-perp cascades, 2022-2025) reports that Binance's per-event liquidation snapshots are no longer served, so intraday liquidation microstructure needs a commercial source. Funding settles every 8 hours, too coarse as an intraday warning. Intraday, only the effects of liquidations (OI changes, aggressor flow) can be seen. Its title says early-warning signals differ from one cascade to the next. Source: [arXiv 2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27070) (preprint)
- Oct 10, 2025 cascade: about $3.21B liquidated "in 60 seconds", BTC maximum intraday drawdown 14.92%, with spreads widening as liquidity vanished. Source: [Amberdata blog](https://blog.amberdata.io/how-3.21b-vanished-in-60-seconds-october-2025-crypto-crash-explained-through-7-charts) (vendor)
- Vendor heuristics: liquidations above 5% of OI tend to feed further forced selling; funding above 15% APR "historically preceded corrections". No methodology shown. Source: [Amberdata, "Leverage & Liquidations"](https://blog.amberdata.io/leverage-liquidations-the-31b-deleveraging) (unverified)
- Exchanges do not publish individual liquidation prices. "Liquidation heatmaps" are estimates built from OI changes and assumed leverage. Sources: [Bitsgap explainer](https://bitsgap.com/blog/liquidation-cascades-explained); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/liquidation-clusters/) (vendor)

### Inferences
- For a 7-minute hold, OFI's predictive half-life (seconds) is too short to call direction. Its best use is folding into the nowcast. For example, heavy one-sided selling on Binance/Bybit perps in the final seconds before minute 8, against a "Yes" favorite, is a reason to skip.
- Liquidation bursts and sharp OI drops on perps are the clearest real-time markers of the jump regime in which 90c+ favorites flip. They make sense as hard filters (skip all crypto favorites in that window), not as a probability model input.
- Expect few true positives. Big flips are rare, so any order-flow filter needs testing on the bot's own losing windows, measuring how many winners each skip gives up.

### Gaps
- No study tests OFI or trade flow specifically for predicting whether a price crosses back over a level within 1-10 minutes (the actual quantity the bot needs).
- Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov's foundational OFI paper (2014) was referenced in search summaries but not fetched. Whether it shows contemporaneous or predictive effects at minute horizons is not confirmed here.
- Whether Binance's real-time liquidation websocket stream still delivers usable intraday data (vs historical snapshots being withdrawn) was not confirmed in docs.

## 4. Volatility regime signals (DVOL, realized-vol jumps, scheduled macro releases, US equity open)

### Takeaway
Scheduled US releases (FOMC, CPI, nonfarm payrolls) cause near-instant crypto moves lasting up to about 30 minutes, at least in 2020-2026 data. That makes "release inside the window" a strong, free filter. Deribit's DVOL (30-day implied volatility) is free over websocket but too slow to time a 7-minute window. Short-window realized volatility on perps is the more relevant input. Evidence on equity-open effects was not found.

### Cited findings
- Kroner, Mohammed & Vega (Federal Reserve Board working paper, Mar 2026): crypto markets show "sharp and near-instantaneous reactions" to FOMC, CPI and payroll releases, lasting up to 30 minutes. Higher-yield or risk-off surprises drive large BTC/ETH declines. Crypto reacts more like US equities than currencies or commodities. Systematic reactions emerged in 2020-2023, and bid-ask spread spikes around releases have shrunk since 2023. Source: [RWA Times summary](https://rwatimes.substack.com/p/how-do-cryptocurrencies-price-economic) (secondary summary; primary paper not accessed)
- Volmex Labs (Sept 2024, industry paper): CPI and Fed rate decisions produce substantial increases in BTC realized volatility and BVIV (Volmex's BTC implied-vol index), with implied vol falling after the release. Source: [Volmex PDF](https://volmex.finance/Macroeconomic%20Announcements%20Fuel%20Volatility%20An%20In-Depth%20Analysis.pdf) (not peer-reviewed)
- Contrary (older data): Benigno & Rosa (NY Fed Staff Report 1052, 2023) use 30-minute windows and find bitcoin "orthogonal to monetary and macroeconomic news". Source: [NY Fed SR1052](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr1052.pdf)
- Jump studies: US news releases influence crypto jumps more than German or Japanese releases (5-minute data, 2016-2019). Another study finds bitcoin realized-variance jumps are not driven by most scheduled US releases. Sources: [IDEAS/RePEc, Rev. Econ. & Finance 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v103y2025ics1059056025006720.html); [Stuttgart PUMA record](https://puma.ub.uni-stuttgart.de/bibtex/6cc4a1f62cbee27a3069d699cc83eb80?lang=en) (results conflict)
- Deribit DVOL ("an expectation of forward volatility analogous to VIX", 30-day, BTC and ETH): live via websocket channel `deribit_volatility_index.{index_name}`, history via REST `public/get_volatility_index_data`. Source: [Deribit docs, options data best practices](https://docs.deribit.com/articles/options-data-collection-best-practices.md)
- Deribit's API docs now also appear on Coinbase's developer site, listing production host `drb.coinbase.com/ws/api/v2` for the DVOL channel. Source: [Coinbase CDP: Deribit volatility index channel](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/coinbase-deribit-app-api/websocket/market-data/deribit_volatility_indexindex_name.md)

### Inferences
- Simplest high-value filter: skip, or require a higher ask, when a top-tier US release (CPI 8:30 ET, NFP 8:30 ET, FOMC statement 14:00 ET, press conference about 14:30 ET) falls between minute 8 and settlement, or in the 30 minutes before. The release calendar is public and known in advance.
- For the 7-minute horizon, realized volatility over the last 1-15 minutes on Binance perps (and its jump vs the trailing hour) is the right volatility input to the distance-to-reference model. DVOL changes too slowly; at most it can scale the baseline or flag high-stress days.
- Crypto now trades like a risk asset (per Kroner et al.), so the US cash equity open (9:30 ET) and close (16:00 ET) are plausible volatility windows. This is speculation; no source found tests it for crypto.

### Gaps
- No source found quantifies crypto volatility at the US equity open/close or at the daily futures/options expiry (08:00 UTC Deribit expiry), at 15-minute granularity.
- The Kroner, Mohammed & Vega paper itself was not accessed; magnitudes and window lengths come from a secondary summary.
- Whether Deribit DVOL access requires an account (vs fully anonymous) was not confirmed in official docs.

## 5. Metals and energy 15-minute markets: settlement references and leading feeds

### Takeaway
Kalshi's 15-minute gold, silver, WTI, natural gas, copper, palladium and platinum markets settle on Pyth Network prices, using single 1-minute candle closes (end vs start), not 60-second averages. Last-second noise therefore matters more than in crypto. The WTI 15-minute series effectively tracks the NYMEX front-month WTI future (with an early roll), and literature finds COMEX gold futures lead spot gold intraday. CME futures are thus the natural leading feed, and cheap for non-professional display use.

### Cited findings
- Kalshi 15-minute commodity series and sources: KXGOLD15M ("Pyth - Gold"), KXSILVER15M ("Pyth - Silver"), KXWTI15M ("Pyth - WTI"), KXCOPPER15M, KXNATGAS15M, KXPALLADIUM15M, KXPLATINUM15M (all Pyth). Mechanism: close of the 1-minute candle at window end vs the candle close at window start. Ties resolve Yes. Candles are labelled by their opening minute, so a 5:00 PM window settles off the 4:59:00-4:59:59 candle. Values round to 2 decimals. If the source publishes nothing, Kalshi uses the most recent value (no void). Gold, silver and WTI 15-minute markets launched Aug 3, 2026. Source: [PredictionMarketsPicks, settlement article (updated Sep 28, 2026)](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/how-kalshi-15-minute-markets-settle) (third-party; confirm against Kalshi series metadata `settlement_sources`)
- WTI warning: the feed Kalshi names (Commodities.Index.PYTHOIL/USD) last published Mar 30, 2026 and is stuck at $103.27. The front-month future feed Commodities.WTIU6/USD matched Kalshi's settled outcomes 99.5% of the time; the spot CFD (USOILSPOT) matched only 49.7%. Source: same as above (unverified third-party measurement)
- WTI roll: 15-minute prints moved from the October to the November contract during the Sept 10, 2026 session, though October expired Sept 22. Kalshi's daily WTI switched Sept 17. Choosing by expiry gives the wrong price for about 12 days. Source: same as above
- Metals feeds: Pyth's Metal.Index.GOLD/USD and SILVER/USD aren't in the public Hermes catalogue. Public Metal.XAU/USD and XAG/USD matched outcomes 99.5% and 99.0% of the time, which doesn't prove they are the same series. The public Pyth endpoints the author used have reportedly stopped serving free reads. Source: same as above (unverified)
- Currency 15-minute series (GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/JPY) read Pyth's published rate at each time, not a candle. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 15-minute series name only a placeholder source ("For example, Google Finance"). Source: same as above
- Separately, a third-party site reports gold/silver 15-minute markets resolve on Pyth feeds. Sources: [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/kalshi-15-minute-gold-silver-prediction-markets/); [Kalshi 15-min commodities category](https://kalshi.com/category/commodities/frequency/fifteen_min)
- Gold price discovery: using 1997-2014 intraday data, New York (COMEX) futures lead in absorbing new information even though London spot volume is about 10x larger. Shares vary by time of day and year. Source: [UTS repository, *J. Futures Markets*](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/41414/4/GoldILS%20JFutMkt%20Forthcoming.pdf) (older data). A 2021 JFM paper studies spot vs futures vs ETF intraday leadership. Source: [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v41y2021i7p1092-1123.html) (abstract not read)
- Contrary: a Kobe University dissertation finds LBMA (London spot) dominates price discovery among COMEX, LBMA and TOCOM, though COMEX dominates in its US-Japan analysis. Source: [Kobe University](https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/D1007097/D1007097.pdf)
- High-frequency: gold futures lead, with a physical gold ETF close behind. Information spreads in under 100 ms. Source: [MSU dissertation](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/3657?q=Low+Mary)
- Fees: Kalshi taker fee is 0.07 × P × (1−P) per contract. Source: [PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/how-kalshi-15-minute-markets-settle)

### Inferences
- Metals and energy settle on one candle close, with no averaging. A favorite near the reference has more last-minute flip risk than a crypto favorite at the same price. If the bot trades these, it likely needs a higher price floor, or a minimum distance from the reference in volatility units.
- Leading feeds: COMEX GC/SI (gold/silver) and NYMEX CL/NG (WTI/natural gas) front-month futures, watching the contract Pyth actually uses (roll early). Pyth's gold/silver spot publishers likely quote off futures, so the futures-leads-spot literature should carry over at sub-second scale. As in crypto, that buys a nowcast, not a 7-minute forecast.
- Natural gas is volatile around the weekly EIA storage report (Thursdays 10:30 ET), and WTI around the EIA petroleum status report (Wednesdays 10:30 ET). These are known-in-advance filter times, analogous to CPI for crypto. They come from general market knowledge and were not verified in this research.

### Gaps
- Kalshi's own rule text and `settlement_sources` metadata for the commodity 15-minute series were not read directly. Every Pyth detail comes from one third-party site.
- No study found measures Pyth spot gold/silver against COMEX futures lead-lag.
- Pyth's current access terms (free vs paid Hermes/Lazer endpoints) are unconfirmed.

## 6. Practical access: APIs, websockets, latency, rate limits, costs

### Takeaway
Crypto exchange market data is free and real-time: Binance spot/futures websockets, Coinbase's unauthenticated feed, Deribit public channels. A single Python process can subscribe to everything the bot needs within published limits. CME real-time data is cheap for non-professional display ($1.55/month per exchange top-of-book). Automated/non-display use may be priced differently. CF Benchmarks BRTI itself and Chainlink Data Streams are not confirmed free, so reconstructing the index from constituent exchanges is the practical route.

### Cited findings
- Binance spot websocket: endpoints `wss://stream.binance.com:9443` or `:443`. A connection is valid 24 h. The server pings every 20 s and drops the connection if no pong arrives within 1 minute. The limit is 5 incoming messages/s (including pings, pongs and subscribe messages). Max 1,024 streams per connection, 300 connection attempts per 5 minutes per IP. Timestamps in ms by default, µs with `timeUnit=MICROSECOND`. `@trade`, `@aggTrade` and `@bookTicker` stream in real time; `@depth` updates every 1000 ms or 100 ms. Sources: [Binance spot WebSocket streams docs (GitHub)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/binance/binance-spot-api-docs/master/web-socket-streams.md); [Binance developer docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/web-socket-streams)
- Binance USD-M futures websocket: base `wss://fstream.binance.com`. `@bookTicker` is real-time; diff/partial depth at 100/250/500 ms. Source: [Binance USD-M futures market streams](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/websocket-market-streams)
- Coinbase Exchange: unauthenticated market-data feed at `wss://ws-feed.exchange.coinbase.com`; you must subscribe within 5 s of connecting. Limits: 8 requests/s per IP (bursts to 20), 100 client messages/s per IP, and 10 subscriptions per product per channel. Sources: [Coinbase Exchange websocket overview](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/websocket-feed/overview.md); [rate limits](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/websocket-feed/rate-limits). Conflict: Tardis reported Coinbase began requiring authentication for full, level2 and level3 channels on July 1, 2023. Source: [Tardis status](https://status.tardis.dev/incidents/hg52tbhcg8p2)
- Deribit: DVOL live via `deribit_volatility_index.{index_name}` and history via `public/get_volatility_index_data`. Source: [Deribit docs](https://docs.deribit.com/articles/options-data-collection-best-practices.md). Historical Deribit tick data is sold by Tardis. Source: [Tardis Deribit docs](https://docs.tardis.dev/historical-data-details/deribit.md)
- CME Group fee list (effective June 1, 2026): non-professional top of book $1.55/device/month per exchange ($4.65 for the all-exchange bundle). Non-professional depth $12.10/month ($36.50 bundle). Rising to $1.60 and $12.50 in Jan 2027. "Non-professional" means a natural person not registered with a regulator and not managing assets for a business. Sources: [CME June 2026 fee list](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/files/june-2026-market-data-fee-list.pdf); [CME January 2027 fee list](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/files/january-2027-market-data-fee-list.pdf)
- Chainlink Data Streams: a pull-based service; reports are fetched and verified on-chain. Sub-second latency is Chainlink's own claim. Source: [Chainlink Data Streams architecture](https://docs.chain.link/data-streams/architecture)
- Pyth: per one third-party report, the public endpoints used to verify Kalshi metals settlement have stopped serving free reads. Source: [PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/how-kalshi-15-minute-markets-settle) (unverified)
- Historical cross-venue research data: the OpenMarket dataset (synchronized Polymarket 15-minute BTC + Binance BTC/USDT, Feb-May 2026) has code and data on GitHub and Hugging Face. Source: [arXiv 2607.26245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26245)

### Inferences
- A minimal stack for the bot, all free: Binance USD-M `bookTicker` + `aggTrade` for BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP/DOGE/BNB perps; Coinbase, Kraken, Bitstamp and Gemini spot tickers (the BRTI proxy); Deribit DVOL; and a static macro-release calendar. That is well within one connection per venue. Latency from a home connection (tens to hundreds of ms) is fine for a once-per-window decision at minute 8.
- Metals/energy: getting CME data into an automated program likely triggers CME's "non-display" licensing rather than the $1.55 display fee (assumption; check the fee list's non-display section). Lower-cost alternatives would be a retail futures broker API or Pyth itself, if still accessible.
- Before any live use, backtest each signal on the bot's existing Kalshi snapshot history. That means buying or collecting historical Binance/Coinbase ticks (e.g. Tardis, or free Binance public data dumps). Validation comes first: Polymarket's market price already beat a 43-feature model.

### Gaps
- CF Benchmarks BRTI real-time licensing cost and any free delayed display were not researched.
- Bybit, OKX and Kraken websocket limits were not fetched; they are assumed similar to Binance but not confirmed.
- CME non-display (automated-use) fees for COMEX/NYMEX were not extracted from the fee list.
- Kalshi's own API rate limits and websocket latency were out of scope and not checked.
