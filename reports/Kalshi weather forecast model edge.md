# Read the station, not the sky

*Research synthesis, 26 Oct 2026. Desk research only. Nothing was built or traded, and the live bot was not touched.*

**No. A weather forecast model we build from online data will not beat Kalshi's prices by enough to make money.** The market already misses the true daily high by about 10% less than the best free forecast. That forecast is the National Blend of Models (NBM), the Weather Service's automatic mix of many weather models. A careful blend of six free forecasts only ties the market. Since May 2026 the NBM itself has used two AI weather models as inputs. So AI's head start is already in the price. The best realistic station model (statistical fixes tuned to one airport) lands at the market's level, not past it. What is left is narrow, and it is not forecasting. Late in the day, the official high can sit a degree above the hourly readings. Free data reveals it before trading stops. Posting resting orders (orders that wait to be filled) is cheaper than paying the spread. The spread is the gap between the buy and sell price, what it costs to get in. But the one public small weather maker (a trader who posts resting orders) lost more to faster traders than it earned in rewards. **My advice: build no forecast model. Run two cheap desk checks on data we already hold. Then, after 27 Nov, run a log-only maker test: record what resting orders would have earned, without placing any.**

## Two earlier studies already closed the easy doors

Each claim in this report carries its evidence type. **Hard evidence** means a peer-reviewed paper, a large data study, an official rule, a live data read or our own pre-registered test. A pre-registered test is one whose rule was written down and committed before any data was scored. **Self-published** means one person's backtest or trading log, with the method shown but not checked by anyone else. **Vendor claim** means the source sells a product. **Anecdote** means a news story, screenshot or post about one trader.

Our 25 Oct study found that every input a weather bot needs is free. The settlement rules can be read down to the station and the minute. But every honest 2026 test of a forecast-driven bot lost money. So that study advised testing our favourite rule instead ([25 Oct study](<Kalshi weather bot feasibility.md>)). The Oct 26 test did that on 44,262 settled Kalshi markets. Its rule was committed before any data was scored. **Daily highs failed clearly.** Heavy favourites (the likely side) lost 48 times in 1,113 bets, where the prices predicted 28.7 losses. That is a loss of about 2.5 cents for every dollar bet (−24.5c per $10 bet). The 95% range (the range the true number is probably in) ran from −40.1c to −8.9c per $10 bet, all below zero. Rain had only 110 bets, too few to judge ([Oct 26 test](<Weather favourites test Oct 26.md>)). One lead came out after the verdict. It covers the 33 city-days where a favourite lost at 6 PM local standard time (the clock with no summer shift). On 32 of them, the official high came in above the bracket (the 2°F range) the market favoured. This report asks whether a model of our own can find money where the favourite rule could not.

## AI weather models are already inside the free forecast

The AI models that matter in Oct 2026 are ensembles. An ensemble is many runs of one model from slightly different starts, and the spread of the runs gives probabilities. ECMWF, the European weather centre, has run its AIFS model as a 51-run ensemble since July 2025 (hard evidence: [ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/29407)). It is free, and its licence allows commercial use ([ECMWF AIFS data page](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/aifs-machine-learning-data)). NOAA made three AI systems operational on 17 Dec 2025, including a 31-run AI ensemble ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2025/12/17/noaa-deploys-new-generation-of-ai-driven-global-weather-models/)). Google released WeatherNext 3 in Sept 2026. It forecasts every hour on a 5 km grid and is "trained to target specific weather data stations" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3160336)). The first wave of one-answer AI models, such as GraphCast and Pangu, are now research baselines. ECMWF stopped running them in May 2026 ([ECMWF AIFS data page](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/aifs-machine-learning-data)).

On world-wide grid scores, AI ensembles now beat the best physics ensemble (traditional, equation-based models). One Oct 2026 preprint (a paper not yet peer reviewed) scored land temperatures three days out. WeatherNext 2's error score was 25% smaller than ECMWF's raw physics ensemble ([Wessel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20519)). But the AI lead is clearest at day 3 and beyond. Kalshi trades the next day and the same day. **We found no published test of any AI model against the NBM at US airport stations for tomorrow's high.** The known weak spots all hurt station work. The open feeds use grid boxes about 28 km wide, while Kalshi settles on one airport. Most step every 6 hours, which can miss the afternoon peak. At Kalshi stations, the raw European physics model ran 1–5°F cold until corrected ([Crosier](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23969v1)). AIFS also forecasts heavy rain less often than it happens ([AIFS update paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18994v1)). One-answer AI models underplay record heat and cold, because they forecast a smoothed average ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15724v1); [Wessel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20519)).

**The deciding fact is that the free blend already contains AI.** NBM version 5.0 went live on 5 May 2026. Its release note lists "the ECAIGS and AIGFS artificial intelligence forecasting systems as inputs" (hard evidence: [NOAA MDL](https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/mdl/-/national-blend-of-models-nbm-upgraded-to-version-5.0-1)). AIGFS is NOAA's AI model. ECAIGS is very likely ECMWF's AIFS, but no source confirms the name. Traders read the NBM. WeatherNext 3 also powers Google Search and Google Maps ([Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/15/google-launches-weathernext-3-for-sharper-weather-forecasts)). So a free AI forecast is no longer a private signal. The market's 10% lead over the NBM was measured mostly before this. Only about 3 months of Crosier's data overlap NBM 5.0. Today's NBM may sit closer to the market than his numbers show.

Getting the AI forecasts is easy, but they come late or with strings. WeatherNext 3 reaches Google's cloud about 7 hours 45 minutes after each main run (hard evidence: [Google schedule](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/dissemination)). That is far behind the hourly NBM and LAMP on the trading day. LAMP is the Weather Service's hourly station forecast that uses live readings. WeatherNext 3's real-time data also falls under "experimental" terms that may not allow trading ([Google access guide](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/access-forecast)). Running AIFS ourselves needs one large graphics card. The first AIFS ensemble needed about 38 GB of graphics memory ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ecmwf/aifs-ens-1.0)). But our run would start from the same ECMWF data, so it would mostly copy the free forecast. A backtest would be short and mixed. AIFS ensemble history covers about 15 months across two versions. WeatherNext 3 has about 2 months.

Nobody has shown AI weather models making money on prediction markets. Energy-trading firms sell AI weather products, but every accuracy claim we found is their own (vendor claim: [Pelmorex](https://www.pelmorex.com/en/media-hub/pelmorex-corp-unveils-ai-powered-advanced-weather-modeling-built-for-energy-traders/)). The market studies point the other way. When the NBM updates, it moves toward the market about four times as far as the market moves toward it (hard evidence: [Crosier](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23969v1)). The crowd already knows what the next forecast will say. A second study compared ForecastEx's daily-high markets with twenty forecasts, including an ECMWF AI system. The market's misses were 21–25% smaller than the next best. That study was published by the broker that runs ForecastEx, and we saw it only in search snippets ([Interactive Brokers](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/ibkr-climate-energy/prediction-markets-might-already-be-the-best-source-for-todays-weather-forecast/)).

## The best realistic station model only ties the market

Station-level machine learning is mature, and its gains are well measured. Post-processing means a statistical fix that turns raw model output into a forecast for one station. Against a raw model, it cuts the error score by 15% to 33%. Against a forecast that is already corrected, fancier methods add only 3% to 13% (hard evidence: 537 German stations, [Rasp & Lerch](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.09091)). The NBM is already corrected and blended. So only the small number applies.

**The cheapest real gain is a plain blend, and it stops at the market.** Crosier blended six free forecasts for 7 Kalshi cities, refitting each day on past days only (hard evidence: 7,590 city-days, [Crosier](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23969v1)). The table shows each one's RMSE (the typical size of a miss, with big misses counted extra). Lower is better.

| Forecast | Typical miss, day before at 11 AM Eastern (RMSE) | Typical miss, evening before (RMSE) | Typical miss, morning of the day (RMSE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kalshi market prices | 2.44°F | 2.21°F | 1.88°F |
| Blend of six free forecasts, built on past days only | 2.45°F | 2.25°F | 1.90°F |
| NBM alone, the best single free forecast | 2.70°F | 2.45°F | 2.12°F |
| European physics model, average error removed | 3.31°F | 3.16°F | 2.68°F |

Source: [Crosier](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23969v1).

A plain average of the six did as well as the fitted blend: a typical miss of 2.43°F against 2.45°F. On the days both existed, the market's typical miss was 2.38°F, about 2% smaller than the plain average. The market's lead over the NBM is biggest where weather is hardest. From November to June, the market's typical miss was 10–16% smaller than the NBM's. From July to October it was only 1–4% smaller. In Miami the NBM's typical miss was only 1.6°F, and the market did no better. At LAX, on the coast, the market beat the NBM by 10–17% in every season. Local knowledge, like the marine layer (low sea cloud that holds highs down), counts most there.

With only a handful of Kalshi stations, the realistic best model is simple. It is a per-station linear blend, or EMOS (a fit that sets a forecast's middle and width from its recent misses). Its inputs would be the NBM, LAMP, HRRR (a fast-updating 3 km US model) and the European model. Fancier neural networks only pay off when pooling hundreds of stations. Our estimate from these studies is that such a model lands 8–12% below the NBM's typical miss. That is market level, not better. Version changes make even that hard. The NBM changed on 5 May 2026 and again on 28 Jul 2026 ([NWS notice](https://www.weather.gov/media/notification/pdf_2026/scn26-70_nbmv5.0.14.pdf)). The current NBM has under 3 months of history.

Our research notes ran a best-case simulation. It assumes our model knows everything the market knows, plus something extra. It also assumes perfect probabilities and unlimited fills at the mid-price (the midpoint between the buy and sell prices) plus half a cent ([our notes](<../research_notes/Kalshi weather forecast model edge/station_ml_postprocessing.md>)).

| How much smaller our typical miss is than the market's | Our typical miss (RMSE) vs the market's 2.44°F | Edge (profit per contract, in cents) after costs, best case | Cents of profit for every dollar bet, best case |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8% smaller | 2.42°F | about 1.1c | about 2.1c |
| 2.9% smaller | 2.37°F | about 1.9c | about 3.6c |
| 5.7% smaller | 2.30°F | about 2.8c | about 5.2c |

**This is a ceiling, not a forecast.** A public-data model does not know what the market knows. Its misses look a lot like the market's misses. If the two are 90% alike (a correlation of 0.9), combining them beats the market by only about 2.4%. If the market already holds all public information, the gain is about zero. The trading record fits that view. One Kalshi bot ran on GFS, the free US weather model. It won 86% of trades in a 678-day backtest, but only about 60% live. It was shut down below breakeven (self-published, reported second-hand: [Weather Station Advisor](https://weatherstationadvisor.com/home-weather-station-prediction-market/)). Three other 2026 backtests lost after fees (self-published: [MiguelTheGamer](https://github.com/MiguelTheGamer/kalshi-weather-backtest); [DEV post](https://dev.to/myfirstcodeo/i-backtested-a-forecast-based-model-against-kalshis-temperature-markets-the-market-won-heres-442c); [jattree](https://github.com/jattree/weather-edge)). That pattern is adverse selection: getting picked off by someone who knows more than you.

**One test decides whether any of this matters.** It is called an encompassing test: a check of whether our forecast adds anything once you already know the market price. On data neither was built on, check how much weight each forecast deserves in predicting the actual high. If ours gets a weight near zero, there is nothing to trade, however good it looks against the NBM. No published study has run this test on Kalshi.

## The evening gap is about reading the rules, not the weather

Live readings are the one input that clearly helps on the day itself. LAMP drove most of the free blend's gains through the day. Without LAMP, the market's lead over the blend on the target morning grew from 3.0% to 6.4% (hard evidence: [Crosier](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23969v1)). But the market reads live data too. A self-published model with live airport readings still lost to the market at 11 AM, 1 PM, 3 PM and 5 PM. By 5 PM, New York and Chicago prices had nearly locked in the right answer (self-published: [MiguelTheGamer](https://github.com/MiguelTheGamer/kalshi-weather-backtest)). The answer also firms up fast. By 2 PM standard time, the final high ended within 1°F of the high so far on 72% of New York days. By 4 PM it was 87% ([25 Oct study](<Kalshi weather bot feasibility.md>)). So the same-day question is narrow: does the market ever misread the station near the end of the day?

The official daily high is not the highest hourly reading. ASOS, the automated airport weather station, samples temperature every few seconds. Once a minute it updates a short running average, kept in whole °F. The day's high is the highest of those averages, midnight to midnight standard time (hard evidence: [ASOS User's Guide](https://www.weather.gov/media/asos/aum-toc.pdf); [NWS Chicago](https://www.weather.gov/lot/weather_observations_faq); [NWS Phoenix](https://www.weather.gov/psr/HiResASOS)). The hourly report, called a METAR, shows only one minute per hour, at about :51. A peak between reports never shows in any of them. Our own 2024–2025 check found the official high averaged 0.7–0.8°F above the highest hourly reading. It was at least 1°F higher on 57% of New York days, 68% in Miami and 64% at LAX. Adding the 6-hour maximum groups closed the gap. These are extra codes in four reports a day that give the highest temperature of the past 6 hours. With them, we matched the official high on 94–99% of days ([25 Oct study](<Kalshi weather bot feasibility.md>)).

**Sources disagree on the averaging window.** The ASOS User's Guide and three Weather Service offices say a 5-minute running average. The Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM, a free university weather archive) and one data vendor say 2 minutes ([IEM](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/onsite/news.phtml?id=1469); [minuteTemp](https://minutetemp.com/docs/market-settlement)). We could not settle it. Either way, the high is a short average checked every minute, so the practical point stands.

The exact high becomes public in pieces, at known times. The table shows New York, in standard time; the summer clock reads one hour later. A DSM is the station's daily summary message, the raw machine summary behind the official report. The CLI is the Weather Service's daily climate report, which holds the official high.

| What comes out | Time (UTC, world standard time) | New York standard time | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hourly report (METAR) | :51 each hour | :51 each hour | The exact whole-°F average for that one minute only |
| Midday 6-hour group | about 17:51 | about 12:51 PM | The exact high from about 6:51 AM to 12:51 PM |
| First afternoon summary (DSM) | 20:13–20:21 | about 3:15 PM | The exact high so far, to 3 PM, and its minute |
| Early climate report (preliminary CLI) | 20:32–20:33 | about 3:33 PM | The same high so far, to 3 PM |
| Second afternoon summary (DSM) | 21:14–21:21 | about 4:15 PM | The exact high so far, to 4 PM |
| Evening 6-hour group | about 23:51 | about 6:51 PM | The exact high from about 12:51 to 6:51 PM |
| 24-hour group | about 04:51 | about 11:51 PM | The exact high of the past 24 hours, 9 minutes before trading stops |
| Kalshi stops trading | 05:00 | midnight | Nothing new |
| Final climate report (CLI) | 06:21–06:46 | about 1:30 AM | The settlement number |

Times are from late October, during summer time. The preliminary CLI follows the wall clock, so it moves an hour after clocks change on 1 Nov. Sources: [NYC summary](https://api.weather.gov/products/188a1349-42cd-4150-bf7c-2467ead0ab39); [NYC CLI times](https://api.weather.gov/products/types/CLI/locations/NYC); [Wethr KNYC guide](https://wethr.net/edu/market/newyork); [ASOS User's Guide](https://www.weather.gov/media/asos/aum-toc.pdf); [Kalshi rules](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXHIGHNY&limit=2).

Fast traders already work these releases. A community guide names bots that react to the summaries, the 6-hour groups and the climate report. It warns against leaving resting orders near release times, but gives no timings (self-published: [Wethr](https://wethr.net/edu/market-bots)). A public New York maker project measured when prices move. Most moves cluster in the minutes after the hourly report and summary messages. That window held 33% of all price movement from October to March, and 51% from April to September (self-published: [dfong03](https://github.com/dfong03/apma2026-kalshi-weather)). Racing a release means racing those bots.

**Our 32-of-33 lead is weaker than it looks.** By 6 PM, the high so far is a floor, because the day's high can only rise. A favourite at 6 PM is usually a bet that the high is done, like "70°F or below" at 97c. Such a bet can only lose upward. So nearly all late losses must land above the favoured bracket, whatever the cause. The useful question is different: was the extra degree already measured, and public, at 6 PM? If yes, prices were stale and a careful reader could have caught it. If it was measured but not yet released, the market faced a real gap between releases. If the air warmed late in the evening, it was a forecasting miss, not a rules quirk.

Time zones make Eastern cities the likeliest place for a gap. At 6 PM standard time in New York, the evening 6-hour group is still 51 minutes away. Central cities get it 9 minutes before their 6 PM. Mountain and Pacific cities get it earlier, but it covers only to about 4:51 PM or 3:51 PM local time. New York does have its 4 PM summary. But the Weather Service's public summary feed lists New York, not Midway, Miami, Austin, Denver or LAX ([DSM location list](https://api.weather.gov/products/types/DSM/locations)). IEM holds Midway's ([IEM archive](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=DSMMDW&limit=4&fmt=text)). The others probably exist under other names, but nobody has confirmed it.

The prize is small, even if real. In our test, about 19 extra losses came in 1,113 bets, or 1.7 extra losses per 100 bets. Buying the cheap side of every favourite would collect about 1.7 cents per contract before costs (our arithmetic). But that buyer pays the far side of a spread of up to 3c, plus the fee. So a blind flip would likely make nothing. Only a filter that finds the right days could pay.

One live feed sees inside the gap, and it is weak. The 1-minute airport feed reports whole °C only and arrives 2–5 minutes late. It was down from Oct 2023 to Jan 2026 (hard evidence: [Synoptic](https://docs.synopticdata.com/services/high-frequency-asos)). Synoptic's free tier is for non-commercial use only ([Synoptic pricing](https://synopticdata.com/pricing/)). NOAA's own data hub, MADIS, offers the same feed to the public ([MADIS](https://madis.ncep.noaa.gov/madis_OMO.shtml)). Whole °C can split some bracket edges but not others. 70°F and 71°F fall in different °C steps, so a 22°C reading proves the high reached 71°F. But 87°F and 88°F share one °C step, so no whole-°C feed can tell them apart (our calculation, in [our notes](<../research_notes/Kalshi weather forecast model edge/intraday_nowcasting.md>)).

The free archives make a fair test possible. IEM keeps raw METAR text, including the 6-hour and 24-hour groups, and the summary messages with their issue times ([IEM ASOS archive](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/asos.py)). So we can rebuild, for any past minute, the best public estimate of the official high. Then we can compare it with the market price at that hour.

## Radar cannot rescue the rain market

Daily rain (KXRAIN) pays Yes if the day's official rain is above zero. A trace, too little to measure, counts as No. The market "will close and expire early if the event occurs" (hard evidence: [Kalshi rules](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXRAIN&limit=3)). So once rain is recorded, the market is gone. Late in the day, the only live bet is No, the "stays dry" side. A No bet at 95c needs a better than 95% chance of no measurable rain before standard midnight.

Radar answers the next hour or two, not the evening. MRMS is NOAA's national radar map. It updates its rain rate every 2 minutes, about 90 seconds late (seen only in search summaries: [NSSL tables](https://nssl.noaa.gov/projects/mrms/operational/tables.php); [Martinaitis](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/Martinaitis-WERA21.pdf)). Simple radar extrapolation (moving today's rain areas forward) stayed useful for about 25 to 116 minutes, depending on the storm ([Imhoff et al.](https://presentations.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-13376_presentation.pdf)). At 6 PM standard time, six hours remain, which is past radar's range. That stretch needs LAMP's hourly rain chance, HRRR or an AI model ([LAMP elements](https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/mdl/lamp-elements)). Google says its MetNet-3 beats HRRR out to 24 hours (Google's own paper: [MetNet-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06079)). **We found no published score for "will this one airport record 0.01 inch before midnight" from any of these tools.**

The market leaves little room. On 26 Oct, 70 rain markets were open, with 400,000 contracts traded in a day. Only 3 of the 70 had a bid between 15c and 85c (hard evidence, live read: [Kalshi API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXRAIN&status=open)). In plain words, most rain trading happens in markets that are nearly decided. Kalshi also pays one designated firm to quote daily rain through 31 Dec 2026 ([Kalshi LP page](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15410219-liquidity-provider-program)). A small bot would compete with a paid professional.

The one rain edge with numbers did not last. A self-published AI-agent account found No was too cheap late in the day across 1,598 rain markets. It looked worth +5 to +9 cents per contract after fees. Then the weekly edge fell from about +11c in July and August to about +1c in late October. It shrank as volume grew (self-published: [lip-ledger case study](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mirabilis-agent/lip-ledger/main/docs/CASE_STUDY.md)). Our own rain test had only 110 bets on 25 dates ([Oct 26 test](<Weather favourites test Oct 26.md>)). The series started on 15 Jul 2026, so our data holds only 2,390 rain city-days on 97 dates.

One speed idea remains. The rules do not say which data feed triggers the early close. A bot reading radar or minute-level rain might see rain minutes before Kalshi closes the market, and buy Yes. That is a pure race against the designated firm and other bots. We do not recommend building for it.

## Thin markets look soft, but their edges die in weeks

**The more attention a weather market gets, the closer its price sits to the best forecast.** Big-city highs draw the most traders, the tightest spreads and the clearest proof that models lose. Tomorrow's New York brackets traded at a 1c spread on 26 Oct (live read: [Kalshi API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?event_ticker=KXHIGHNY-26OCT27&limit=20)). The table ranks the rest.

| Market | How busy | Spread (gap between buy and sell price) | Who else is there | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Big-city daily highs | 110,000–150,000 contracts a day in Miami, NYC and LA; 3,000–55,000 elsewhere | About 1c | Many skilled traders; prices beat the best free forecast by about 10% | Most efficient. Skip for forecasting |
| Daily lows | 1,000–18,000 contracts a day | 6–18c | Same reward as highs; no paid firm listed | Least watched market that still trades. Maker test only |
| Daily rain | 400,000–480,000 contracts a day, about 35 cities | 2c on the busiest (Tampa, 13c/15c) | One paid firm; most trading is in nearly decided markets | Skip |
| Hourly temperature index | About 20,000 a day in Miami; 1,000–2,000 elsewhere | 21c on one live Miami bracket | One paid firm; the answer is published live | A speed race. Skip |
| Monthly rain and snow | About $18,000 in total for NYC in October | 2c on the one Chicago bracket checked | Fast news traders in storm weeks | Too thin |
| Polymarket city highs abroad | $43,000–$91,000 per city-day | 1–7c near the likely bracket | A global crowd; US users are barred | Off limits |

Sources: [KXLOWTNYC](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXLOWTNYC&status=open); [KXTEMPMIAH](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXTEMPMIAH&status=open); [Kalshi monthly NYC rain](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxrainnycm/monthly-rain-in-new-york/kxrainnycm-26oct); [Polymarket Gamma API](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events?slug=highest-temperature-in-london-on-october-27-2026); [Kalshi LP page](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15410219-liquidity-provider-program); our 25 Oct scan of all weather series.

Daily lows are the clearest attention gap on Kalshi. They settle on the same report as highs and use the same forecasts. They pay resting orders the same reward as highs (live read: [incentive list](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/incentive_programs?status=active&limit=1000)). That is $20 per bracket across 6 brackets, so $120 per city for each day's event. Yet they trade 5 to 100 times less. Wide spreads can also mean makers got burned there before. Nobody has tested their calibration (whether a 70c price really wins 70% of the time). Lows carry their own end-of-day trap. A cold front can set the low in the last hour before standard midnight. Only the 24-hour group, about 9 minutes before trading stops, shows it in advance ([ASOS User's Guide](https://www.weather.gov/media/asos/aum-toc.pdf); [NWS Chicago](https://www.weather.gov/lot/weather_observations_faq)).

The hourly index markets show what happens when the answer is public. They settle on a Kalshi average of 1-minute airport readings, which Kalshi publishes about 2.5 minutes late. One self-published replay of passive selling (resting sell orders) showed +5 to +14 cents per contract on its first 99 fills. By 139 fills it showed −0.1c. Its first live day lost 1.8c per contract (self-published: [lip-ledger index notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mirabilis-agent/lip-ledger/main/docs/WEATHER_INDEX.md)). The author concluded the edge was timing and queue position (your place in line at a price), not prediction. Our own Oct 18 hourly temperature test also failed ([Oct 18 backtest](<Other markets backtest Oct 18.md>)).

Polymarket's foreign city highs look softer, but we cannot use them. Makers there pay nothing and get 25% of taker fees back ([Polymarket fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)). Even so, a careful 24-city model lost to the market (self-published: [jattree](https://github.com/jattree/weather-edge)). International Polymarket has blocked US users since 2022 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/linked/131383/following-cftc-settlement-prediction-platform-polymarket-geoblocks-trades-in-the-us)). One odds site hints that Polymarket US listed an NYC high in August, but we could not confirm it ([prediction.com](https://prediction.com/odds/highest-temperature-in-nyc-on-aug-7-2026/30751)).

Weather prices show only a mild favourite-longshot bias (cheap unlikely bets overpriced, heavy favourites underpriced). A study of Kalshi data to April 2025 found weather longshots lose less than longshots elsewhere (hard evidence: [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf)). Its break-even, the price above which bets made money on average before fees, sat near 32c. A public New York study put that crossing at 27c (self-published: [dfong03](https://github.com/dfong03/apma2026-kalshi-weather)). Our Oct 26 test found the opposite for late heavy favourites on highs: they lost more often than priced.

## Making beats taking on cost, but pick-offs decide the result

On cost alone, resting orders clearly beat paying the spread. A maker posts an order and waits for someone to trade with it. A taker trades against an order already there. All six weather series we checked charge makers nothing (hard evidence, live read: [Kalshi series](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series/KXHIGHNY)). Only takers pay the taker fee (Kalshi's charge for trading, biggest near 50c). The table shows what a taker pays on a 1c spread.

| Contract price | Taker fee per contract | Taker's total cost, fee plus half of a 1c spread (measured from the mid-price) | Maker's fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10c | 0.63c | about 1.1c | 0c |
| 32c (a typical top bracket a day ahead) | 1.52c | about 2.0c | 0c |
| 50c | 1.75c | about 2.25c | 0c |
| 97c | 0.20c | about 0.7c | 0c |

At 50c, the taker fee alone is bigger than the whole 1c spread. The fee is 7% × price × (1 − price) per contract ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)).

Kalshi also pays people to rest orders. Its Liquidity Incentive Program (LIP) samples the order book once a second and splits a fixed reward among qualifying orders. Most US members can join, and it runs to 1 Jan 2027 unless Kalshi changes it (hard evidence: [LIP help page](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)). On 26 Oct, each daily high and low bracket paid $20 over its event's life of about 40 hours ([incentive list](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/incentive_programs?status=active&limit=1000)). That is about 50 cents an hour per bracket, shared among everyone who qualifies. An order one cent behind the qualifying price earns half credit, and two cents behind earns a quarter.

Kalshi's own trade data shows weather makers earned about 1.3 cents for every dollar traded, before fees. Takers lost the same (hard evidence: 4.4 million weather trades, June 2021 to Nov 2025, [Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). A reported code issue may drop some weather markets from that count ([GitHub issue #74](https://github.com/Jon-Becker/prediction-market-analysis/issues/74)). It is also an average over all makers, mostly professionals.

**The only first-hand log from a small weather maker is a warning.** An AI-agent account quoted weather markets with $20 for 8 days in Oct 2026. It earned $20.30 in rewards and lost $36.06 in trading. Its account fell to about $0.10 (self-published: [lip-ledger case study](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mirabilis-agent/lip-ledger/main/docs/CASE_STUDY.md)). Blocks of 200–600 contracts swept its resting bids within seconds. Those were pick-offs: faster, better-informed traders filling its orders just before the price moved against it. Its lesson: a reward model "is not an edge until adverse-selection cost is measured on your own orders." The New York maker project defends itself by design. It pulls quotes around station reports. It never bids in brackets the readings have ruled out. Its orders expire after 3 minutes, and fills are capped at 25 contracts per 15 seconds. It publishes no profit figures (self-published: [dfong03](https://github.com/dfong03/apma2026-kalshi-weather)).

**Does a forecast help a maker? A little, but not as a forecast.** The market's own price beats anything we could build, so quotes should centre on it. The model's real job is defence. It must know when not to quote. That means around the hourly report at about :51, the 6-hour groups, the summaries and new model runs. It must also pull bids from brackets the readings have already ruled out. That is the same station knowledge as the evening gap. Kalshi's tools support this. Post-only orders never pay the taker fee. Orders can expire on their own. Order groups cancel everything after a burst of fills (hard evidence: [Kalshi order groups](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/order_groups)).

The money is small. Kalshi says weather traded $564 million through July 2026, about $80 million a month (company figure, via [Benzinga](https://www.webull.ca/news-detail/15475678967514112)). All of Kalshi traded $53 billion in September ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-09-30-kalshi-ends-trader-incentive-program-417247)). So weather is about 0.2% of Kalshi. Tomorrow's New York bracket had only 25 contracts at the best bid, about $10 (live read: [order book](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets/KXHIGHNY-26OCT27-B60.5/orderbook?depth=8)). The top 8 price levels held about 830 contracts. That is depth (how much money you can trade before moving the price) of a few hundred dollars. Our rough ceiling for a careful small maker is tens of dollars a day, mostly rewards. It needs roughly $5,000–$10,000 resting across 10 cities, and it only works if pick-offs stay near zero (our estimate). The top Polymarket weather wallets show 2–8 cents of profit per dollar traded over 30 days. But that list shows only winners (anecdote: [Polycopy](https://polycopy.app/polymarket-leaderboard/weather)).

## Verdict: test one station model, build no forecast model

**Do not build a day-ahead forecast model, AI or otherwise, to trade against Kalshi weather prices.** The hard evidence and every honest 2026 test agree that it would lose. The free blend already holds the AI models. The best station model ties the market. And the market leads the forecasts, not the reverse.

**The one model worth a cheap test is a same-day station model for daily highs.** It would not forecast the weather. It would estimate what the official high already is, from free METAR and summary data, at each moment of the day. It is cheap because the data is free and we already hold the prices. It targets the one lead our own data produced. It also doubles as the defence a maker would need. Its weakness is competition. Bots already trade the releases. Our hourly price data cannot see gaps that last only seconds.

**Making is the cheaper way to hold weather positions, but only after a log-only test.** It saves the taker fee and earns rewards. The one public small maker still lost to pick-offs. Daily lows are the best place to try, because they pay the same reward as highs with far less trading. A stale-price trade from step 2 below would still be a taker trade. That is fine, because the taker fee near 3c or 97c is only about 0.2c per contract.

**Skip** AI model feeds, renting graphics cards, day-ahead station machine learning, radar rain forecasting, the hourly index, monthly totals and Polymarket abroad. Nothing goes live before the live bot's 6-week test ends on 27 Nov 2026. Every new test is written down and committed before any data is scored, as in `research/weather_favourites/PREREG.md`.

## Step 1 result (run Oct 26): the stale-price idea is dead

We checked all 33 losing city-days against free IEM data, with no Kalshi calls. The data covered hourly and special
airport reports, their 6-hour and 24-hour maximum groups, station summaries and the afternoon climate reports. The
code is `research/weather_favourites/losses_check.py`, with tests written first. Each day was put in one class by
when the settling degree (the official high) first became public. The stop rule was fixed before running.

| What happened on the losing day | City-days |
|---|---|
| The settling degree was already public before the 6 PM checkpoint | **0** |
| It was measured before 6 PM, but became public only after it | 8 |
| It was measured before 6 PM, and never became public before trading stopped | 0 |
| The air warmed after 6 PM: the high was set later that evening or night | **25** |

**The stop rule fires.** Most losses (25 of 33) were real late warming, not a misread station. On zero days was
the answer already public at 6 PM. So the market was not ignoring public data.

- **The 25 late-warming days** were mostly winter warm fronts at night (20 of 25 fell in November to March). Several
  highs were set at 11:59 PM. These are forecasting misses, not rules quirks.
- **The 8 "measured but hidden" days** all followed one pattern. The high was set around 4 PM. It showed up only in the
  evening 6-hour maximum group, about 51 minutes after the checkpoint. On all 8, the winning bracket then repriced:
  it went from 1.5–9.5c at 6 PM to 61–99.5c before trading stopped. So the market did react to the release. Eight
  days in a year, across 24 cities, is far too rare to build on, and bots already trade that release.

What survives is a forecasting question, not a data-plumbing one. On 25 evenings the market priced "the high is
done" at 90–99c, and the air warmed anyway. Did the free hourly forecasts (LAMP and the NBM) see that warming
coming? If so, the market underprices forecast overnight warming. That would be a narrow version of step 2. It needs
its own pre-registration, and it must count the wins as well as these losses.

## Conclusion

The question has flipped. We set out to forecast the weather better than the crowd. The evidence says the crowd's lead is biggest where weather is hardest: winter fronts, the LAX marine layer, the day before. Our one lead sits at the other end. There, the weather is already over, and the only task is reading the measurement correctly. That is not a weather edge. It is a data-plumbing edge. It lives in a handful of timestamps: the summaries, the 6-hour groups and the 24-hour group. The same knowledge serves both ideas left standing. It is what a filter needs to spot a stale favourite, and what a maker needs to avoid being picked off. If step 2 passes, it is worth building once, tests first, as a shared station library.

Thin weather edges also have short lives. The rain No-side edge fell from about 11 cents to about 1 cent per contract in 12 weeks. Our rain favourite test would need about 8 months to reach its bet count. A test that slow measures an edge after it has gone. So the next tests should use data we already hold and finish in days, which steps 1 and 2 do. A fast, clean fail would close the weather question for good. A pass would point to small money: tens of dollars a day, never a second engine beside the crypto bot.
