# Patient makers collect most prediction market profit

The best-proven way to make money on prediction markets is to be the maker. A maker is a trader who posts a resting order and waits. A taker is a trader who fills an order already on the book. Large studies of tens to hundreds of millions of real trades agree that money flows from takers to makers. On Kalshi, takers lost **about 1.1 cents per dollar on each trade**, on average, and makers gained about the same ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). The best maker seats belong to big firms with venue deals, such as Susquehanna and Jump. Private information has the next-best proof. A few researchers won big, like the 2024 election whale's estimated **$79 million**. Some insiders who traded on secrets now face criminal charges. Arbitrage (trades that lock in a profit whatever happens) inside one market made real money. So did buying heavy favourites (the likely side). But after fees and trading costs, little of either is left for takers. Speed trading, forecasting models, cross-venue arbitrage and copying top wallets have weak proof. Their biggest numbers mostly come from people selling bots or courses. Nearly every edge (profit per contract) is shrinking as firms arrive and venues add fees. The project's own bot is an exception worth watching. Its early live profit beats what public studies find for takers buying favourites. That makes the current 6-week test the deciding check, with a maker version the main idea to test afterwards.

## Eight strategies ranked: only market making has broad proof

I ranked each strategy by how strong the proof is that real money was made. This is not a ranking of what a reader could copy. Strong proof means large studies of real trades, court records or company filings. Weak proof means social posts, self-reported results, or numbers from people who sell bots, courses or sign-up links. I flag those sources where they appear. All findings are as of 25 October 2026.

A few words recur. Each contract pays $1 if it wins, so a 92c price means paying 92 cents to win $1. A **maker** is a trader who posts a resting order and waits. A **taker** is a trader who fills an order already on the book. The **spread** is the gap between the buy and sell price, what it costs to get in. An **edge** is profit per contract, in cents. **Arbitrage** is a set of trades that locks in a profit whatever happens.

| Rank | Strategy | How strong the proof of real profit is | Who gets the money | Is the edge fading? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market making: posting resting orders on both sides, earning the spread plus venue rewards (cash for keeping orders on the book) | Strong. Studies of 72 million to 588 million trades agree that money flows from takers to makers | Big firms with venue deals, sportsbooks, and bots | Slowly. Spreads are tighter and rewards smaller, but new products still pay makers well |
| 2 | Information edges: your own research, or secret information | Strong, but only for a few named people. Insider cases come from charging papers | A few researchers with big bankrolls; insiders | Insider trading, yes: US criminal charges began in 2026. Legal research, no clear sign |
| 3 | Arbitrage inside one market: buying every outcome for less than $1 in total | Medium. Peer-reviewed (checked by other researchers), but one year's estimate runs from $0.3 million to $40 million | Fast bots | Yes. Gaps now last about 16 seconds, and 2026 fees eat most of them |
| 4 | Buying heavy favourites (the likely side), including near-certain bets close to the end | Medium. The pricing pattern is well measured. For takers, profit after costs is about zero | Mostly makers, by my reading of the studies | Slowly. One study sees the pattern shrinking |
| 5 | Speed and news trading | Weak. Viral profit claims are unverified. Measured cases are small | Bots on servers next to the venue; people with faster feeds | Yes. Venues added fees and order delays to blunt it |
| 6 | Forecasting models: weather, sports, economic data, elections | Weak. Careful tests find no edge (profit per contract) left after fees | A few niche traders, at small size | Yes. Bots reprice within seconds of public data |
| 7 | Arbitrage across venues | Weak. Price gaps of 2% to 4% exist, but no audited profit, and the rules differ | Firms with accounts and cash on both venues | Slowly. Rule disputes grew in 2026 |
| 8 | Copying top wallets (accounts) | Weakest. Mostly tests by tool sellers. Past winners rarely repeat | Tool sellers and the copied wallets | Not measured |

Information edges rank above arbitrage because the dollar proof is larger and comes from several independent counts. But that money belongs to a handful of people, and some of it is criminal. Arbitrage ranks above favourite-buying because its profit was measured as real, completed trades. Favourite-buying returns are averages across price levels, and the after-cost tests for takers come out at zero or below.

The ranking rests on one fact about who wins. Most traders lose. On Polymarket, **69% to 84% of wallets lose money**, depending on how losses are counted ([Akey et al.](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21615); [TechFlow on Sergeenkov](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/31018)). A wallet is one account on the public blockchain record, and one person can own many. In a study of 588 million Polymarket trades, the top 1% of winners took **about 76.5% of all profit** ([Akey et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cpr:ceprdp:21615)). A Wall Street Journal analysis found **0.1% of accounts took 67% of profits**, nearly $500 million ([Benzinga on WSJ](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/05/52251195/0-1-of-polymarket-accounts-take-home-67-of-all-profits-investigation-finds)). Kalshi has no public account data. Its chief executive said in July 2026 that two in three of its users lose, a company statement ([How Gambling Works](https://howgamblingworks.substack.com/p/are-30-of-kalshi-users-really-profitable)).

The winners share a habit. They mostly post resting orders instead of taking prices ([Akey et al.](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21615)). Bloomberg found about 800 bot-like Polymarket wallets made **about $131 million** between January 2025 and April 2026. Everyone else lost about the same amount, combined ([Finance Magnates on Bloomberg](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/100000-polymarket-wallets-lost-at-least-1000-bloomberg-analysis-shows/)). Betfair, the UK betting exchange, shows where this tends to end. Since 2008 it has charged extra to a tiny group of steady winners, at first under 0.5% of members ([Wikipedia: Betfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betfair)). Since January 2025, its "Expert Fee" takes 40% of yearly gross profit above £100,000 ([Racing Post](https://racingpost.com/news/britain/betfair-exchange-to-introduce-new-commission-system-for-2025-as-premium-charge-is-dropped-a7wbg0v4GCAJ)). A venue only taxes a group like that if the group keeps winning.

## Strong proof: makers and the well-informed take the money

In this section, a maker is a trader who posts a resting order and waits. A taker is a trader who fills an order already on the book. The spread is the gap between the buy and sell price, what it costs to get in. Adverse selection means getting picked off by someone who knows more than you.

### Market making pays about 1.1 cents per dollar, mostly to big firms

The cleanest number comes from Jon Becker's study of Kalshi. It covers 72.1 million trades worth $18.26 billion, up to November 2025. Takers lost **about 1.12 cents per dollar on each trade**, on average. Makers gained about the same. Takers lost money at 80 of 99 price levels ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). Becker says makers win by soaking up one-sided, biased orders, not by forecasting better. A second Kalshi study, by Bürgi, Deng and Whelan, points the same way with bigger numbers. Takers lost almost 32 cents per dollar on the average contract, and makers lost about 10 cents ([VoxEU column](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market); snippet only, because the page blocked direct access). That average counts each contract equally. So cheap longshots (the unlikely side), where buyers lose over 60%, drive it ([UCD working paper](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf); snippet only). On Polymarket, the 588-million-trade study finds the same split. Winners mostly post resting orders, and losers mostly take prices ([Akey et al.](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21615)).

The pool makers draw from is large. The Roosevelt Institute estimates Kalshi takers lost **$583.5 million net** from July 2021 to 15 May 2026. Nearly two thirds of that, $371.6 million, was in sports ([Roosevelt Institute](https://rooseveltinstitute.org/blog/since-kalshis-launch-ordinary-users-have-lost-half-a-billion-dollars/)). Kalshi disputes the method. It says the study mixes up makers and takers with professionals and casual users ([Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/kalshi-rebuffs-think-tank-claim-regular-traders-lost-584-million-on-platform/)). Parlays (several bets combined into one) are richer still. Kalshi parlay takers lost **over $870 million after fees** since parlays launched in September 2025. Kalshi itself took $336 million in parlay fees over that span ([InGame](https://www.ingame.com/parlay-takers-lose-dkex-polymarket-us/)). My reading is that most of the rest went to the makers on the other side. Company results agree, though these are the companies' own figures. Flutter, FanDuel's parent, booked $6 million of market-making revenue from prediction markets in the second quarter of 2026. It expects **$50 million for the year**. DraftKings says it makes these markets profitably on three exchanges ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)).

Small makers can still earn, but the best proof is old and self-reported. One Polymarket trader turned $10,000 into about $200 a day of income. He scaled to $700 to $800 a day at the 2024 peak. He stopped when rewards were cut after the election ([The Oracle](https://news.polymarket.com/p/automated-market-making-on-polymarket); self-reported, in Polymarket's own newsletter, May 2025). Liquidity rewards are cash a venue pays makers for keeping orders on the book. Polymarket scores them with a squared formula. So tight, large, always-on quotes take most of each pool ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/liquidity-rewards)). A former Kalshi trader put Polymarket's reward spend at about $300,000 a month in August 2025 ([Rajaprabhakaran Substack](https://ufoholdings.substack.com/p/kalshis-new-liquidity-incentives)). Since 2026, Polymarket also pays makers a rebate (fee money handed back). It is 20% of crypto taker fees, 15% in sports and 25% elsewhere ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/maker-rebates-program)). One site counted 126 market-making wallets that collected $3.75 million in rebates and $1.12 million in rewards over 90 days ([Polbots](https://polbots.com/guides/does-polymarket-copy-trading-work); the site lists paid tools).

The main cost is adverse selection: getting picked off by someone who knows more than you. A study of 41.6 million Kalshi trades found makers earn **twice as much per contract** in single-event markets, like one game. But runs of one-sided trades there predict maker losses. Markets on a broad price level, like a stock index, showed no such pattern ([Stanford Law summary of Bartlett and O'Hara](https://law.stanford.edu/2026/04/21/adverse-selection-in-prediction-markets-evidence-from-kalshi/)). A one-day test on Polymarket's 5-minute bitcoin markets shows the danger. Resting orders at the best bid lost $26,133. Prices moved 4 to 4.6 cents a share against each fill ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/levl/two-polymarket-strategies-that-look-fine-until-you-add-fees-and-a-queue-5g4p); self-reported, by a maintainer of the open-source test tool). On 21 August, Polymarket US parlay takers made $9.4 million after fees on $4.4 million of stakes. InGame blames possible maker mispricing in a two-hour window that morning ([InGame](https://www.ingame.com/parlay-takers-lose-dkex-polymarket-us/)). Even Kalshi's own maker desk was not profitable in late 2025, its co-founder said ([InGame](https://www.ingame.com/kalshi-in-house-trading-arm-not-profitable/)). The job also needs a bot that quotes all day and pulls quotes fast on news. Kalshi's designated makers must quote for 98% of every hour ([Kalshi Help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823819-how-to-become-a-market-maker-on-kalshi)).

Big firms hold the best seats. Susquehanna became Kalshi's first outside market maker in April 2024 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240403664852/en/Kalshi-Onboards-Its-First-Dedicated-Institutional-Market-Maker)). Jump Trading took stakes in Kalshi and Polymarket around February 2026, in return for making markets. It doubled its team to about 20 people. DRW also runs a dedicated team ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/jump-trading-prediction-markets-wall-street/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/389086/jump-trading-to-serve-as-polymarket-and-kalshi-market-maker-in-exchange-for-stake-bloomberg)). These firms get deals small traders cannot. Kalshi's member terms allow makers fee discounts, rebates and a share of fee revenue. The amounts sit in confidential filings with the CFTC, the US regulator ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)). Only caps are public. Kalshi's newest program is open only to firms with a signed market-maker agreement. Its lower tier caps pay at $1,000 per market per day. Its top tier caps pay at $50,000 per series per week ([Navnoor Bawa Research](https://www.navnoorbawaresearch.com/p/kalshi-publishes-one-liquidity-subsidy); a paid Patreon, citing CFTC filings).

The edge is moving, not vanishing. Early Kalshi takers, in 2021 to 2023, made about 2 cents per dollar per trade. The flow reversed after the 2024 election boom brought in professional makers ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). One site reports a typical Kalshi spread of 2.4 cents. It says spreads shrank 43% while volume rose 166% ([StockAlarm](https://pro.stockalarm.io/predict/research); unclear who measured it). Polymarket cut rewards sharply after the 2024 election ([The Oracle](https://news.polymarket.com/p/automated-market-making-on-polymarket)). Kalshi's open Liquidity Incentive Program ran only to 1 September 2026 ([Kalshi Help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)). Its Volume Incentive Program ended from 13 October 2026, under regulator scrutiny ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/kalshi-ends-volume-incentive-program/); end date snippet only). But new products still pay well. Parlay takers lose 14% to 26% of their stakes on Kalshi, Polymarket US and DraftKings' exchange ([InGame](https://www.ingame.com/parlay-takers-lose-dkex-polymarket-us/)). **Market making is the steady money in this field. But the best seats belong to firms with deals small traders cannot get.**

### Private information pays the most per bet, for a handful of people

The largest single win came from legal research. "Théo", a French trader, bet more than $45 million on Trump in 2024. He paid for polls that asked people how their neighbours would vote, to get around shy voters ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/324996/french-polymarket-whale-us-election-profit-france-ban)). Blockchain analysis firm Chainalysis put his profit at **about $78.7 million** across nine accounts. Other counts say $48 million from four accounts, or $85 million from eleven ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/324996/french-polymarket-whale-us-election-profit-france-ban); [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/polymarket-whale-funded-neighbor-surveys-to-win-85m-on-trump/)). None of these is audited. They are estimates from the public blockchain record. And it was one event. That is one very large bet, not a method anyone can test.

Two steadier human cases exist. Domer, Polymarket's top trader in 2024, reported over **$2.5 million of net profit** across nearly 10,000 predictions by January 2026 ([MetaMask interview](https://metamask.io/news/advanced-prediction-market-trading-strategies); self-reported). Most of his trades are his own resting orders being filled. He reviews hundreds of markets a day and finds nine in ten fairly priced. He favours brand-new markets where nobody has a head start ([On Chain Times](https://www.onchaintimes.com/a-chat-with-domer-the-1-trader-on-polymarket/); older, 2024). Brian Golden, a former theatre director, made over $2 million on Kalshi economic contracts. That is more than three times the next public account in that category ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-13/kalshi-s-top-inflation-trader-beats-wall-street-banks-from-his-garage); snippet only, because the page was blocked). Becker's data fits these cases. The gap between makers and takers is smallest in finance contracts, about 0.17 cents per dollar per trade ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). That is where a skilled outsider can compete.

Insider trading is the illegal version. These are the best-documented cases.

Charges are allegations, not convictions.

The wider problem is bigger than these cases. Researchers Mitts (Columbia) and Ofir screened Polymarket from February 2024 to February 2026. They estimate **at least $143 million** of suspicious profit. That is a floor, a minimum. No trade in it is a confirmed insider trade. Flagged traders won 69.9% of the time, far too often to be luck (over 60 standard deviations above chance) ([Mitts and Ofir, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6426778); [Harvard Law forum](http://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/03/25/from-iran-to-taylor-swift-informed-trading-in-prediction-markets/)). Insiders earn the biggest percentage returns in this field. They buy at prices near zero, so $34,000 can become $400,000. But each case is one event, and the dollars are small next to market making.

For legal research, the cost is small next to the money needed. Théo's poll only mattered because the 2024 election market could absorb $45 million. That depth has shrunk for politics. It was about 0.4% of Kalshi volume in July 2026, by Pew's figures ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/23/prediction-markets-trading-volume-doubled-between-may-and-july-largely-driven-by-sports/)). For insiders, the cost is now legal risk. The US brought its first criminal case over a prediction-market bet in April 2026 ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/us-soldier-charged-using-classified-information-profit-prediction-market-bets)). Polymarket adopted insider rules on 23 March 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-23/polymarket-implements-new-insider-trading-rules-after-scrutiny)). Kalshi opened more than 150 insider investigations in the first quarter of 2026 ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/regulation/kalshi-insider-trading-investigations-q1-employer-checks)). Kalshi's own fines are small, from about $540 to $71,356 ([Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/kalshi-political-insider-trading-enforcement-update); [WJLA](https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/kalshi-permanently-bans-santos-over-bets-on-state-of-the-union-attendance-president-trump-new-york-republican-congress)). Legal research stays legal. The CFTC's ban covers information used in breach of a duty, not all private knowledge ([CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11406)). **Information pays the most per bet. But it belongs to a handful of people, and the illegal kind now faces prosecution.**

## Middling proof: real money, mostly erased by fees and costs

In this section, arbitrage is a set of trades that locks in a profit whatever happens. The taker fee is Kalshi's charge for trading, biggest near 50c. Polymarket's taker fee, added in 2026, has the same shape. A taker is a trader who fills an order already on the book. A maker is a trader who posts a resting order and waits. The spread is the gap between the buy and sell price, what it costs to get in.

### Arbitrage inside one market made $0.3 million to $40 million in a year

The simplest arbitrage buys every outcome of one market for less than $1 in total. One outcome must pay $1, so the gap is profit. The biggest study found **about $40 million** of such profit taken on Polymarket from April 2024 to April 2025 ([Saguillo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)). It was peer-reviewed (checked by other researchers) and published at the AFT 2025 conference ([Dagstuhl](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27)). Polymarket charged no trading fee then, so none was subtracted. About three quarters came from markets with many outcomes. Most came from politics around the 2024 election ([Saguillo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03474v1)). A stricter 2026 study counted only gaps the system actually let you cash in. It found **just $291,424** in the same year, about 1/136th as much. Across all of Polymarket's history to the end of 2025, it found $1.12 million ([Gebele, Mutzel and Matthes](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00666v1)). So treat $40 million as a ceiling, not a measured profit. Neither figure is audited.

The money went to a few fast accounts. The top account made about $2 million over 4,049 transactions, about $496 each. The top ten took about a fifth of the total ([Saguillo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03474v1)). The gaps are short. In April and May 2026, the typical (median) cashable gap lasted 16 seconds ([Gebele, Mutzel and Matthes](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00666v1)). Depth caps the size. Depth is how much money you can trade before moving the price. In busy 5-minute bitcoin markets it is about $5,000 to $15,000 per side ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/02/21/how-ai-is-helping-retail-traders-exploit-prediction-market-glitches-to-make-easy-money)). CoinDesk also relayed a social post about a bot that made nearly $150,000 over 8,894 trades. Nobody verified it. A developer who ran his own bot for 176 trades made about $2 in total ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/manja316/176-trades-on-polymarket-what-my-bot-actually-made-its-not-what-you-think-3iib); self-reported).

Fees killed most of what was left. Polymarket added taker fees in steps from January to March 2026. On crypto markets the fee is up to 1.75 cents per share at 50c, on each leg ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees); [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout)). So a 2-cent gap spread across two legs no longer pays. The old PredictIt case shows what kept gaps open before. Its $850 cap per contract kept arbitrage traders small. So its prices across outcomes often added to more than 110 cents, a sure profit even after fees ([Zitzewitz, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35845); older data, 2016 to 2022). **Arbitrage inside one market made real money in fee-free 2024. Now it belongs to the fastest bots and pays little after fees.**

### Heavy favourites beat their price by a cent per dollar or less

The favourite-longshot bias is the best-measured pricing pattern in this field. It means cheap unlikely bets are overpriced, and heavy favourites underpriced. A favourite is the likely side, and a longshot the unlikely side. Calibration is whether a 70c price really wins 70% of the time. On Kalshi, buyers of contracts under 10c lose over 60% of their money. Contracts above 50c earn a small positive return that is unlikely to be luck ([UCD working paper](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf); snippet only). Becker found 95c contracts won 95.83% of the time, a bit more than their price implies ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). Kalshi politics is the most biased category. A 70c political contract one week before close won about 83% of the time ([Le](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v2)).

Polymarket has the clearest dollar figures. A study of 588 million trades looked at favourites priced 90c or more. Before fees, they earned **+0.28 to +0.83 cents of profit for every dollar bet**. The low figure counts each market once. The high figure weights markets by money traded ([Cardozo and Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1)). Politics favourites did best, at +1.04 to +1.42 cents for every dollar bet. Crypto earned +0.54 to +0.64 cents. Sports earned about nothing, between −0.23 and +0.14 cents ([Cardozo and Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1)). A separate database study gives favourites at 90c to 100c about +0.56 cents of profit per contract, before fees ([Qin and Yang](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04217v1)).

After costs, the gain shrinks to about zero for takers. An independent GitHub study, not peer-reviewed, checked 46,168 resolved markets. Kalshi contracts at 95c or more, one day out, lost 22 times out of 3,360. Their prices implied about 46 losses. But after the spread and the taker fee, buying favourites **lost 0 to 2 cents per dollar**. No price bucket was clearly profitable. A price correction built on older markets also failed on newer ones. That was an out-of-sample test, on data the rule never saw while being built ([hrishi-tailor](https://github.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration)). A one-day test on Polymarket's 5-minute bitcoin markets tells the same story. Buying the favourite in the last minute won 82.4% of 108 trades. Then came fees of $52.90 and slippage of $4.22. Slippage is filling worse than the price you aimed at. Together they turned a small gain into a $15.62 loss ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/levl/two-polymarket-strategies-that-look-fine-until-you-add-fees-and-a-queue-5g4p); self-reported). One more warning: on Polymarket, the accounts that buy favourites most often earn **less** than other accounts ([Cardozo and Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12878)).

The taker fee decides who can earn this. Kalshi's taker fee is 7% × price × (1 − price) per contract, rounded up to the next cent ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)). For every dollar bet, that works out to 7% × (1 − price). So it barely touches favourites. The table is my calculation from that formula. Polymarket's crypto fee uses the same 7% rate and shape ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)).

| Buy price | Fee per contract, before rounding up | Fee for every dollar bet |
|---|---|---|
| 5c (a longshot) | 0.33 cents | 6.65 cents |
| 50c (a coin flip) | 1.75 cents | 3.5 cents |
| 90c | 0.63 cents | 0.70 cents |
| 92c | 0.52 cents | 0.56 cents |
| 95c (a heavy favourite) | 0.33 cents | 0.35 cents |
| 99c | 0.07 cents | 0.07 cents |

At the favourite end, the spread costs more than the fee. Paying half of a 1-cent spread at 95c costs about 0.5 cents for every dollar bet (my arithmetic). Kalshi's fee schedule describes rounding up to the next cent. That would triple the fee on a one-contract order at 95c. But the bot's own live fills show Kalshi rounds up only to the next hundredth of a cent (checked 25 October 2026; see the last section). So rounding costs almost nothing. The spread and the fee together can still swallow the whole measured edge (profit per contract, in cents). That is why makers, who post resting orders and pay no spread, keep most of it.

The rare loss is the other cost. At 95c, one loss wipes out 19 wins. Some losses come from disputes, not facts. In March 2025, a $7 million Polymarket market on a Ukraine minerals deal settled Yes. Its Yes price had jumped from about 9 cents, yet no deal was signed. So buyers of No, the favourite at about 91c, lost. Polymarket blamed a large holder in UMA, the token-holder vote it uses to settle disputes ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348171/polymarket-says-governance-attack-by-uma-whale-to-hijack-a-bets-resolution-is-unprecedented); older). A $16 million UFO-files market settled Yes amid disputes, after late buying at 99c to 99.9c. Critics said large holders forced the result without evidence ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/polymarket-faces-major-credibility-crisis-after-whales-forced-a-yes-ufo-vote-without-evidence/); snippet only). On Kalshi, the "Khamenei out" market ran into a rule covering a leader's death in March 2026. Kalshi settled at the last price before the news, not at Yes. It reportedly refunded fees and net losses ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/?p=6405)). It now faces a class action seeking $54 million ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/kalshi-sued-khamenei-trade-carveout)).

Short crypto markets have no peer-reviewed study by price level. Kalshi crypto prices look close to right at short horizons ([Le](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v2)). One bitcoin sample found late flips depend on distance, not price. Some Kalshi 15-minute windows finished $50 or more from the start price. Those flipped in the last two minutes about 1% of the time. Windows that finished within $50 flipped about 21% of the time ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/are-kalshi-15-minute-bitcoin-markets-rigged); affiliate links and a Polymarket referral code). A backtest of 4,904 Kalshi 15-minute bitcoin strategies found only 102 made money ([Turbine](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/5000-strategy-backtest-kalshi-btc-15m); vendor). The blunt cousin of this strategy, betting No on everything, also fails. About 72% of contracts settle No, but prices already reflect that ([Delta Confidence](https://deltaconfidence.substack.com/p/nothing-ever-happens)).

The pattern may be fading. The Kalshi authors see some evidence the bias is shrinking over time ([UCD working paper](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf); snippet only). On Polymarket in 2026, weather prices even lean the other way, with favourites overpriced ([hrishi-tailor](https://github.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration)). The studies also conflict on where the bias lives. One finds it in thin Kalshi markets. Another finds it stronger in heavily traded Polymarket events ([hrishi-tailor](https://github.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration); [Cardozo and Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1)). **The favourite pattern is real. For takers, it is about the size of the fees and spread, so makers keep most of it.**

## Weak proof: speed, models, cross-venue gaps and copying

In this section, a taker is a trader who fills an order already on the book. A maker is a trader who posts a resting order and waits. Adverse selection means getting picked off by someone who knows more than you. Depth is how much money you can trade before moving the price. An edge is profit per contract, in cents. Arbitrage is a set of trades that locks in a profit whatever happens. The taker fee is Kalshi's charge for trading, biggest near 50c. Polymarket's taker fee works the same way.

### Speed trading wins seconds-long races, and the famous numbers are unverified

Latency means delay. A latency bot trades in the gap between a price move on a big exchange and the prediction market catching up. The famous case is the Polymarket wallet "0x8dxd". Reports say it turned **$313 into $414,000 to $438,000** from December 2025 to January 2026 ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/); [MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/news/417346)). Nobody independent has checked those figures, and they differ by source. A later review looked at the top 40 wallets. It says 0x8dxd bought YES and NO at the same moment in about 94% of its trades. Its typical position was under $6. That looks like market making, not sniping stale prices ([LeoLabs](https://leolabs.me/blog/pm-top20-strategy-patterns/en/); sells a paid quant product and course, and uses affiliate links). Hands-on reports are sobering. One directional 15-minute strategy lost 37.81% ([LayerX](https://layerx.xyz/blog/polymarketbots)).

The best sign the speed trade paid is that the venue moved to stop it. Polymarket added a taker fee to 15-minute crypto markets on 5 January 2026. Its stated aim was to blunt latency trading ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/)). In February 2026 it quietly removed a half-second hold on taker orders. That hold had let makers pull stale quotes in time. One trader said speed is now the only thing that keeps rivals out ([Protos](https://protos.com/polymarket-ends-trading-loophole-for-bitcoin-quants/)). Polymarket's servers sit in Amazon's London data centre and Kalshi's in Amazon's Ohio one. So the fastest bots rent servers next to them ([Glassnode](https://latency.glassnode.com/prediction-markets/about)).

In live sports, the edge is broadcast delay. At the February 2026 Super Bowl, streams ran up to 62 seconds behind the action. Over-the-air TV ran 19 seconds behind. Traders bought antennas, and one timed an anthem rehearsal with a stopwatch ([NPR via KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/science-technology/2026/02/17/tv-antennas-and-super-bowl-rehearsals-how-prediction-market-traders-seek-an-edge)). Polymarket now holds sports orders that would fill at once for 3 seconds. It also cancels resting orders when a game starts ([Polymarket Help](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364444-limit-orders)). A UCLA study of 173 NBA games found only 7 arbitrage chances inside single markets. They lasted a typical 3.6 seconds. Over the month, they were worth **$210** with a $100 cap per chance, or $4,418 with no cap. The average chance could fill only about 14.8 shares. The study's snapshots came every 3.6 to 5.5 seconds, so it missed faster chances ([arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1)). A claimed $8 million sports bot is unconfirmed ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/sports-bot-earns-8-million-on-polymarket-by-exploiting-time-lag-55871)).

Election night 2024 was fast reading of public vote counts, not a secret edge ([Sherwood](https://www.sherwood.news/world/donald-trump-has-won-the-election-traders-were-betting-on-it-overnight/)). I found no hard evidence of bots profiting in the seconds after inflation or jobs data on Kalshi. Speed needs little money but hits depth limits fast. The prize pool is big, though. Kalshi's 15-minute bitcoin markets traded 6.74 billion contracts in September 2026. Taker fees on them were about $60.4 million that month, by an outside estimate from Predict Charts. Fast markets made about 80% of Kalshi's non-sports fees in early October ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/igaming/kalshis-15-minute-gold-bets-top-ether-fast-markets-drive-fees/)). **Speed edges are real but short and small per trade, and venues keep adding fees and delays to blunt them.**

### Forecasting models rarely beat prices that already hold public data

| Category | Best public test result | How deep the market is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weather (daily high temperature) | No usable edge (profit per contract) after fees, across 1,506 New York days | About $22,000 traded on one sample New York day | Fails for takers; small edge for makers in one test |
| Sports | Kalshi prices sit about 0.75 percentage points of win chance from Pinnacle's fair price (Pinnacle is a bookmaker known for accurate odds) | Typical $501 at the best price; $13,067 for NFL games | Fails as a model; becomes a speed race |
| Economic data (inflation, Fed rates) | Kalshi's inflation forecast beat Wall Street's average forecast | Fed-rate contracts about $12 million across 2026 meetings | The market already beats the public models |
| Elections | Models beat the old PredictIt in 2020 to 2022, but all lost on the 2024 presidential race | Politics about 0.4% of Kalshi volume in July 2026 | Fails on big races; thin in small ones |

The careful weather test came from OddsReference ([OddsReference](https://oddsreference.com/predictions/research/kalshi-weather-model); affiliate, as it may earn a commission on Kalshi sign-ups). It blended two US weather models for New York, over 1,506 days and 40,032 contracts. Its best rule, selling unlikely temperature ranges, won 96.6% of the time. But as a taker it earned almost nothing. As a maker it did better, though the article's own numbers disagree with each other. Its verdict: Kalshi weather prices already reflect public forecasts. An AI trading-agent blog went 0 for 32 on Kalshi daily highs. Big forecast misses came about twice as often as its bell-curve model assumed. Other bots also repriced within seconds of each forecast update ([Northlake Labs](https://www.northlakelabs.com/max/blog/kalshi-weather-postmortem-and-pivot/); self-reported). Polymarket has small named winners. Bloomberg found a 23-year-old law student among the top six all-time weather earners. A Finnish developer made about $33,000 in six months. A Swiss weather-AI firm said its returns are high, but the market is too thin for a big fund ([Claims Journal, from Bloomberg](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/04/15/336839.htm)). A claim that the student made $1.11 million from one bet is secondhand and unchecked ([KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/traders-on-polymarket-earn-millions-by-predicting-weather)). The one-day depth figure comes from a market data site ([SimpleFunctions](https://simplefunctions.dev/markets/will-the-high-temp-in-nyc-be-89-on-jul-13-2026-kalshi-kxhighny-26jul13-t89)). Kalshi weather prices also look too sure of themselves in the final hour. That is the reverse of the favourite pattern ([Le](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v2)).

Sports is about 80% of Kalshi volume ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/27/trading-volume-on-prediction-markets-has-soared-in-recent-months/)). Yet Kalshi prices sat close to the fair odds at Pinnacle, a bookmaker known for accurate prices. Across 88 games, the typical gap was about 0.75 percentage points of win chance. Kalshi's built-in cut was a quarter of Pinnacle's ([OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/polymarket-api-kalshi-api-vs-sportsbooks-the-developers-guide/); sells an odds data service). That gap is smaller than the taker fee, Kalshi's charge for trading, which is 1.75 cents at 50c. Two public bots tried copying Pinnacle's prices. One made about $5,000 net by posting orders. It stopped when faster bots began picking off its orders ([polymm on GitHub](https://github.com/kachence/polymm); self-reported). The other, an NHL project, got prices about 1.2% worse than the final pre-game price (closing line value −1.23%). The range the true number is probably in runs from −2.01% to −0.46%. Only 8 of its 102 test bids filled ([nhl-edge on GitHub](https://github.com/HeikkiMatias/nhl-edge/issues/317)).

Economic data is the reverse case: the market beats the models. A Federal Reserve staff paper found Kalshi's headline inflation forecast missed by 0.063 percentage points on average. The Bloomberg survey of forecasters missed by 0.081. The gap was unlikely to be luck. Kalshi's Fed-rate forecasts matched futures markets, where big investors bet on rates ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/kalshi-and-the-rise-of-macro-markets.htm); [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/study-finds-kalshi-predictions-reliable/); numbers via news reports, as the PDF was not opened). Nobody has published a test showing that trading against Kalshi with the Cleveland Fed's inflation model makes money. The Fed-rate volume figure was seen only in a search summary ([Dune](https://dune.com/blog/pricing-the-fed-kalshi-polymarket-cme-fedwatch)). Brian Golden's $2 million shows a human edge here. But it is skill, not a public model.

Election models scored better than PredictIt in the 2020 and 2022 Senate races ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/155928); older, scores via a search summary). PredictIt's $850 cap kept smart money out ([Zitzewitz, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35845)). In 2024, every model would have lost about 10% trading against Polymarket on the presidential race ([Sethi](https://rajivsethi.substack.com/p/an-election-day-update-on-forecasting); older). All models failed to beat the market on that headline contract. Some did beat it in less-watched races ([Barnard](https://barnard.edu/news/professor-rajiv-sethi-publishes-new-research-political-prediction-and-collective-intelligence)). Those quiet races are the thinnest markets. **Public data reaches the price within seconds, so models only earn in thin niches at small size.**

### Cross-venue gaps of 2% to 4% are really bets on rule wording

Prices for the same question often differ across venues. A 2026 study matched events across 10 venues, including Kalshi, Polymarket and PredictIt, from 2018 to August 2025. For the most traded pairs, the typical gap after trading costs was **2% to 4%**. Many pairs stayed apart for nearly their whole shared life ([Gebele and Matthes](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706v1)). A simple rule that always took the best gap returned 1,218.66% over 800 days, about 13 times the starting money. But that came from only 15 trades, far too few to trust. In 2024, the presidential gap after costs averaged about 3 cents and held at up to 7 cents ([Gebele and Matthes](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706v1)).

The gaps last because the contracts are not truly the same. In the 2024 election, Polymarket settled on the TV networks' call, and Kalshi on the inauguration ([Gebele and Matthes](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706v1)). At the 2026 Super Bowl, Kalshi called Cardi B's appearance unclear. It settled at the last traded price, 26 cents for Yes. Polymarket first settled Yes, then sent it to an UMA token-holder vote ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/cardi-b-is-a-cautionary-tale-for-prediction-markets/)). A hedge holding both sides would have won or lost depending on which way round it was. In January 2026, Polymarket ruled the US capture of Maduro was not an "invasion" under its wording ([Quartz](https://qz.com/polymarket-says-us-raid-venezuela-not-invasion)). Each mismatch turns a sure thing into a bet on how two rulebooks read.

Costs stack up. Near 50c, a two-leg trade pays about 1.75 cents on Kalshi plus 1 to 1.75 cents on Polymarket ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf); [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)). Cash sits locked until settlement on both venues. Kalshi pays interest on it, shown at 3.25% to 4.05% a year at different times. Polymarket pays only on a few long-dated markets ([Kalshi Help](https://help.kalshi.com/navigating-the-exchange/your-portfolio/apy-on-kalshi); [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/holding-rewards)). Many US traders also cannot use global Polymarket ([NYC Servers](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/prediction-market-arbitrage-guide); vendor). The project's own test of Kalshi against Polymarket US on 15-minute bitcoin (13 to 16 October 2026) found no arbitrage. Polymarket's public price feed was a typical 15 seconds old. That stale data made 30% of pairs look profitable. With fresh prices, only about 20% were positive after fees. The typical pair **lost 0.8 to 1.4 cents**. Scanner sellers advertise hundreds of chances a week ([Oddpool](https://www.oddpool.com/pricing); vendor). One scanner seller admits most scanners use last-trade or middle prices you can never trade at ([Apify](https://apify.com/ebag73/polymarket-arb-scanner); vendor). No audited profit exists for any cross-venue trader. **Cross-venue gaps are real on paper, but the profit depends on rule wording, locked cash and two sets of fees.**

### Copying top wallets repeats at about coin-flip odds

Copy trading means mirroring a winning wallet's trades. Polymarket has no built-in copy feature, so every tool is third-party. The best test comes from Polbots. It says it sells no copy trading, but it lists paid tools from $49 a month ([Polbots](https://polbots.com/guides/does-polymarket-copy-trading-work)). It filtered 407 leaderboard wallets down to 33 active winners who traded at a human pace. Copying them 1 to 2 minutes late kept **about 78% of the leader's profit**. Leaders made 5.5% on $22.6 million of buys. Copiers 1 to 2 minutes behind made about 3.8%. But that replay scored the same 30 days used to pick the wallets. That is an in-sample test, which flatters results. It also ignored price impact and partial fills.

The forward test is the honest one. It built the lists first, then scored them on the next 30 days. That makes it out-of-sample: tested on data the rule never saw while being built. It returned +15.3%, +15.8%, +10.3% and −5.3% across four overlapping runs. One wallet drove the +10.3% run. Without it, the rest lost 1.7%. The authors call this evidence too thin ([Polbots](https://polbots.com/guides/does-polymarket-copy-trading-work)). The deeper problem is picking. A winning month was followed by another winning month only 41% to 51% of the time, near a coin flip ([Vultax](https://vultax.com/research/best-polymarket-traders-leaderboards); sells a trading terminal). Over half of wallets that made $5,000 or more in a month were active for only that month ([TechFlow on Sergeenkov](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/31018)). Leaderboards also hide losses. One top sports wallet showed $10.35 million of lifetime profit but lost $479,000 in the 30 days to mid-March 2026 ([LeoLabs](https://leolabs.me/blog/pm-top20-strategy-patterns/en/); sells a quant course, uses affiliate links).

The best wallets cannot be copied at all. Market makers earn rebates and rewards that copiers never see. Sub-second bots act before any copier can. Copiers only inherit the taker side. So they pay the spread (the gap between the buy and sell price, what it costs to get in) that the leader avoided. Late copiers also push prices up for each other. **Copy trading's proof comes almost entirely from tool sellers, and past winners repeat at about coin-flip odds.**

## Conclusion

Prediction-market profit is mostly not made by forecasting. It is made by holding the right seat. The pool of profit is ordinary takers' losses, plus the fees they pay. (A taker is a trader who fills an order already on the book.) Venues now design their fees to steer that pool. Polymarket's 2026 taker fees fund rebates for makers, the traders who post resting orders and wait. Kalshi's private deals give its biggest makers discounts and a share of fees. Across this evidence, a trader's seat says more about profit than the quality of the trader's model. The seat can be maker, taker, insider or venue partner.

Two patterns follow. First, the taker edges (profit per contract) that survive sit where the taker fee is smallest, at the price extremes. On Kalshi, that fee is its charge for trading, biggest near 50c. Or they rest on information nobody else has. Anything built on public data gets priced within seconds. Second, the quality of evidence runs opposite to its volume. The loudest numbers, like viral bots and copy-trading returns, are the least checked. The best-checked numbers are small: about a cent per dollar for makers, and a cent or less for favourites. Betfair adds one last lesson. Once a group of steady winners shows up, the venue eventually charges it more. Any trader who keeps winning should expect the rules to change.

## What this means for the bot: test a maker version after 27 November

The bot sits in strategy four. It buys heavy favourites (the likely side) priced 92c and up in Kalshi's 15-minute crypto markets. It is a taker, so it fills orders already on the book. It pays the taker fee, Kalshi's charge for trading, biggest near 50c. Public studies say takers buying favourites roughly break even or lose after costs. One Kalshi study puts it at **0 to 2 cents lost for every dollar bet** ([hrishi-tailor](https://github.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration); a GitHub study, not peer-reviewed). Polymarket crypto favourites earned +0.54 to +0.64 cents of profit for every dollar bet, before fees ([Cardozo and Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1)). The bot's early live result was **about +1.4 cents of profit for every dollar bet** (16 October). That matched its backtest of +0.9 to +1.3 cents. It sits above the public numbers for crypto favourites, and far above the after-cost numbers for takers. Either the bot's two entry signs find something the broad studies average away, or the early sample is lucky. The fixed 6-week test, running unchanged to 27 November, is the right way to tell. A short run of losses can move the result a lot. At 92c, one loss erases about 11.5 wins. A few losses in a row would set the deepest fall (the worst drop from a peak).

Some of the evidence backs the bot. The fee is near its smallest at these prices. It is about 0.56 cents for every dollar bet at 92c, and 0.35 cents at 95c. A large Kalshi study found markets on a broad price level showed less sign of informed traders than single-event markets ([Stanford Law summary](https://law.stanford.edu/2026/04/21/adverse-selection-in-prediction-markets-evidence-from-kalshi/)). Crypto price markets look like the broad kind, by my reading. That fits the bot's own finding of no adverse selection, no sign of getting picked off by someone who knows more. In early October, Kalshi earned about 80% of its non-sports fees from 15-minute markets. So it has reason to keep them open ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/igaming/kalshis-15-minute-gold-bets-top-ether-fast-markets-drive-fees/)). Rule risk is lower than in event markets. One price feed decides each window, and there is no wording to dispute. The project's own check found Kalshi and Polymarket US settled the same way in 206 of 206 windows.

Some of it is a warning. In crypto, Kalshi takers did about 2.7 cents per dollar worse than makers on each trade, on average ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). The bot pays that gap on every fill. Big firms are moving into fast markets, and the favourite pattern shows signs of shrinking. Kalshi's 15-minute bitcoin markets traded 6.74 billion contracts in September 2026, so the competition is heavy ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/igaming/kalshis-15-minute-gold-bets-top-ether-fast-markets-drive-fees/)). And Kalshi's Khamenei ruling shows a venue's rules can override the price in rare cases ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/?p=6405)).

These are ideas to test after 27 November, not changes to make now. The first is a maker version, run in shadow mode. A maker is a trader who posts a resting order and waits. The shadow bot would log where a resting bid would have filled, and what the price did next. That targets the maker-taker gap above. The risk to measure is adverse selection. One maker test on Polymarket's 5-minute bitcoin markets lost heavily, because fills came just before prices moved against it ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/levl/two-polymarket-strategies-that-look-fine-until-you-add-fees-and-a-queue-5g4p); self-reported). It is also worth checking whether the 15-minute crypto series charges makers a fee. Kalshi's open reward programs ended in September and October 2026, so rewards would add little.

The second idea is distance from the start price. One affiliate site's bitcoin sample says late flips cluster in windows within $50 of the start price ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/are-kalshi-15-minute-bitcoin-markets-rigged); affiliate). The bot's two signs may already capture this. If not, it deserves one out-of-sample test, on data the rule never saw while being built. Eight richer filters already failed that kind of test, so the bar is high. A third idea, checking fee rounding, is already done. On 25 October, all 1,918 live orders placed from the bot's server were checked against the exact fee formula. Kalshi rounds each order's fee up to the next hundredth of a cent, not the next cent. The worst order paid one hundredth of a cent extra. In total, rounding cost $0.09 on $25,701 bet. That is about 0.0004 cents for every dollar bet, so there is nothing to fix.

Among other markets, hourly gas already passed a fresh-data backtest. It made about +1 cent of profit for every dollar bet. Its depth was good too. Depth is how much money you can trade before moving the price. Politics favourites show the biggest bias in the studies. But politics is tiny on Kalshi and carries rule risk. Weather favourites near the close look overpriced ([Le](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v2)). The project also found temperature markets too thin, with only about $50 on offer at the best price. The evidence says to skip four ideas. Three are cross-venue arbitrage, copying wallets, and weather or sports models as a taker. The fourth is speed trading near 50c, where fees are highest.
