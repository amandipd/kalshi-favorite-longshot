# Only two US venues rival Kalshi's API

Besides Kalshi, only two platforms are legal for a US resident, run their own order book for short crypto windows, and offer a Kalshi-class API (signed REST order entry plus WebSocket push of the book and your own fills). **Polymarket US** lists BTC Up/Down in 15-minute and 1-hour windows. **Gemini Predictions** lists 5-minute, 15-minute, hourly and daily crypto contracts on BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP. Every other candidate fails at least one hard test. Crypto.com's CDNA (the former Nadex) has the richest menu, including the only sub-daily index binaries, but it has no API, and its flat 2¢ fee turns a 99¢ buy into a guaranteed loss. Robinhood, Coinbase and Webull resell Kalshi's own book. The only API-reachable index and commodity contracts (CME, Cboe) are daily. The offshore venues with good bot tooling (international Polymarket, Limitless, Hyperliquid) bar US persons. The heavy-favourite strategy can run on both survivors at about Kalshi's cost, because both charge the same 0.07·p·(1−p) taker fee. Neither offers a structural edge, though. Published evidence puts favourite underpricing at 0.3–1 percentage point, about the size of the taker fee at 90–95¢. The one academic study of 5-minute crypto markets finds prices accurate to within a point even in the tails. Polymarket US is the stronger fit: its docs say it settles on the same CF Benchmarks index (BRTI) and the same 60-second averaging as Kalshi, on the same quarter-hour grid. That makes it extra capacity and a second price for the same bet, not diversification. Gemini matches the contract design but has thin, market-maker-set books and an unpublished settlement index. On either venue, the edge has to be proven again from the bot's own fills there; it can't be carried over from Kalshi.

## Legal access and a real API eliminate all but two venues

The screen has two hard gates. The first is legal. The venue must be a CFTC-designated contract market (DCM, a licensed US futures exchange) or sit on top of one, and it must be open to US retail. The second is the latency bar Kalshi sets. Kalshi offers signed REST order entry, a WebSocket that pushes order-book deltas and the trader's own fills, and FIX 4.4 for larger users ([Kalshi WebSocket docs](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/websocket_connection); [Kalshi FIX connectivity](https://docs.kalshi.com/fix/connectivity)). There is a third, softer test: the venue must bring a book of its own. A broker that forwards orders to Kalshi adds a network hop and no new prices.

**Crypto.com Derivatives North America (CDNA, the former Nadex) is the biggest near-miss.** It runs its own CFTC-licensed book with 5-minute, 15-minute, 1-hour, 4-hour, daily and weekly contracts on 17 coins. It also lists the only sub-daily US index binaries on any reachable venue: 20-minute and 2-hour contracts on the E-mini S&P, Nasdaq, Dow and Russell ([Nadex crypto specs](https://www.nadex.com/crypto/); [Nadex stock-index specs](https://www.nadex.com/contract-specs-stock-index/)). But Nadex.com has shut. The products now live only inside the Crypto.com app and its OG predictions app, and no API is documented anywhere ([Nadex notice](https://origin-website-prod.nadex.com/notices/cdna-transitions-to-crypto-com-app/); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/02/03/cryptocom-prediction-market-platform-og/)). Even with an API, the fee would kill the strategy. CDNA charges a **flat $0.02 per contract** to enter or exit and nothing at settlement ([Nadex pricing](https://www.nadex.com/pricing/); [Crypto.com Prediction](https://crypto.com/us/prediction/learn/understanding-prediction-event-contract-payout-structure-on-cryptocom)). That fee is 20% of the maximum profit at 90¢, 40% at 95¢ and 200% at 99¢. So the break-even win rate (the share of bets that must win to cover price plus fee) is 92%, 97%, and an impossible 101% at 99¢.

**Robinhood, Coinbase and Webull add access, not liquidity.** Robinhood's 15-minute BTC contract uses Kalshi's BRTI averaging rule and links to a Kalshi terms PDF. Robinhood also routes to ForecastEx, its own Rothera exchange and CDNA, and it has no event-contract API ([Robinhood BTC 15-min page](https://robinhood.com/us/en/prediction-markets/crypto/events/btc-15-min-83-13207-target-oct-13-2026/); [Robinhood support](https://www.robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-event-contracts/)). Coinbase "will source its order flow from Kalshi," and its Advanced Trade API covers spot and futures but not predictions ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/01/27/coinbase-rolls-out-prediction-market-to-u-s-customers); [Coinbase Advanced Trade API](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/coinbase-app/advanced-trade-apis/overview)). Webull is the only router with a real event-contract API. It has REST order entry (limit orders only), streaming quotes since March 2026 and gRPC trade events. But every order still lands in Kalshi's book after a broker hop, so it is slower than the bot's direct connection by construction ([Webull event-contract API](https://developer.webull.com/apis/docs/trade-api/event-contract); [Webull changelog](https://developer.webull.com/apis/docs/changelog/)). At most it is a backup route into the same book.

**For indices, FX and commodities, no US-legal venue offers sub-daily contracts through an API.** CME's legacy event contracts are reachable through futures-broker APIs such as IBKR's TWS, and CME offers co-location (servers placed next to its matching engine in Aurora). So they clear the infrastructure bar. But they are daily contracts paying $100, priced $1–$99, with a 250-lot order cap. The BTC version settles on the daily CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate, and their exchange fees were not found ([IBKR TWS API docs](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/tws-api/doc/event-trading/introduction/cme-event-contracts); [CME co-location](https://www.cmegroup.com/solutions/co-location.html)). CME's hourly $1 contracts on the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, oil, gold and crypto are sold only through the FanDuel Predicts app, which has no API. Flutter (FanDuel's owner) calls the product "behind schedule" ([CME press release](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2025/12/22/fanduel-and-cme-group-launch-fanduel-predicts.html); [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/fanduel-moving-sports-event-contracts-to-crypto-com/)). Cboe's SEC-regulated XSP binaries give 0-day and 1-day S&P exposure through Interactive Brokers, but it is unconfirmed whether IBKR's API can trade them ([Cboe IR](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2026/Cboe-Introduces-Cboe-Predicts-Launching-First-Products-in-New-Prediction-Markets-Suite/)). International Polymarket added EUR/USD and USD/JPY reference feeds on 29 September 2026, but no listed FX market was confirmed, and that venue is closed to US residents anyway ([Polymarket changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog/changelog)).

**The offshore venues with the deepest short-window menus all bar US persons.** International Polymarket runs Up/Down markets from 5 minutes to daily on seven or eight coins. It treats the US as "close-only" on both the website and the API, and it rejects orders from restricted regions. Its primary servers are in London (AWS eu-west-2), and since 4 September 2026 it holds crypto taker orders for 150 ms before they can match (a speed bump) ([Polymarket geoblock](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/CLOB/geoblock); [Polymarket changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog/changelog)). Limitless on Base is the closest on-chain analogue. It has 5- and 15-minute markets settled on a Chainlink TWAP (time-weighted average price), hourly markets settled on Pyth, REST and WebSocket APIs, and off-chain matching. But its terms, updated 15 September 2026, say the platform "is not available for trading" to US users ([Limitless resolution](https://docs.limitless.exchange/user-guide/market-resolution.md); [Limitless terms](https://docs.limitless.exchange/user-guide/terms-of-service.md)). Hyperliquid's HIP-4 outcome markets are US-restricted and so far list only daily BTC ([AInvest](https://www.ainvest.com/news/hyperliquid-28m-washington-push-won-open-2608/); [HIP-4 spec](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-4-outcome-markets)). Two on-chain products fail on design before legality even comes up. PancakeSwap Prediction is a parimutuel pool (all stakes are pooled and the payout ratio is fixed only at lock), and betting closes before the 5-minute price window starts. Overtime Speed Markets set the strike at the current price, so every position is a 50:50 bet with no favourite to buy ([PancakeSwap docs](https://docs.pancakeswap.finance/products/prediction); [DappRadar](https://dappradar.com/dapp/speed-markets)). A missing geoblock on such venues is not a green light. The CFTC fined Polymarket $1.4M in 2022 for offering these contracts to US users ([The Block, 2022](https://www.theblock.co/linked/131383/following-cftc-settlement-prediction-platform-polymarket-geoblocks-trades-in-the-us)).

## Polymarket US copies Kalshi's contract down to the index

| | Kalshi (benchmark) | Polymarket US | Gemini Predictions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short crypto windows | 15-min and hourly | BTC only: 15-min and 1-hour Up/Down | 5-min, 15-min, hourly, daily on BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP |
| Settlement | 60-second average of CF Benchmarks BRTI at window start and end | Same: 60-second simple average of BRTI at start and end; a tie pays Up | Index unpublished; strike = price at window start |
| Taker fee per contract | 0.07·p·(1−p), rounded up | 0.0695·p·(1−p), rounded to nearest cent (half to even) | 0.07·p·(1−p), rounded up to next cent |
| Maker terms | — | Rebate of 0.0125·p·(1−p) until 19 Oct; then none below $10M/month of maker volume | Fee of 0.0175·p·(1−p); rebate is a share of taker fees |
| API | REST + WebSocket; FIX 4.4 | Retail REST + WebSocket; FIX/gRPC for direct-access firms | REST + WebSocket; sandbox |
| Documented order limit | 100 write tokens/s (Basic tier) | 25 requests/s (retail) | About 5 requests/s private (general Gemini limit) |
| Volume signal | About $7B in 15-min crypto, 1–28 Sep 2026 | $27.7M/day in 15-min BTC by 29 Sep 2026 | About $0.5M of prediction revenue in Q2 2026 |

Polymarket US is a CFTC-designated exchange, formerly QCEX, which Polymarket bought for $112M in July 2025. It is funded in US dollars and uses ID-based KYC (identity checks) ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-secures-cftc-nod-us-market-qcx-llc-acquisition-2/); [Polymarket US API overview](https://docs.polymarket.us/trader-guide/api-overview)). Its BTC Up/Down 15-minute markets went live on **22 September 2026** and run around the clock ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-15-minute-bitcoin-markets-us/)). They use the same :00/:15/:30/:45 ET grid as Kalshi. The official docs specify settlement on the **simple average of the 60 one-second BRTI prints before each boundary**. The opening average is published as the "price to beat," and ties pay Up ([Polymarket US Crypto FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/crypto-faqs.md)). That is the same index and averaging design Kalshi uses for its 15-minute crypto contracts ([Kalshi help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823838-crypto-markets)). Two things argue for checking the live rules page before trading: one press report says the US market resolves on Chainlink instead, and the docs still describe the automated BTC families as "preprod" ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-15-minute-bitcoin-markets-us/); [Polymarket US Crypto Schema](https://docs.polymarket.us/trader-guide/crypto-schema.md)). ETH, SOL and the other coins are international-only, so the bot's altcoin bets have no second venue here.

Fees match Kalshi almost exactly. The taker fee is **Θ = 0.0695 times p·(1−p)** per contract: 0.63¢ at 90¢, 0.33¢ at 95¢ and 0.07¢ at 99¢ ([Polymarket US fee schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees)). The difference is rounding. Polymarket US rounds each fee to the nearest cent, half to even, so small orders at 95–99¢ can pay nothing. Kalshi rounds up, so a 1-lot at 99¢ pays its entire 1¢ upside. The maker side is about to get worse. The weekly crypto maker rebate ends on 15 October. From 19 October, the flat maker rebate becomes a volume tier that pays **nothing below $10M a month** of maker volume ([Polymarket US fee schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees); [Polymarket US changelog](https://docs.polymarket.us/changelog)).

Liquidity is real, but it is launch-week liquidity. Daily 15-minute BTC volume climbed from **$2.4M on 23 September to $27.7M on 29 September**, about $290k per window. Kalshi's 15-minute crypto markets traded about $7B over 1–28 September, so Polymarket US ran at roughly a tenth of Kalshi's 15-minute flow ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/polymarket-us-15-minute-bitcoin-volume-hits-92m-in-first-full-week/)). Incentives that expire this week propped up that launch week. No source publishes depth at 90–99¢ in the final minutes, which is the depth the strategy actually uses.

On latency, the design matches Kalshi's. Polymarket US runs a central limit order book with no blockchain step. The retail REST API uses signed headers. The market WebSocket carries the full book and trades, and a private WebSocket pushes order, position and balance updates in sequence ([Polymarket US WebSocket overview](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/websocket/overview.md); [Polymarket US API intro](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/introduction)). Two gaps keep the verdict at "pass pending measurement." First, no round-trip times or hosting region are published; one VPS vendor says AWS us-east-1, which is unverified ([TradingVPS](https://tradingvps.io/polymarket-us-api-trading-how-it-works-automation-vps-setup/)). Second, during overload, new orders and cancel-replaces not processed within 5 seconds are rejected ([Polymarket US rate limits](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/rate-limits.md)). The retail limit of 25 requests per second is ample for a few entries per window. The retail order-type page lists market and limit orders with an immediate-or-cancel option, but no post-only flag, which matters for any maker variant ([Polymarket US order types](https://docs.polymarket.us/learn/trading/basics/order-types.md)).

Legally, Polymarket US is the cleanest option outside Kalshi, but the state fights hit it too. Nevada is the most consistently reported exclusion. One October guide lists eight blocked states (AZ, IL, MA, MD, MI, MT, NV, OH). New York sued two days after the BTC launch. No official state list exists ([OddsAssist](https://oddsassist.com/prediction-markets/polymarket-us-states/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/polymarket-rolls-bitcoin-bets-every-135006140.html); [Polymarket US trading restrictions](https://docs.polymarket.us/learn/trading/access-and-limits/trading-restrictions.md)).

## Gemini lists more coins and windows on a far thinner book

Gemini Predictions launched on 15 December 2025 on Gemini Titan, Gemini's own DCM. Since receiving a clearinghouse (DCO) licence in April 2026, it has cleared trades itself ([Gemini blog](https://www.gemini.com/blog/gemini-predictions-tm-is-now-live); [Gemini DCO announcement](https://www.gemini.com/blog/gemini-receives-dco-license-approval-from-cftc)). Its developer docs define **5-minute (`05M`) and 15-minute (`15M`) "UP" contracts**. Each resolves Yes if the asset is at or above the strike at expiry, where the strike "is the asset's price at the start of the contract window." Daily and longer contracts use fixed strikes ([Gemini dev docs](https://developer.gemini.com/prediction-markets/tickers-crypto)). The docs list BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP, and press coverage adds hourly windows and Zcash ([Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/04/28/bitcoin-predictions-contracts/)). Structurally this is Kalshi's up/down contract, on more coins than Polymarket US offers.

Fees have the same shape as Kalshi's: taker 0.07·p·(1−p) and maker 0.0175·p·(1−p), rounded up to the next cent, with no settlement fee. Makers also receive a share of taker fees, capped at 5% of notional ([Gemini fee page](https://www.gemini.com/fees/predictions); [Gemini maker rebate program](https://developer.gemini.com/prediction-markets/maker-rebate-program)). The API passes. It has authenticated REST order entry, WebSocket streams of "prices, depth, order events, and positions," API-key or OAuth authentication, a sandbox, and public market data that needs no key ([Gemini Prediction Markets API](https://developer.gemini.com/prediction-markets-spec); [Gemini trading quickstart](https://developer.gemini.com/prediction-markets/trading-quickstart)). The only documented rate limits are Gemini's general REST limits: 600 private requests a minute, with about 5 a second recommended. That would constrain cancel-and-replace quoting but not a handful of entries per window. It is also unconfirmed whether those limits apply to the prediction endpoints ([Gemini rate limits](https://developer.gemini.com/rate-limit)).

The problems are depth and settlement. Gemini's whole prediction business booked about **$524K of revenue in Q2 2026**, roughly 1% of company revenue, on a little over 225M contracts since launch ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/exchange/gemini-calls-the-segment-that-brought-in-500000-its-biggest-growth-opportunity/); [The Block](https://theblock.co/news/business/2026-08-13-gemini-shares-slide-108-million-loss-winklevoss-says-we-still-have-work-to-do-411775)). At $1 face value, that is under $225M across every market Gemini lists in its first eight months. Kalshi's 15-minute crypto series traded about $7B in September alone. Gemini "tripled its contracted market makers," so late-window prices at 90–99¢ are most likely set by one or a few paid quoters, not a crowd. That cuts both ways. A market maker hedging against spot would price favourites accurately, while a slow quote that lags the index would leave stale prices to pick off. Neither can be known without data. The settlement index, its averaging rule and the tick size are all unpublished. If Gemini settles on a single print rather than a 60-second average, a favourite held to expiry carries more last-second flip risk than on Kalshi. State access is also murky. Gemini launched nationwide, but a tracker lists eight excluded states, including Arizona, Illinois and Maryland ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/gemini-predictions-market-launch/?rand=59535); [DeFi Rate states](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/states/)).

## The favourite edge is fee-sized, and makers collect it first

The favourite–longshot bias (FLB: heavy favourites win slightly more often than their price implies, longshots much less often) is real at the favourite end, but small. On Kalshi, contracts at 95¢ **won 95.83% of the time** across 72.1M trades from 2021 to 2025, a 0.83-point edge ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). Bürgi, Deng and Whelan studied 313,972 Kalshi prices and find small but statistically significant positive post-fee returns above 70¢. They excluded the hourly-resetting crypto and index markets, though, so the series the bot trades is not in their sample. Their bias slope also fell from 0.048 in 2024 to 0.021 in early 2025 ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). On Polymarket, crypto favourites priced at 90¢ or more returned **+0.54% to +0.64% before fees** over 2022–2026. At 95¢ and above, the gap between win rate and price ran from +0.11 to +1.14 points ([Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1)). The same paper finds favourite returns are realised at resolution, not through price drift: +0.017% after 5 minutes against +1.94% at settlement. That supports holding to expiry rather than trading out.

Evidence specific to 5- and 15-minute windows is thinner and less encouraging. The only academic calibration study (calibration: whether contracts priced at X% win X% of the time) covers about 14,800 Polymarket 5-minute crypto contracts priced a minute before close. It finds outcomes match prices "to within about one percentage point across the range, including in the extreme tails." An apparent FLB "vanishes under dependence-robust inference," meaning once correlated trades in the same window stop being counted as independent ([Gruener, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6863546)). A vendor study of 6,298 Kalshi 15-minute BTC windows found the leading side settled about a point better than its price, +0.67¢ gross per contract. It still called taking "net negative at every entry time tested," against an average taker fee of 1.55¢ ([Bot for Kalshi](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/kalshi-bitcoin-bot)). That average fee is dominated by mid-priced fills, though. At 90–99¢ the fee is only 0.07–0.63¢, so the verdict does not automatically apply to the favourite band, and the vendor has not published per-band results. There is also counter-evidence: in Polymarket weather markets, favourite overpricing is "much stronger in the final 1–12 hours" ([Qiu & Qiu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13593)).

Break-even win rates for 100-lot orders held to settlement, computed from each venue's published fee formula:

| Venue and order type | 90¢ | 95¢ | 99¢ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kalshi taker (benchmark) | 90.63% | 95.34% | 99.07% |
| Polymarket US taker | 90.63% | 95.33% | 99.07% |
| Polymarket US maker, rebate until 19 Oct | 89.89% | 94.94% | 98.99% |
| Gemini taker | 90.63% | 95.34% | 99.07% |
| Gemini maker | 90.16% | 95.09% | 99.02% |
| CDNA / Crypto.com | 92.00% | 97.00% | 101% (impossible) |
| DraftKings DKeX taker | 92.00% | 97.00% | about 99.5–100% |

Set against a measured edge of +0.3 to +1 point, the table sorts the venues cleanly. On Kalshi, Polymarket US and Gemini, a taker at 95¢ gives up 0.33 points. That leaves roughly 0 to +0.7¢ per contract, if the venue's favourites are as underpriced as the Kalshi and Polymarket averages. At 90¢ the 0.63-point fee eats most of the typical edge. At 99¢ the upside is 1¢, so one loss erases 99 wins, and the per-order round-up on Kalshi and Gemini punishes small tickets. CDNA's flat fee needs a 2-point edge at every price, and DKeX's 2¢ taker fee needs the same from 20¢ to 96¢. That is double the largest edge anyone has measured ([DKeX fees via Parameter](https://parameter.io/draftkings-debuts-dkex-platform-to-compete-in-prediction-markets-space/)). The offshore venues have workable economics but are closed: Limitless charges takers about 0.53% at 95¢ and makers nothing ([Limitless fees](https://docs.limitless.exchange/user-guide/fees.md)). PancakeSwap's 3% cut of the whole pot means a favourite holding 95% of the pool needs a 97.9% win rate, by the pool-share arithmetic ([PancakeSwap docs](https://docs.pancakeswap.finance/products/prediction)).

Three mechanisms decide whether the thin edge survives on a new venue.

**Distance to strike.** Flip risk depends on how far the price is from the strike, not on the contract price. In one 200-window sample of Kalshi 15-minute BTC markets, all 31 late flips came in windows that finished within $50 of the line. Across later reruns, only 7 of 821 windows finishing $50 or more away flipped. In one example, a 91¢ Yes with two minutes left settled No ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/are-kalshi-15-minute-bitcoin-markets-rigged)). Filters built on distance relative to remaining volatility carry straight over to Polymarket US, because it settles on the same index. They have to be rebuilt for Gemini once its settlement rule is known.

**Adverse selection (fills arriving just before the price moves against you).** A resting bid at 90–95¢ is most likely to fill when spot has just ticked toward the strike, which is exactly when the true probability has dropped. Bürgi and colleagues note that maker returns depend on cancelling when news arrives ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). On Polymarket, a skilled 3% of accounts place orders that predict short-term price moves ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study)). So the right test on any new venue is calibration conditional on fill: compare the win rate of filled orders with that of unfilled orders at the same price that would have won.

**Crowding.** Polymarket wallets that systematically buy favourites earn **+0.25% against +0.88%** for incidental favourite buyers. They hold 14–15% of favourite dollars but collect only 2.9–4.8% of the gains ([Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1)). Jump Trading makes markets on both Kalshi and Polymarket in exchange for equity ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/389086/jump-trading-to-serve-as-polymarket-and-kalshi-market-maker-in-exchange-for-stake-bloomberg)). Kalshi's maker–taker return gap swung 5.3 points toward makers after the 2024 election ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)).

The best case for new venues is that biases run largest where flow is retail-heavy: Kalshi's maker–taker gap is 2.69 points in crypto against 0.17 in finance ([Becker](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). Launch-phase flow on Polymarket US and Gemini could therefore be mispriced more than Kalshi's mature series. On Gemini, though, that edge comes with little capacity. On Polymarket US it should shrink as incentives end and professional makers arrive.

## Conclusion

Adding Polymarket US gives the bot a second order book on the same outcome, not a new venue in any useful sense. Both settle on 60-second BRTI averages, on the same quarter-hour grid, with ties to Up. So a Kalshi position and a Polymarket US position on the same window win or lose together, and Kelly sizing (staking a bankroll fraction in proportion to edge) has to treat them as one bet, not two. The same identity is an opportunity. Whenever Yes on one venue plus No on the other costs less than $1 after fees, the pair locks in a profit with almost no basis risk (the risk that the two settlement prices diverge), provided the live rules match the docs. That is cleaner than the Kalshi-versus-international-Polymarket "arbitrage" bots, which straddle BRTI and Chainlink. In daily use, the bot can buy each BTC favourite on whichever book is cheaper.

Gemini is the only route to more coins on a second US book. Its value is unproven until its depth and settlement rule are known, and a week of recording its key-free order book answers the depth question at no cost. The venue map is also about to move. Polymarket US has 5-minute and 4-hour BTC in preprod ([Polymarket US Crypto Schema](https://docs.polymarket.us/trader-guide/crypto-schema.md)). Limitless Markets US has had a DCM application pending since May, which would bring 5-minute contracts and a Kalshi-like API onshore; a crowd forecast gives approval in 2026 about two-in-three odds ([CFTC filing page](https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/IndustryFilings/TradingOrganizations/60686); [Manifold](https://manifold.markets/realTomBayes/limitless-markets-us-approved-as-a)). Kraken and Blockchain.com are building or seeking DCMs ([iGaming Today](https://www.igamingtoday.com/kraken-completes-100m-acquisition-of-small-exchanges-eyes-us-prediction-market-entry/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/blockchain-com-cftc-prediction-markets.html)). The broader lesson is that no venue prices favourites cheaply enough to beat fees on price alone. Whatever edge the bot has comes from its execution rules: when to enter, how far from the strike, and which fills to trust. Those rules carry over only to venues whose settlement mechanics match Kalshi's.
