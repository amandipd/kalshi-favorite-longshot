The favorite-longshot bias in Kalshi prediction markets

Loading…

The answer. Kalshi's prices are close to honest probabilities, with a small favorite-longshot bias that survives every statistical check: long shots win a little less often than their price says, favorites a little more. The bias beats fees out of sample, but by less than a cent a contract, and with realistic fills the growth-optimal (Kelly) bet is zero.

Why the six-hour curve looks flat

Fit the outcome on both prices' log-odds at once. If the earlier price knew something the later one didn't, its weight would survive.

Weight on the six-hour price

Noise in a forecast flattens its calibration curve. A six-hour price that adds almost nothing to the one-hour price is a noisier reading of the same forecast, not a market with a different bias.

Kelly bet for 90¢+ favorites

The growth-optimal share of bankroll for one bet, f* = (q − c) / (1 − c): q is how often the favorite won, c its all-in cost a contract.

Below zero, the growth-optimal bet is nothing. Recorded prices are the ask at minute 8; a live order fills about 0.4¢ a contract worse, and Kalshi's fee is charged on the price paid.
